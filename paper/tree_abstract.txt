Latent Tree Priors for Bond-Free Molecular Flow Matching

Molecular flow matching often starts from isotropic noise or a spatial prior constructed from a supplied chemical graph. We study spatial source distributions when atomic composition and electronic conditions are given but chemical bonds are unavailable. A latent dependence tree connects isotropic finite-length edge distributions, and a matrix-tree ratio gives the normalized marginal coordinate density after removing translation. Small composition-conditioned networks learn tree affinities directly from coordinates, without bond targets or gradients through sampled discrete trees. We integrate this source with a molecular flow and examine which source properties improve generation. In a bounded OMol25 development study, a fixed tree prior reduces fragmentation. Fresh draws from two matched training continuations also show higher graph acceptance than isotropic and covariance-matched single-Gaussian sources; comparison with a harmonic-tree mixture varies across continuations. An earlier weaker confirmation stream is retained. Learned affinities improve held-out source likelihood but have not established an increment over the fixed prior in final generation. A one-seed energy readout provides a node-network signal relative to an isotropic source. These results motivate separating source fit, spatial dependence and chemical utility; they do not establish equilibrium sampling or broad generator superiority.
