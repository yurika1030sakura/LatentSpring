Latent Tree Priors for Bond-Free Molecular Flow Matching

Molecular flow matching often starts from isotropic noise or a spatial prior constructed from a supplied chemical graph. We study a learnable coordinate source when composition and electronic conditions are given but bonds are unavailable. A latent dependence tree connects normalized isotropic edge laws; a matrix-tree ratio gives the marginal coordinate density on the translation-free subspace. Small networks learn affinities directly from coordinates without bond targets or gradients through sampled trees. We integrate this source with molecular flow matching and distinguish source fit from generation utility. Tree sources repeatedly improve geometric support in bounded OMol25 studies, while chemical graph improvements vary across continuations. Broad-panel reference checks expose limited graph-assay coverage. A prospective test therefore selects21 neutral organic monomers whose original references all pass the unchanged assay and whose compositions are absent from two verified processed corpora. On10,752 generated outputs, learned node/pair sources still do not establish an increment over the fixed shell source; a separate static tree-context adapter also fails its matched control. The framework makes the source probability law explicit, but these results do not establish competitive superiority or equilibrium sampling. They motivate testing source learning against terminal generation utility rather than assuming that improved coordinate likelihood is sufficient.
