\section{Bounded accepted-utility learning: internal checks}
\label{app:accepted-utility}

The scalar predictor's failure motivates direct optimization of the actual
Metropolis-corrected proposal utility. For a fixed source $x$, action $a$ and
behavioral candidate $y$, let $q_b$ be the recorded coordinate density and
$q_{\theta,f},q_{\theta,r}$ the learned forward/reverse coordinate densities.
With $A=-(U(y)-U(x))/kT+\log(N_f/N_r)$, the accepted importance contribution is
\begin{equation}
 r(x,y)\min\!\left\{\frac{q_{\theta,f}}{q_b},
                    e^A\frac{q_{\theta,r}}{q_b}\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is the ordinary importance ratio times MH acceptance, not a new sampling
identity. It requires behavioral coverage. Unsupported attempts retain zero
utility in the full empirical denominator. The source population is fixed for
this objective; the expression does not differentiate the future state
population of a learned chain or establish improved long-run sampling.

We add a masked neural log-score residual of magnitude at most $B=0.5$ to the
physical site score. It is a proposal score, not a physical-energy predictor.
Endpoint-linear interpolation retains this bound. A normalized root density
changes by at most $e^{2B}$, and the two-root density, including order
marginalization, by at most $e^{4B}=e^2$ on the common support. This elementary
bound controls the importance ratio; it is not an efficiency guarantee.
A separate observed-density interface differentiates all conditional scores and
normalizers with respect to model parameters, while production sampling retains
its no-gradient boundary. Exact finite-state tests, actual-map finite differences,
independent density quadrature and symmetry checks qualify the implementation.

The existing physical site-arc FIT trajectories provide 1,671 attempts, including
74 unscored failures. Their 12,288 calls plus 5,822 preparation calls give 18,110
data-construction calls. A fixed hash assigns 36 parents to fitting and 12 to
internal selection. Two seeds run 300 fixed steps from physical initialization.
The objective uses signed accepted potential decrease $J$ and expected query cost
$C$, optimizing $J-\eta_0 C$ with the FIT baseline rate $\eta_0=J_0/C_0$ fixed.
An empirical forward/reverse log-ratio penalty is included. Stratified edge
selection uses its exact finite-dataset sampling correction; no behavioral
importance weights are clipped. This remains an empirical one-step proxy.

Independent replay confirms all final metrics and trained-objective gradients.
The internal selection rate increases from $0.0032256$ to $0.0033137$ and
$0.0033132$\,eV per expected raw call, about 2.7\% for each seed. Descriptive
parent-bootstrap intervals for the differences are
$[3.31,15.11]\times10^{-5}$ and $[2.76,15.54]\times10^{-5}$\,eV per call.
The weighted scored-edge effective counts remain about 402 and 401 versus 406
at initialization, with maximum observed importance ratios below 1.68. These
counts are importance diagnostics, not independent molecular sample sizes.

\paragraph{Fresh-proposal oracle check.}
A frozen follow-up takes the first, middle and last joint-attempt source state
from each of the two physical trajectories for every internal selection parent.
This gives 72 fixed states. Each method draws 16 new proposals per state, with
matched per-source/trial random streams. All 3,456 attempts are retained. Each
method scores 1,118 candidates, for 2,236 raw calls per method and 6,708 total;
all proposals and 3,354 MH ratios pass independent replay and quadrature.
The source-state population is narrower than the full offline proxy population,
so their absolute baseline rates should not be equated.

The actual fresh-proposal rates are $0.0026329$\,eV per raw call for physics and
$0.0026352$, $0.0026326$ for the learned seeds. Differences are
$2.29\times10^{-6}$ and $-2.63\times10^{-7}$\,eV per call, with descriptive parent
intervals $[-8.54,11.31]\times10^{-5}$ and
$[-16.20,16.94]\times10^{-5}$. Neither establishes an actual proposal-utility
advantage. These intervals also do not rule out a small effect. No complete-chain
or final-test benefit follows, and the preceding scalar-model negative result is
unchanged. Uniform chemical action selection remains a possible additional
learning target, requiring a separate bounded comparison with correct inverse
probabilities and a fixed move-family schedule.
