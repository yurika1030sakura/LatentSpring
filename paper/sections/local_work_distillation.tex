\section{Local force-escorted work distillation}\label{app:local-work}
The physical teacher constructs nearby candidate structures and determines how
often each is used as a flow-matching target. We compare uniform sampling of
force-shifted candidates with energy-weighted training
\citep{dern2025energyweighted,fab}. A normalized Gaussian around each anchor
makes the complete escorted-work weights explicit \citep{escorted2008}.

\paragraph{Energy, force, and mirror symmetry.}
The energy model gives a scalar $E(X)$ and forces $F(X)=-\nabla_X E(X)$.
Each atom's force points toward decreasing energy. To treat mirror-related
structures consistently, we evaluate both $X$ and its inversion $-X$ and use
$E_+(X)=[E(X)+E(-X)]/2$ and $F_+(X)=[F(X)-F(-X)]/2$.
The minus sign in the force average ensures $F_+=-\nabla E_+$. Inverted
structures thus receive equal energies and opposite forces. Before constructing
a displacement, we subtract the mean atom force so the displacement stays in
the centered coordinate space.

\paragraph{Local reference and target.}
For a graph-valid generated anchor $A$, let $G_A$ be its inferred labeled graph
and define $q_A(X)=\mathcal N_{\HH_N}(A,\sigma_A^2I)$. We use thermal energy
$kT=k_B T_{\rm phys}=0.0258519998$ eV at 300 K and set
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_A=\min\left(0.03\,\text{\AA},
 \sqrt{\frac{0.10\,\text{\AA}\,kT}{\|F_+(A)\|}}\right),\qquad
 \delta_A=\frac{\sigma_A^2}{kT}F_+(A).
\end{equation}
The norm is the Frobenius norm across all atom-force components. These choices
bound the displacement norm by $0.10$\,\AA\ and the Gaussian width by
$0.03$\,\AA. At zero force, we use the maximum width. Both quantities are fixed
before sampling $X\sim q_A$. We then translate each candidate to $Y=X+\delta_A$.
This map stays within the centered subspace and has determinant one.

The unnormalized local target is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_A(Y)=q_A(Y)\exp\!\left[-\frac{E_+(Y)-E_+(A)}{kT}\right]
 \mathbf1_{G_A}(Y).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian keeps structures near the anchor, while the energy factor favors
lower-energy structures. The indicator restricts candidates to the anchor's
inferred labeled bond orders, formal charges, and radical counts, together with
the connected/nonoverlap checks. Thus this is a restrained local distribution,
not an unrestricted equilibrium distribution over molecules.

\paragraph{Why the force displacement has this form.}
Approximate energy near the anchor by
$E_+(Y)-E_+(A)\simeq-\langle F_+(A),Y-A\rangle_F$.
The inner product sums force times displacement over all atoms and coordinates.
Multiplying this linearized Boltzmann factor by the Gaussian and completing the
square gives
\begin{equation}
 q_A(Y)\exp\!\left[\frac{\langle F_+(A),Y-A\rangle_F}{kT}\right]
 \ \propto\
 \mathcal N_{\HH_N}\!\left(Y;A+\frac{\sigma_A^2}{kT}F_+(A),\sigma_A^2I\right).
 \label{eq:linear-boltzmann-escort}
\end{equation}
Before applying the graph restriction, the force translation therefore samples
the energy-weighted Gaussian exactly under the linear approximation. Equivalently,
the Gaussian restraint is a virtual harmonic spring with stiffness
$kT/\sigma_A^2$, and the force shifts its mean by force divided by stiffness.
The same-graph check then truncates this distribution.

\paragraph{Complete local work.}
Uniform weighting uses the linear approximation above. Complete-work weighting
instead evaluates the nonlinear energy to correct departures from that
approximation. With reference Hamiltonian $H_0=-kT\log q_A$ and target
Hamiltonian $H_1=H_0+E_+-E_+(A)$ plus the hard support restriction, the
work is
\begin{equation}
 W_A(X)=E_+(Y)-E_+(A)+kT[\log q_A(X)-\log q_A(Y)]
\end{equation}
on allowed candidates, and $+\infty$ otherwise. The log-density terms correct
for the shift from the proposal $X$ to $Y$. Change of variables gives
$\mathbb E_{q_A}[\exp(-W_A/kT)]=\int\rho_A(Y)\,dY$.
Analytic checks recover constant work for a constant-force translation, and the
normalizer and weighted mean for a quadratic energy. Substituting the linearized
energy in Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-boltzmann-escort} gives
$W_A=-\sigma_A^2\|F_+(A)\|_F^2/(2kT)$ for every allowed candidate. Their
weights are therefore equal in that approximation, explaining the less expensive
force-only teacher.

\paragraph{Training pools and controls.}
Each fine-tuning run selects eight distinct training compositions with 17--28
atoms using a fixed metadata hash, then generates 32 anchors per composition.
These compositions are excluded from evaluation. Invalid anchors count as
generation attempts but do not enter the teacher pool. Each valid anchor produces
eight force-shifted candidates; candidates outside its support receive zero
weight. The complete-work model samples training endpoints with self-normalized
$\exp(-W_A/kT)$ weights. The unweighted-escort model samples uniformly from the
same allowed candidates. The replay model uses the original retained anchors,
while the frozen model receives no further training. Anchors with no allowed
candidate are recorded and excluded from all student pools in the same way.

Each student alternates between 500 FM updates on original references and
500 updates on generated endpoints. Initialization, anchor draws, optimization,
and sampling budgets are shared. Replay needs no force queries; the unweighted
escort needs anchor forces; complete work additionally needs energies at valid
candidate endpoints. We distinguish the queries used in the shared research
experiment from those required by each method on its own. Generation uses only
the final network. Its temperature feature is fixed during training and
evaluation; 300 K sets the local teacher weights.

Self-normalized weights introduce finite-particle bias. We report effective
sample size (ESS) over each anchor's eight candidates:
$(\sum_j w_j)^2/\sum_j w_j^2$. Equal weights give ESS equal to the candidate
count, while a single dominant candidate gives ESS close to one.
