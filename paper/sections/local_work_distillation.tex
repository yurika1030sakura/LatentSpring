\section{Local force-escorted work distillation}\label{app:local-work}
The physical teacher constructs nearby structures and assigns their sampling weights as FM targets. We compare uniform sampling of force-shifted candidates with energy-weighted training \citep{dern2025energyweighted,fab}. A normalized Gaussian around each anchor makes the complete escorted-work weights tractable \citep{escorted2008}.

\paragraph{Mirror-symmetric energy and force.}
Given $E(X)$ and $F(X)=-\nabla_X E(X)$, we define $E_+(X)=[E(X)+E(-X)]/2$ and $F_+(X)=[F(X)-F(-X)]/2$. This construction gives $F_+=-\nabla E_+$, equal energies for inverted structures, and opposite forces. Subtracting the mean atomic force keeps subsequent displacements in the centered subspace.

\paragraph{Local reference and target.}
For a graph-valid anchor $A$ with inferred labeled graph $G_A$, define $q_A(X)=\mathcal N_{\HH_N}(A,\sigma_A^2I)$. At 300 K, $kT=k_B T_{\rm phys}=0.0258519998$ eV. Set
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_A=\min\left(0.03\,\text{\AA},
 \sqrt{\frac{0.10\,\text{\AA}\,kT}{\|F_+(A)\|}}\right),\qquad
 \delta_A=\frac{\sigma_A^2}{kT}F_+(A).
\end{equation}
The Frobenius force norm determines a width at most $0.03$\,\AA\ and displacement norm at most $0.10$\,\AA. At zero force, the maximum width is used. These quantities are fixed before drawing $X\sim q_A$. Translation to $Y=X+\delta_A$ has determinant one and preserves centering.

The unnormalized target is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_A(Y)=q_A(Y)\exp\!\left[-\frac{E_+(Y)-E_+(A)}{kT}\right]
 \mathbf1_{G_A}(Y).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian restrains the neighborhood around $A$, the energy factor favors lower-energy configurations, and the indicator preserves inferred labeled bond orders, charges, and radicals while enforcing connectivity and nonoverlap. The target is therefore a restrained, same-graph local distribution.

\paragraph{Force-shift derivation.}
Linearizing energy gives $E_+(Y)-E_+(A)\simeq-\langle F_+(A),Y-A\rangle_F$. Completing the square in the Gaussian times this Boltzmann factor yields
\begin{equation}
 q_A(Y)\exp\!\left[\frac{\langle F_+(A),Y-A\rangle_F}{kT}\right]
 \ \propto\
 \mathcal N_{\HH_N}\!\left(Y;A+\frac{\sigma_A^2}{kT}F_+(A),\sigma_A^2I\right).
 \label{eq:linear-boltzmann-escort}
\end{equation}
Thus force translation exactly samples the linearly reweighted Gaussian before graph restriction. Equivalently, the Gaussian has spring stiffness $kT/\sigma_A^2$ and its mean shifts by force divided by stiffness. Applying the same-graph screen truncates this distribution.

\paragraph{Complete work.}
To correct the linear approximation, we evaluate the nonlinear energy. With $H_0=-kT\log q_A$ and $H_1=H_0+E_+-E_+(A)$ on the allowed support, the work is
\begin{equation}
 W_A(X)=E_+(Y)-E_+(A)+kT[\log q_A(X)-\log q_A(Y)]
\end{equation}
and is $+\infty$ outside it. The density ratio accounts for translation from $X$ to $Y$, giving $\mathbb E_{q_A}[\exp(-W_A/kT)]=\int\rho_A(Y)\,dY$. Analytic tests recover the constant-force identity and the quadratic-energy normalizer and weighted mean. Under Equation~\eqref{eq:linear-boltzmann-escort}, every allowed candidate has $W_A=-\sigma_A^2\|F_+(A)\|_F^2/(2kT)$, so uniform weights recover the linear approximation.

\paragraph{Training pools.}
Each run selects eight 17--28-atom training compositions by metadata hash and generates 32 anchors per composition. Evaluation compositions are excluded. Invalid anchors remain in generation counts but not teacher pools. Each valid anchor produces eight shifted candidates. Complete-work training uses self-normalized $\exp(-W_A/kT)$ weights, with zero weight outside the support; the unweighted teacher samples uniformly from the same admissible candidates. Replay uses original retained anchors. Anchors without an admissible candidate are recorded and excluded consistently from all students.

Each student receives 500 reference FM updates alternating with 500 generated-target updates, sharing initialization, anchor draws, optimization, and sampling budgets. Replay needs no physical labels; uniform escort requires anchor forces; complete work also requires candidate energies. Actual shared-experiment queries and standalone method requirements are reported separately. Sampling uses the final network, with a fixed temperature feature; 300 K determines the local teacher weights.

Self-normalized weighting has finite-particle bias. We report ESS as $(\sum_j w_j)^2/\sum_j w_j^2$, ranging from one for a dominant candidate to eight for equal weights.
