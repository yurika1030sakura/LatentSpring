\subsection{What self-conditioning contributes}\label{sec:sc-ablation}
We remove self-conditioning from the harmonic-source model while keeping its
initialization, 3,000 training structures, source distribution, and pairing
procedure unchanged. One single-pass model receives 3,000 optimizer updates.
A second receives 6,000, matching the self-conditioned model's number of
backbone training evaluations. Both single-pass models use 64 midpoint steps at
generation, so every model uses 128 network calls per output.

\centering

outperforms both single-pass training budgets.}

Self-conditioning improves graph validity by 7.23 percentage points at equal
optimizer updates (95\% interval [4.69,9.77]) and by 12.43 points at equal
training forward passes ([9.70,15.23]). Both runs improve in both comparisons.
Matching pass counts does not match every operation or wall-clock cost. This
control asks whether additional single-pass training recovers the validity gain;
it does not in these runs. The update-direction control is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:update-ablation}.

\paragraph{Contribution of the force displacement.}
We also remove only the force shift from local training targets. Gaussian
perturbations, candidate indices, widths, training schedule, and the paired
parameter update remain unchanged. Relative to this control, force-shifted
targets lower energy on paired, jointly valid outputs by $0.01939$ eV/atom
(95\% interval [-0.02417,-0.01455] for the signed difference) and force RMS by
$0.3926$ eV/\AA\ ([-0.4696,-0.3263]). Both runs improve. Graph validity remains
similar: force minus control is $+0.39$ percentage points, with interval
[-1.33,2.19]. Because the original force-dependent widths and candidate-selection
rule remain fixed, this ablation tests the displacement itself, not the removal
of all force information.
