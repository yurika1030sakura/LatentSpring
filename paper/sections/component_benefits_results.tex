\subsection{What self-conditioning contributes}\label{sec:sc-ablation}
To test the provisional-geometry feedback, we train the harmonic-source model
with self-conditioning removed. Initialization, the 3,000 training structures,
source law and pairing are unchanged. One readout uses 3,000 optimizer updates;
a second uses 6,000, matching the self-conditioned model's number of backbone
training evaluations. A single-pass model takes 64 midpoint steps, so all methods
use 128 network evaluations at generation.

\centering
\caption{Self-conditioning ablation on the 24-composition panel. Each continuation
has 768 raw attempts. The full model improves over both single-pass budgets.}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Model & Updates & Training passes & Valid outputs & Rate (\%)\\
\midrule
Single pass &3,000&3,000&363;336&45.51\\
Single pass &6,000&6,000&277;342&40.30\\
Self-conditioned &3,000&6,000&435;375&52.73\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\label{tab:sc-ablation}
\end{table}

Self-conditioning adds 7.23 percentage points at equal optimizer updates
(95\% interval [4.69,9.77]) and 12.43 at equal training-pass count
([9.70,15.23]). Both continuations improve under both comparisons. Matching pass
counts does not equate every operation or wall-clock cost; the comparison tests
whether additional single-pass training recovers the observed validity gain.

\subsection{Why subtract the replay update}\label{sec:update-ablation}
A physical continuation also changes the model through ordinary refitting of
generated anchors. We compare the paired update with direct physical continuation
and a control that scales the direct update to the same parameter-space length.
For this control,
$\theta_{\rm control}=\theta_{\rm base}+alpha(\theta_{\rm physical}-\theta_{\rm base})$,
where $\alpha$ is the ratio of the paired and direct update norms. These norms sum
squared changes over trainable parameters only. The resulting coefficients,
0.2536 and 0.2564, are determined from training weights before generation.

The same-norm control largely preserves graph validity but raises energy relative
to the base model. The paired update instead lowers it. Directly comparing the
paired and same-norm updates gives an energy change of $-0.03232$ eV/atom
([-0.03981,-0.02485]) and a force-RMS change of $-0.4170$ eV/\AA\
([-0.5529,-0.2859]). Both continuations favor the paired direction, and the
all-output comparisons have the same signs. Their graph-validity difference is
$+0.39$ points with interval [-1.95,2.73]. The result supports the direction
identified by replay subtraction, beyond simply limiting update magnitude.
