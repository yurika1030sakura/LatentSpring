\subsection{Comparison with generative baselines}\label{sec:main-comparison}
We compare LatentSpring with Gaussian-source flow matching, EDM, and GAGA.
LatentSpring combines the harmonic-mixture source and learned physical
correction; the baselines use their own generators without these additions.
Figure~\ref{fig:main-generators} reports both measures on the same compositions.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../research/figures/round2_results_v4/quality_gap.pdf}
\caption{Generation quality on 64 unseen compositions. (A) Open markers show graph
validity; filled markers show joint yield at the 5 eV/\AA\ force threshold. The
connecting segment counts graph-valid outputs above that threshold. (B) Empirical
joint-yield curves computed from every saved force score; the dashed line marks
5 eV/\AA. Colors and line styles identify the same methods in both panels. Gaussian FM and EDM
use two fits (2,048 outputs per method); GAGA and LatentSpring use five (5,120).
The hydrogen readout is disabled.}\label{fig:main-generators}
\end{figure}

LatentSpring has the highest mean graph validity and joint yield in this
comparison. Its joint yield is 24.00\%, compared with 8.75\% for GAGA,
8.15\% for EDM, and 6.25\% for Gaussian FM. The gains over GAGA and
EDM are 15.25 and 15.85 percentage points, respectively. The separation is larger
for joint yield than for graph validity: more generated structures satisfy
both the chemical-graph checks and the physical force threshold.

A stricter screen of bond lengths, angles, steric distances and planarity raises
the fraction satisfying both that screen and the force criterion from 1.64\%
for the FM parent to 4.43\% after correction. Appendix~\ref{app:chemical-geometry}
reports the complete geometric diagnostics.
