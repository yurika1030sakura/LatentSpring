\section{Capped conditional proposal proof}
Fix a terminal/anchor pair and context $c$. Let $h_c(v)$ be the base angular
proposal density and let its internal acceptance satisfy
$h_c(v)a_c(v)=C(c){\bf 1}_{D(c)}(v)\exp s_\theta(v;c)$, with $C(c)>0$ independent
of $v$. Let $Z(c)=\int h_c(v)a_c(v)\,d\Omega(v)$. Independence of successive trials
gives the non-self density after a cap $K$ as
\[
 Q_K(v\mid c)=h_c(v)a_c(v)\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}(1-Z(c))^j.
\]
The probability of exhausting the cap is $(1-Z(c))^K$ and yields a self-transition.
For two eligible directions with the same context, all factors except their
unnormalized angular scores cancel. Including the selection probability of the
leaf/anchor pair yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular-mh}. The usual minimum probability
flow then gives detailed balance. The chart determinant on $\HH$ is constant,
and the common radius contributes the same $r^2$ factor in each direction.

This argument does not apply if the guide, support or cap depends on the old
direction through an unmasked feature. In that case the factors involving
$C$, $Z$ or $K$ can differ and must enter the reverse ratio. Our finite-state
negative control exhibits the resulting bias when context invariance is broken.

\section{Directional envelope proof}
Let $t=|v^\top u|\in[0,1]$, $d=\lambda_3-\lambda_2\ge 0$. The spectral bound gives
$u^\top A u\le\lambda_2+d t^2$. Since the derivative of $\log\cosh z$ is bounded
by one for $z\ge 0$,
\[
 \log\cosh d-\log\cosh(dt)\le d(1-t).
\]
Consequently
\[
 u^\top A u-\log\cosh(dt)-[\lambda_3-\log\cosh d]
 \le-d(1-t^2)+d(1-t)=-dt(1-t)\le 0.
\]
For natural parameters $\eta_\pm=\eta\pm dv$, let $C_\pm$ be their vMF normalizers.
Mixture weights proportional to $C_\pm^{-1}$ produce a normalized base density
proportional to $\exp(\eta^\top u)\cosh(dv^\top u)$. Multiplying by the bounded
rejection probability gives a constant times $\exp(\eta^\top u+u^\top A u)$.
This constant is fixed by the invariant context and therefore cancels in the
capped conditional MH construction.

For identical coefficients and any common support, let $Z_D=\int_D\exp(s_\theta)
\,d\Omega$. Uniform score rejection succeeds per trial with probability
$Z_D/(4\pi e^M)$, whereas the two-vMF envelope succeeds with probability
$Z_D/(H e^B)$, where $H$ is the normalizing integral of the base mixture's
unnormalized score. Their exact ratio is $4\pi e^M/(H e^B)$; no support volume
or $Z_D$ estimate is needed. Since
$H\le 4\pi e^{\|\eta\|}\cosh d$ and $B=\lambda_3-\log\cosh d$, this ratio
is at least one for $M=\|\eta\|+\lambda_3$.
On identical saved development contexts, median per-trial factors are 183.6/265.4
for the two tensor models and 86.4/85.7 for vector models. These are internal
rejection-success factors, not runtime speedups or reductions in equilibrium error.

\section{Joint coordinate volume and reversibility}
Fix two moved roots and the relative positions of the passive atoms. Let
$P=I-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top/N$. Changes to the two Cartesian root vectors followed
by COM projection have derivative columns $Pe_i$ and $Pe_j$ in each spatial
component. Their Gram matrix has diagonal $1-1/N$ and off-diagonal $-1/N$.
The induced six-dimensional volume factor is therefore
$[(N-2)/N]^{3/2}$, independent of the anchor assignment or endpoint. Equivalently,
a full chart including the passive relative coordinates is linear with constant
Jacobian. Either construction yields a common factor in the conditional
forward/reverse ratio. Each moved vector contributes
$r^2 dr\,d\Omega=r^3 d\ell\,d\Omega$, proving the radial term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint-q}.

For fixed source, action and order, the two radial laws are normalized; the two
angular conditionals are normalized in their sequentially constructed contexts.
Their autoregressive product is thus a normalized proposal on the six-dimensional
conditional coordinate chart. The graph/geometry check is applied only after this
single draw. Its failures produce self-transitions rather than an unknown
support-conditioned proposal normalization. For an eligible endpoint, the inverse
attachment action exists and uses the same augmented order with probability $1/2$.
Applying the minimum forward/reverse probability flow gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint-mh} and detailed balance for each paired action/order. Summing
over these pairs preserves detailed balance. Mixing both orders also prevents
the canonical ordering of atom indices from introducing a permutation preference.

Re-evaluating the first conditional using both final root positions would not
evaluate this autoregressive proposal. Tests reconstruct both source-dependent
templates explicitly and include a negative check distinguishing the completed
endpoint context. A correlated log-normal radial target checks stationary radial
and Cartesian moments with nontrivial radial proposals; removing the $r^3$ term
produces detectable bias. This known-target check supplements, rather than
replaces, molecular proposal and physical-query replay.

\section{Normalized passive-atom mixtures and defensive correction}
Each vMF component integrates to one on $\sphere$, and its invariant mixing
probabilities sum to one. Marginalization gives the explicitly normalized
directional law used by the implementation. Differentiating its log density with
respect to the direction gives a posterior-weighted sum of natural parameters;
projecting onto the sphere gives the surface-score formula in the main text.
The conditioning context remains masked and fixed during this derivative.

Passive-atom permutations permute component pairs; orthogonal transformations
rotate their natural parameters. The scalar density is consequently invariant.
For a two-atom system the only passive vector is the zero anchor vector, so the
guide becomes uniform and does not introduce a preferred spatial axis. Tests
cover this limit, zero-concentration parameter derivatives, normalized quadrature,
sampling moments, both joint root orders and full reverse-template reconstruction.

Fix a paired action/order and absorb its selection probability and the common
coordinate chart factors into proposal densities. For $q=wq_0+(1-w)q_1$ with
constant $0<w<1$, the accepted off-diagonal probability flow is
\[
 \min\{\pi(x)q(y\mid x),\pi(y)q(x\mid y)\}
 \ge w\min\{\pi(x)q_0(y\mid x),\pi(y)q_0(x\mid y)\}.
\]
This follows by discarding the nonnegative second-component contributions in
both arguments of the minimum. The implementation uses $w=1/2$ and evaluates
both whole-joint component densities; it does not score only the drawn component
while claiming to evaluate the marginal mixture. The inequality concerns
off-diagonal flow, not a uniform bound on every diagonal self probability,
wall-clock cost, training cost or generalization quality.

\section{Pendant-fragment augmented map}
Use a linear COM chart comprising passive relative coordinates, the two old root
vectors and root-relative internal offsets of both disjoint fragments. Add six
Cartesian root auxiliaries and two circular torsion auxiliaries. The map swaps
old/new root vectors, rotates every internal offset properly and negates the
torsions. In a suitable variable ordering its derivative is block triangular
apart from coordinate swaps: the internal diagonal blocks are proper rotations,
the swaps have unit absolute determinant, and the two angle derivatives are $-1$.
Dependence of the rotation on old/new root vectors occurs off the diagonal.
The common linear COM chart factors cancel, giving augmented absolute Jacobian 1.

With $A(b,a)=A(a,b)^\top$ and alignment conjugating rotations about their axes,
\[
 R(a,-\delta)A(b,a)=[R(b,\delta)A(a,b)]^{-1}.
\]
Hence the inverse action, old root-vector auxiliaries and negated angles recover
every coordinate. Near-antipodal alignment is excluded symmetrically, producing
self-transitions without renormalizing the auxiliary law. Under improper
orthogonal transformations the circular auxiliary changes sign; its uniform law
is invariant. The root-density ratio retains the $r^{-3}$ factors already derived.

Tests cover unequal fragment sizes, the singleton limit, the full COM-plus-auxiliary
Jacobian, graph inverses, reflections/permutations and a known Gaussian target with
a paired action label. Omitting the proposal-density ratio biases the known-target
test. Independent molecular replay reconstructs rotations using vector Rodrigues
formulas and recalculates root densities and MH ratios.

For future learning, the infinitesimal direction response at the current anchor
is $P_a[r\sum_{j\in F}F_{\HH,j}+\tau_F\times a]/(kT)$, with
$\tau_F=\sum_{j\in F}(x_j-x_i)\times F_{\HH,j}$, and the torsional derivative is
$\tau_F^\top a/(kT)$. These identities were checked by independent differentiation
of the full coordinate update. They are not yet used by a trained fragment guide.

\section{Implementation verification and interpretation}
Tests check masked-context equality under leaf motion and COM recentering,
orthogonal and permutation covariance, batch independence, surface derivatives,
rejection envelopes, normalized vMF densities and known supported $\sphere$ targets.
The original molecular paths retain paired raw forces and energies, every
candidate trial and all random streams. Frozen-producer replay exactly reproduces
the first learned study's coordinates, and a separate calculation checks the
physical-energy and angular-score acceptance ratio.

These checks verify the specified implementation and its mathematical contracts.
They do not independently validate the energy model, establish quantum chemical
validity, estimate a source likelihood or prove finite-time convergence to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular-target}. Those scientific limits remain explicit in the main
text and must be addressed before submission.
