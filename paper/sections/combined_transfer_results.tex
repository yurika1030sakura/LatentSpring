\subsection{Transfer across generative models}\label{sec:joint-transfer}
We attach both FM-trained correction networks to EDM and GAGA without
retraining. Their weights and strengths remain fixed, while each target keeps
its source, backbone and sampling schedule. The endpoint relation converts the
coordinate correction into the target's native noise prediction.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../research/figures/geometric_followups_v1/geometric_transfer.pdf}
\caption{Transfer of both correction networks. Bold lines show the two-fit means;
faint lines show each paired fit. Each setting uses 2,048 attempts on 64
compositions. The target retains its original source and backbone, and the
hydrogen readout is disabled. (A) Graph-and-force yield. (B) The stricter
geometry-and-force yield.}\label{fig:joint-transfer}\label{fig:seed-replication}
\end{figure}

Joint yield rises from 8.15 to 29.69\% for EDM and from 9.47 to 31.54\% for
GAGA. Geometry-and-force yield rises from 2.83 to 9.96\% and from 3.03 to
10.45\%, respectively. Both fits improve for both targets and both metrics.
Gaussian and harmonic one-pass FM also improve, as shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:source-head-ablation}. These experiments reuse the correction
block across generative objectives within the shared EGNN architecture.

\paragraph{Physical-head transfer and repetitions.}\label{sec:cross-head}\label{sec:seed-replication}
An earlier five-pair study transferred the physical head alone. FM-trained
heads raised GAGA joint yield from 8.75 to 25.27\%, close to 25.37\% for
GAGA-trained heads. In three additional pairs, the mean gain was 16.54 points
(crossed 95\% interval $[12.66,20.67]$). The reverse transfer raised initial
FM yield from 7.21 to 25.59\%. Appendix~\ref{app:seed-replication} gives all fits.

\paragraph{Source adaptation and hydrogen placement.}
A separate source-transfer experiment retrained EDM and GAGA with harmonic
corruption noise and attached the physical head alone. It gave 0.00\% and
0.05\% joint yield, versus 8.15\% and 9.47\% for the corresponding parents
(Appendix~\ref{app:joint-design-transfer}). The successful transfers above
preserve the target's source. An optional hydrogen flow on the initial
force-corrected generators gave 26.33\% joint yield for FM and 25.74\% for GAGA;
the main experiments use the raw outputs before this readout.
