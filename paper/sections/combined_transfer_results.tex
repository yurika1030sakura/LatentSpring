\subsection{Transfer across generative models}\label{sec:joint-transfer}
The endpoint interface allows the learned correction to be reused with another
sampler. We exchange the FM and GAGA heads within each trained pair, preserving
their tensors and calibration strength. The target retains its backbone, source
distribution and sampling schedule. Figure~\ref{fig:joint-transfer} compares each
parent with its own head and with the transferred head.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../research/figures/round2_results_v4/head_transfer.pdf}
\caption{Direct transfer of correction weights. Each line follows one training
pair through the uncorrected parent, its own fitted head, and the other model's
head. Dashed open markers show the two earlier pairs; colored filled markers
show the three new pairs. Each setting contains 1,024 attempts per pair on the
same 64 compositions. The source distribution and target backbone are preserved;
the hydrogen readout is disabled.}
\label{fig:joint-transfer}\label{fig:seed-replication}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Direct weight transfer.}\label{sec:cross-head}
Across all five pairs, the FM-trained heads raise GAGA joint yield from
8.75 to 25.27\%, close to the 25.37\% obtained with GAGA-trained heads.
In the three new pairs, the gains are 16.02, 18.36 and 15.23 percentage points:
a mean of 16.54 points with a crossed fit/composition 95\% interval of
$[12.66,20.67]$. The reverse direction raises FM joint yield from 7.21 to
25.59\% across five pairs, compared with 24.00\% for its own head. Its three
new pairs improve by 17.12 points on average (composition interval
$[13.61,20.77]$). On the two-fit additional-baseline panel, the same FM heads
raise EDM joint yield from 8.15 to 22.22\%. These transfers share an EGNN
architecture and element vocabulary while changing the generative objective.

\paragraph{Independent training repetitions.}\label{sec:seed-replication}
For heads fitted to their own parents, the three new FM runs gain 8.11,
18.46 and 20.31 joint-yield points, averaging 15.62 points with crossed
interval $[8.50,22.43]$. GAGA's corresponding average is 16.89 points.
Across five fits, graph validity changes from 20.12 to 24.86\% for FM and
from 22.60 to 26.25\% for GAGA. Figure~\ref{fig:joint-transfer} retains all five
parents, including the weaker FM initialization.

\paragraph{Transferring both designs.}
A separate experiment retrains EDM and GAGA with the tree-conditioned harmonic
noise process and attaches the same frozen FM heads. Joint yield is 0.00\%
for EDM and 0.05\% for GAGA, compared with 8.15\% and 9.47\% for their two-fit
original baselines. Thus this noise adaptation reduces quality in the tested
configuration, whereas direct head transfer improves the original generators.
Appendix~\ref{app:joint-design-transfer} gives the complete outcomes and training
setup for this experiment.

\paragraph{Optional hydrogen readout.}
Adding the conditional hydrogen flow to the corrected parents gives FM
27.40\% graph validity and 26.33\% joint yield, and GAGA 26.62\% and 25.74\%.
Their joint-yield difference is 0.59 points (composition interval $[-1.04,2.21]$).
For the three new FM fits, learned hydrogen placement reduces all-output energy
by 19.31 meV/atom relative to the fixed radial rule, with interval
$[15.17,23.67]$ for the reduction. The main comparison, ablation and weight-transfer
figure all use outputs before this readout.
