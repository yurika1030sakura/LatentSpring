\section{Reader's guide to the notation}\label{app:notation}
The method has three stages: construct random starting coordinates from the
atomic input, learn a flow toward molecular reference structures, and use physical
targets to update the trained network. The symbols below refer to these stages.

\paragraph{Structural and physical readouts.}
Geometric validity checks that all atoms belong to one contact-connected structure
and that no pair has an excessive short-distance overlap. Chemical-graph validity
also requires a chemically consistent bond graph inferred from those coordinates.
A pair of outputs is \emph{jointly valid} when both pass these checks; their
inferred chemical graphs can differ. By contrast, the local physical teacher's
same-graph restriction keeps the anchor's labeled bond orders, formal charges and
radical counts fixed.

Energy changes are compared within the same atomic composition and divided by
its atom count. Force RMS is
$[N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\|F_i\|_2^2]^{1/2}$, the root mean squared magnitude of the
atomic force vectors. Lower force RMS indicates smaller residual forces, while
the energy score measures relative energetic preference under the chosen potential.
