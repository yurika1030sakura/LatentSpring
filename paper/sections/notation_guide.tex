\section{Reader's guide to the notation}\label{app:notation}
LatentSpring first samples starting coordinates from atomic composition, then
learns a flow to reference structures, and incorporates physical feedback through
paired fine-tuning or an explicit velocity correction. This appendix collects the notation used in those stages.

\paragraph{Inputs and outputs.}
The condition $c$ fixes the atom identities, charge, and spin. Random starting
coordinates $X_0$ provide variation between generated structures. Each network
evaluation takes current coordinates $X_t$, flow time $t$, and $c$ and predicts
one velocity vector per atom. Self-conditioning uses a first prediction to form
a provisional geometry for a second pass. Integrating the velocities and adding
the specified terminal noise gives the final coordinate matrix $X_{\rm gen}$.
The learned-correction variant sets this terminal noise to zero.
The input atom identities and electronic conditions do not change. Bonds are
inferred separately for evaluation.

\paragraph{Structural and physical metrics.}
Geometric validity requires one connected contact structure with no excessive
atom overlap. Chemical-graph validity additionally requires a chemically
consistent bond graph inferred from the coordinates. Two paired outputs are
\emph{jointly valid} when both pass these checks; they need not have the same
inferred graph. This differs from the physical teacher's \emph{same-graph}
restriction, which preserves the anchor's labeled bond orders, formal charges,
and radical counts.

We compare energy within the same atomic composition and report changes per atom.
Force RMS is $[N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\|F_i\|_2^2]^{1/2}$, the root mean squared
magnitude of the atomic force vectors. Lower force RMS means smaller residual
forces. Lower energy means greater energetic preference under the chosen
potential. These are different measurements from graph validity.
