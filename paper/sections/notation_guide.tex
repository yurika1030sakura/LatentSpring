\section{Reader's guide to the notation}\label{app:notation}
LatentSpring samples starting coordinates from atomic composition, transports
them with a learned flow, and adds geometric and physical corrections. This
appendix collects the notation used in those stages and the related studies.

\paragraph{Inputs and outputs.}
The condition $c$ fixes the atom identities, charge, and spin. Random starting
coordinates $X_0$ provide variation between generated structures. Each network
evaluation takes current coordinates $X_t$, flow time $t$, and $c$ and predicts
one velocity vector per atom. Self-conditioning uses a first prediction to form
a provisional geometry for a second pass. Integrating the corrected velocities
gives the final coordinate matrix $X_{\rm gen}$. The main FM sampler has no
terminal coordinate perturbation; earlier sampling variants are described in
their own experimental sections.
The input atom identities and electronic conditions do not change. Bonds are
inferred separately for evaluation.

\paragraph{Structural and physical metrics.}
Contact support requires one connected contact structure with no excessive atom
overlap. Graph validity additionally requires a sanitized bond graph inferred
from the coordinates. The main paper's stricter geometry screen also checks
bond lengths, angles, internal distances, planarity and assigned radicals
(Appendix~\ref{app:chemical-geometry}). Joint graph-and-force yield requires
graph validity and force RMS at most 5 eV/\AA; geometry-and-force yield applies
the stricter screen at the same force threshold. In the earlier paired-update
experiments, \emph{jointly valid} instead describes a pair whose two members
pass contact and graph checks. A teacher's \emph{same-graph} restriction preserves
an anchor's labeled bonds, formal charges and radical counts.

We compare energy within the same atomic composition and report changes per atom.
Force RMS is $[N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N\|F_i\|_2^2]^{1/2}$, the root mean squared
magnitude of the atomic force vectors. Lower force RMS means smaller residual
forces. Lower energy means greater energetic preference under the chosen
potential. These are different measurements from graph validity.
