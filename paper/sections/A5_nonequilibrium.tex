\section{Non-equilibrium Work: A Corrective Framework}
\label{app:nonequilibrium}

This development branch supplies a mathematically specified correction, not a
reinterpretation of the archived molecular results or a claim of a new work
identity. Jarzynski's relation and its single-molecule pulling applications
\citep{jarzynski1997master,hummerszabo2001}, escorted transport
\citep{escorted2008}, stochastic normalizing flows \citep{snf2020}, and FEAT
\citep{feat2025} are direct prior work. Microsoft's enhanced diffusion sampling
\citep{enhanceddiffusion2026} uses steering and unbiasing relative to a pretrained
model distribution; taking that distribution as the equilibrium reference does
not establish that our FM model matches an independently specified eSEN target.

\paragraph{Generalized work and the BGFM residual.}
Fix composition, charge and spin, an orthonormal coordinate system on $H$, and
a potential $U$ including any declared confinement, with
$0<Z=\int_H e^{-\beta U(x)}\,dx<\infty$. Translation removal alone does not
ensure this condition. For a normalized prior $q_0$ and an invertible map
$x_1=\Phi_\theta(x_0)$, change of variables gives the dimensionless work
\begin{equation}
w=\beta U(x_1)+\log q_0(x_0)-\log|\det D\Phi_\theta(x_0)|
 =\beta U(x_1)+\log q_\theta(x_1).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\E_{q_0}[e^{-w}]=Z$ and
$\E_{q_\theta}[w]+\log Z=\KL(q_\theta\Vert\pi)$, where
$\pi=e^{-\beta U}/Z$. This identifies the \emph{ideal} residual as generalized
work. The archived readout is not a validated $\log q_\theta$, and variance
within disconnected off-policy clouds is neither this ensemble average nor a
constraint on global basin masses. The time parameter of an artificial
transport need not be physical molecular-dynamics time.

\paragraph{A finite-step path identity.}
Let the implemented forward path have density
$Q(x_{0:K})=q_0(x_0)\prod_{k=1}^K K_k(x_k\mid x_{k-1})$.
Choose normalized auxiliary backward kernels $L_k(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)$ and assume
the resulting target path measure is absolutely continuous with respect to $Q$.
The weight
\begin{equation}
 a(x_{0:K})=
 \frac{e^{-\beta U(x_K)}}{q_0(x_0)}
 \prod_{k=1}^K\frac{L_k(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)}
                         {K_k(x_k\mid x_{k-1})}
 \label{eq:finitework}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\E_Q[a f(x_K)]=\int_H e^{-\beta U(x)}f(x)\,dx$ for integrable $f$.
Indeed, multiplying by $Q$ cancels every forward factor; integrating the
normalized backward kernels successively leaves the stated endpoint integral.
This is ordinary path importance sampling, as used in
\citet{snf2020,feat2025}, rather than a new theorem.

Gaussian forward and backward kernels give evaluable finite-step factors
without a CNF trace. Their means may use learned flow velocities; neither
kernel needs to be the exact physical dynamics. They must describe the
actual sampled state, including the coordinate measure. The implementation
therefore uses explicit Euclidean coordinates and an orthonormal basis for
the molecular COM-free subspace. It also implements the value-only AIS
reference \citep{neal2001ais}, with work updates before Metropolis moves
invariant to each annealed density.

\paragraph{Noise and sampling efficiency are separate issues.}
An unbiased log estimate is not an unbiased exponential weight:
if $\widehat{\log q}=\log q+\epsilon$ and
$\epsilon\mid x\sim\mathcal N(0,s(x)^2)$, then
$\E[e^{-\epsilon}\mid x]=e^{s(x)^2/2}$.
For two states with true masses $(0.2,0.8)$ and variances $(0,2)$, naive noisy
reweighting gives limiting second-state mass
$0.8e/(0.2+0.8e)\simeq0.9158$. Independent replica products correct a quadratic
objective; they do not remove this exponential bias. We therefore do not use
the stochastic CNF readout as an exact work weight.

Exact weight formulas also do not imply useful effective sample size.
We report $(\sum_i a_i)^2/\sum_i a_i^2$ and the maximum normalized weight.
The raw mean weight estimates $Z$ without bias under the assumptions; its
logarithm and self-normalized observables have finite-sample bias. Weighted
endpoint clouds may supervise conditional flow matching by pairing endpoints
with independent Gaussian starts and interpolation times. The distilled
student is approximate and requires its own evaluation.

\paragraph{Evidence scope.}
New tests enumerate every path of a finite-state non-reversible example,
recover constant work for an exactly reversible Gaussian step, and check AIS
Gaussian moments and normalization, energy-offset invariance, intrinsic
coordinate measure and weighted-CFM gradients. A five-seed, known-target
mixture experiment compares unweighted paths, terminal-energy-only weighting,
AIS correction and matched FM distillation. This is an implementation and
mechanism experiment; it supplies no molecular improvement or novelty claim.
Promotion to the main empirical contribution requires matched molecular
oracle budgets, adequate ESS, independent evaluation and verified source
metadata, all beyond the mathematical identity.

\paragraph{Completed development results.}
With $8{,}192$ particles and $16$ AIS stages in each of five seeds, the target
right-basin mass is $0.8$. Mean occupancies are $0.5089$ for unweighted paths,
$0.8039$ for terminal-energy-only weighting and $0.7982$ for AIS weights.
Mean squared distance to the nearest mode is respectively $1.9616$, $0.3011$
and $0.4854$, against target $0.5$: energy-only weighting happens to get the
occupancy close while overconcentrating the within-basin distribution.
Mean AIS ESS is $10.99\%$ of the particle count.
Matched 1,000-update FM students trained on unweighted/weighted teachers attain
occupancies $0.4996/0.7630$ and spreads $1.8380/0.7113$.
Thus the weighted teacher helps this toy student, but distillation does not
preserve exact target statistics.

\paragraph{Raw-source recovery and corrected electronic states.}
Subsequent recovery of the public 4M archive provides 3,986,754 original records.
Replaying preprocessing matches all 3,941,522 accepted records bitwise in the
legacy coordinates, atom types, charge features, forces and rounded energies.
Original charge, spin and float64 energies are retained in separate sidecars.
There are 72,423 charges outside the old feature range and 678,514 non-singlets;
403,895 records have multiplicity above the minimum allowed by electron parity.
An explicit global-state adapter therefore supplies total charge and multiplicity
to the geometry model, replacing the atom-zero total-charge marker.
Matched 10,000-update data-FM continuations, with and without that adapter,
both give 32/32 successful xTB evaluations at the original electronic states.
Median strain is 3.9438 versus 3.8931 eV, respectively.
This is an electronic-state correction, not demonstrated quality improvement.

\paragraph{Independent thermodynamic reference.}
For the neutral AgBr2 doublet (validation row 1137, verified raw row 118392),
rotation-reduced randomized-Sobol integration using analytic Gaussian proposals
gives $\log\widehat Z=144093.1276$ at 3,072 points per scramble and four
independent scrambles. The estimated relative standard error is $1.67\%$,
larger than the final resolution change of 0.00146 nat. It is a statistical
reference rather than exact truth. In three-seed direct importance sampling
with 1,024 potential queries per seed, the confinement Gaussian attains mean
weight ESS 233.85 and mean absolute log-normalizer error 0.05897 nat.
The defensive ordinary and symmetry-averaged template mixtures attain ESS
44.52 and 90.35, with errors 0.15055 and 0.09840 nat. Thus symmetry averaging
helps the template proposal in this experiment but does not surpass the simple
Gaussian reference proposal. The harder eight-atom system remains strongly
weight-degenerate. Neither corrected theory nor these implementation checks
establish improved molecular generation or converged sampling on the broader
system panel.

\paragraph{Physical-temperature development and stronger controls.}
The preceding $kT=1$ eV target corresponds to approximately $11{,}604.5$ K;
its extended configurations should not be interpreted as ambient-temperature
sampling failure solely from compactness diagnostics. Subsequent experiments
fix $kT=0.025852$ eV (300 K) and retain the explicit restraint
$U=E_{\rm eSEN}+0.05\sum_i\|x_i\|^2$. They target this approximate potential
and labelled COM-free coordinate measure, not an unconfined DFT ensemble.
An independent shape-mixture reference for AgBr2 uses eight randomized-Sobol
scrambles of 8,192 points each, with an estimated normalizer relative standard
error of $1.82\%$. Its pilot uses independent Gaussian quadrature, not learned
proposal samples. Refinement reuses 16,384 verified prefix evaluations and adds
49,176 potential evaluations including symmetry and batching checks.

Three separately trained Gaussian-path proposals use 500 updates, batch 16,
16 transitions and the same 8,512-query training/evaluation protocol. Their
noise amplitude decreases as $(1-t)^{1/2}$. An additional fresh evaluation of
4,096 paths per proposal yields ESS 581.18, 654.38 and 353.98, with
$\log\widehat Z-\log\widehat Z_{\rm ref}=-0.08614,-0.05316,-0.12530$ nat.
Estimated relative standard errors are $3.84\%,3.58\%,5.08\%$, in addition to
reference error. The three arms share evaluation random numbers; their error
agreement is not independent replication. A second predeclared independent
stream of 4,096 paths gives differences $-0.02384,-0.01661,-0.00368$ nat.
Combining both streams, without selecting either, yields ESS 802.65, 823.79,
640.28 out of 8,192 and differences $-0.05450,-0.03472,-0.06264$ nat.
Combined empirical relative errors are $3.35\%,3.30\%,3.79\%$, with the shared
$1.82\%$ reference error still additional. The first-stream discrepancy is thus
not sufficient evidence of persistent bias. Finite weight moments cannot reveal
entirely missed tails, so these checks do not certify calibration.

Equal-query MALA and HMC controls choose eight random FM starts and retain
eight correlated draws per chain after longer trajectories. Both spend 8,512
potential queries including rejected proposals; uncertainty is clustered by
chain and excludes unknown initialization bias. The maximum Ag--Br distance
is 3.826 A for MALA and 2.558 A for HMC, against reference 2.537 A.
Independent xTB relaxations converge for 27/32 and 32/32 respectively, with
successful-only median strains 0.487 and 0.649 eV. HMC is thus a materially
stronger control than the earlier short MALA run; no learned-method superiority
is established. Equal potential-query counts do not equate backbone calls,
wall time or independent sample counts.

On the eight-atom condition, three cold training arms improve relaxed geometry
but retain ESS approximately one out of 256. This failure remains unresolved.
The fixed-noise AgBr2 joint control, evaluated with the same two fresh streams,
attains ESS 886.45/8,192 and log-normalizer difference $-0.03239$ nat; the
annealed energy-gradient control gives ESS 13.72/8,192 and difference $-5.06827$
nat. Thus the energy-gradient ablation collapses in this condition, whereas
these results do not establish an advantage of noise annealing over fixed noise.
Both controls use one training seed, and additional inference cost is separate.

A fixed-observation log-variance path loss from the established framework of
\citet{richter2024improvedsampling} passes implementation checks but fails after
500 eight-atom updates: final ESS is 1/256, the overlap heuristic is 99.22\%,
and xTB converges for 25/32 attempts with successful median strain 316.30 eV,
versus initial 32/32 and 4.405 eV. A frozen 16-batch diagnostic measures about
37.6 times greater forward gradient covariance trace after correcting the
population scale, relative to the pathwise mean-work estimator. This does not
identify a unique cause of failure: the backward objectives differ as well.
The distinction between joint log-variance and reverse-KL updates is already
studied by \citet{sanokowski2025rethinking}. We retain this failed recipe and
make no new-objective claim. Continuing the three original arms from 500 to 1,500 updates gives cumulative
cost 25,024 queries each. Fixed-joint, annealed-joint and energy-gradient arms
retain ESS 1.174, 2.218 and 1.092 out of 256, respectively. All three xTB panels
converge for 32/32 attempts, with median strains 0.494, 0.529 and 0.140 eV.
The matched HMC control gives 32/32 and 0.561 eV, with correlated retained draws.
Thus longer training improves some geometry statistics but does not establish
calibrated sampling or a total-compute advantage.

\paragraph{Global auxiliary and endpoint-score diagnostics.}
Reparameterizing the original 16-transition sampler in its independent Gaussian
innovations gives $x=F(z)+s\epsilon$, with $z\in\mathbb R^{336}$ and
$x\in\mathbb R^{21}$ for the eight-atom condition. The map $F$ returns the
original last conditional mean. A triangular change of variables preserves the
forward path measure and every endpoint. We compare the sequential auxiliary
with normalized global Gaussians of precision $I+\gamma J^TJ/s^2$, where $J$
is the Jacobian of $F$ at a four-iteration Gauss--Newton approximate posterior
mode and $\gamma\in\{0,0.01,0.1,1\}$. Initialization depends on the endpoint
only, never on its generating noise. A 32-path screen gives sequential ESS
1.915 and global ESS 1.012, 1.001, 1.000 and 1.000. Mode residuals remain large
on some paths. This failed approximation is not a result about all converged
or non-Gaussian auxiliary inference methods. It costs 32 new energy evaluations,
besides four engineering-smoke evaluations; coordinates and model weights
remain unchanged.

The standard relative-entropy chain rule separates an endpoint error and an
auxiliary approximation gap:
\[
\mathrm{KL}(Q_\theta\Vert\pi L_\phi)
=\mathrm{KL}(q_\theta\Vert\pi)
+\mathbb E_{q_\theta(x)}\mathrm{KL}(Q_\theta(z\mid x)\Vert L_\phi(z\mid x)).
\]
We therefore implement a separate endpoint-gradient prototype. Under the usual
differentiation assumptions and with the true proposal score, its update is
$\mathbb E[(s_{q_\theta}(x)-s_\pi(x))^T\partial_\theta x]$; the score vectors
are held fixed during differentiation. Score-difference generator updates
are prior work, including \citet{yin2024dmd}. For an actual last Gaussian
innovation, denoising regression estimates the score of that very endpoint
sampler, without interpreting an arbitrary FM velocity as a score.

A scalar known-answer check separates these objectives: for
$x=az+0.2\epsilon$, target variance $0.25$ and an independent fixed reverse
auxiliary, joint-KL training collapses to variance $0.04$. Five fitted linear
score-critic seeds reach variances $0.24818$--$0.25142$, whereas a stale critic
again collapses. This elementary mechanism example does not identify the cause
of the molecular failures or establish a new objective.

Before allowing any molecular generator update, we fit a small invariant energy
critic on 4,096 frozen-proposal conditional means with fresh final innovations.
Two-layer message passing uses actual element, charge, spin and temperature
conditions. The initial independent assessment uses 2,048 different parent paths.
We retain raw DSM, scaled IID, grouped IID, antithetic and mean-control-variate
training, all with 500 updates and matched score-point evaluation counts.
Control variates for small-noise DSM are already established
\citep{birrell2025ndsm}. Only the antithetic arm initially passes both a paired
denoising-loss comparison and four necessary Stein-moment checks.

That initial pass fails independent confirmation on 8,192 newly generated
parents: the selected critic's largest standardized Stein residual is 4.60,
above the prespecified threshold 3; a second training seed reaches 11.84.
Longer 3,000-update antithetic runs reach 18.61 and 12.87; matched grouped-IID
runs reach 26.20 and 31.83. This panel becomes development data after the first
confirmation. All four longer runs fail. We consequently make no molecular
actor update and no sampling-improvement claim. These score experiments require
no new energy-oracle evaluations, but their neural computation is retained in
the run records. Neither denoising improvement nor a small-panel pass is a
complete score-accuracy certificate.

\paragraph{Conservative residual score calibration: partial improvement.}
We implement a subsequent, explicitly limited calibration candidate.
For a fixed proposal score estimate $s_0$, conservative fields
$f_j=\nabla\psi_j$ and a valid Stein identity under the actual generator law $q$,
define $G_{ij}=\mathbb E_q[f_i^Tf_j]$ and
$r_j=\mathbb E_q[s_0^Tf_j+\nabla\cdot f_j]$.
The correction $s_c=s_0-\sum_jc_jf_j$ has Fisher-risk change
\[
\mathbb E_q\|s_c-s_q\|^2-\mathbb E_q\|s_0-s_q\|^2
=c^TGc-2c^Tr.
\]
This is a standard finite-feature score-matching projection, related to
established Stein score estimation \citep{li2018stein,shi2018spectral}; it is
not claimed as a new identity. At population moments, constrained ridge
minimization with zero correction feasible cannot increase this risk. With
$\psi_0=\|x\|^2/2$ and bounded radial potentials, the constraint
$c_0\geq-\kappa_0/2$ retains a positive quadratic tail and hence a normalizable
critic. Finite estimated moments need separate validation; the calibrated
critic density is not substituted for the generator density.

We fit four directions (scale and radial centers 1, 2, 3 Angstrom, width 0.5)
on the existing 8,192-parent development panel, with ridge 0.01 after feature
RMS scaling. Assessment uses 16,384 newly generated parents, with independent
parent/noise seeds 9117/9118. Both frozen neural seeds are retained, along with
a Gaussian-base-plus-identical-calibration ablation. On this new panel,
the Stein estimates of risk change per coordinate are
$-0.1567\pm0.0334$ and $-0.8920\pm0.0740$ (mean $\pm$ SEM).
Both calibrated neural scores beat the calibrated Gaussian in paired denoising
loss. These are limited score-estimation improvements, not molecular sampling
improvements or a validated novel learning algorithm.

Both seeds pass all four fitted moment checks on new samples, but only 2/10
and 4/10 additional, unfitted checks pass. Those checks use six shifted radial
centers with width 0.35, two size functions and two symmetric triangle-angle
functions, with exact divergence checks. Failures reach 20.72 and 13.23 SEM.
Thus the four-direction correction leaves substantial errors and does not
qualify either critic for an actor update. All data, coefficients, tail
constraints and fitted/unfitted diagnostics are preserved in
\texttt{research/evidence/score\_calibration\_audit\_v1.json}.

\paragraph{Learned-feature refitting and its remaining failure.}
We next freeze the invariant critic's 32 final-head features and fit their
score coefficients, together with the quadratic scale feature, by constrained
ridge score matching. Deep-feature score matching and linear solves are prior
work \citep{wenliang2019deepkernels}. Antithetic final-noise pairs estimate the
Stein terms at the actual finite noise level, without neural Hessian traces.
The 16,384-parent panel is now development data for this variant. Both learned
seeds outperform a random 33-feature control and a stronger 105-feature,
element-pair-specific radial control in paired denoising error. However, only
4/14 and 6/14 moment checks pass; maximum standardized residuals are 24.70 and
13.39. The improved score-risk estimates do not qualify a score-based generator
update. Complete results remain in the reproducible learned-feature audit.

\paragraph{Exact-entropy refinement from an implicit base generator.}
To obtain a control that does not require an estimated base score, we freeze
the molecular generator $q_0$ and train a separate invertible map $T$.
For a fixed target and integrable terms, the standard change-of-variables
identity gives
\[
\mathrm{KL}(T_\#q_0\Vert\pi)-\mathrm{KL}(q_0\Vert\pi)
=\mathbb E_{q_0}\!\left[\frac{U(Tx)-U(x)}{kT}
-\log|\det_H DT(x)|\right].
\]
The unknown base entropy and target normalizer cancel. This is established
normalizing-flow/Boltzmann-generator methodology \citep{noe2019boltzmann}, not
a new objective. Carrying the original auxiliary through $T^{-1}$ also keeps
its conditional approximation gap fixed; the work increment equals the bracketed
quantity. We do not unfreeze the base generator under this objective.

The bounded linear control acts on atom coordinates as $T(x)=\exp(K)x$, where
$K=(W-\operatorname{diag}(W\mathbf1))/N$, and symmetric pair weights lie in
$[-0.25,0.25]$. It preserves COM and joint atom/element permutation and spatial
equivariance. Its exact intrinsic log volume is $3\operatorname{tr}K$.
The typed model has 13 parameters for this condition; the scalar control ties
all pair weights. Both start at identity and receive 200 updates, batch 16,
with the same cosine learning-rate schedule and 3,712 oracle queries each.

\paragraph{Implemented nonlinear species couplings.}
We implement a separate adapter that partitions the labelled COM-free space
into element-group internal displacements and element-centroid differences.
For an internal update, the conditioner replaces every active-group coordinate
with its centroid. For a centroid-pair update, it replaces both group centroids
with their count-weighted common centroid while retaining internal displacements.
Thus the context is constant along the active coordinates. A shared invariant
two-layer message network (width32,24 radial features) produces scalar coefficients
and equivariant directions from this context, with element/role embeddings and
charge, spin and target-temperature inputs. No dropout or batch normalization
couples different samples. Context derivatives are retained in the full map.

With fixed context, the point map is
\[
 F(u)=\lambda u+\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_j\operatorname{sigmoid}(b_j^\top u+c_j)b_j
       +a_r\frac{u}{\sqrt{\ell^2+\|u\|^2}}.
\]
The parameterization enforces $\lambda=\exp[.25\tanh(s)]$,
$\|b_j\|\leq1/\ell$, $|a_j|\leq2\beta\lambda\ell^2/M$,
and $|a_r|\leq\beta\lambda\ell/2$, with $\beta=.25$.
Consequently $\|DF-\lambda I\|\leq\beta\lambda$ and $DF$ is positive definite.
For a group of $n$ atoms with zero-sum internal coordinates, use
$T_i(u)=F(u_i)-n^{-1}\sum_jF(u_j)$. Writing $A_i=DF(u_i)$, its exact
intrinsic log volume is
\[
 \log\det_H DT=\sum_i\log\det A_i+
       \log\det\!\left(\frac1n\sum_i A_i^{-1}\right).
\]
The second term is the translation-subspace Schur complement; omitting it
gives the wrong volume. Only $3\times3$ matrices need factorization.
The centered residual fixed-point inverse contracts by at most $\beta$.
Centroid-pair layers apply the uncentered point map plus a bounded equivariant
shift; opposite count-weighted centroid movements preserve total COM.
Their determinant is the ordinary three-dimensional determinant. This is
a reconstruction inverse; training differentiates the forward map and exact
volume without differentiating the iterative inverse.

The molecular composition uses internal-group layers followed by adjacent
element-centroid pairs in atomic-number order. It has seven layers and19,365
parameters for the eight-atom condition. Zero final heads initialize identity.
Complete intrinsic Jacobians, inverse composition, O(3) and joint atom/element
equivariance, parameter finite differences and independent batch evaluation
are checked, including a200-atom reconstruction test. Passive-context-poor or
homogeneous systems restrict angular expressivity; no universality is claimed.

Equivariant coupling flows \citep{midgley2023eacf,bose2021equivariantfinite},
strongly convex gradient maps \citep{huang2020convex}, and contractive residual
flows \citep{chen2019residual} are direct prior art. The constrained-volume
identity and relative-KL objective are established mathematics. Whether this
particular molecular decomposition provides a useful architectural contribution

\paragraph{Ablating active nonlinearity with the same neural context.}
The13-parameter typed-linear baseline does not isolate the benefit of the
nonlinear point transform from that of its19,365-parameter conditioner.
We therefore retain the exact same neural parameters, group splits, coupling
schedule, initialization and learning-rate schedule, and replace each active
point map by its tangent $F(0)+DF(0)u$. The full conditioned composition remains
nonlinear. For internal-group updates, centering cancels $F(0)$ and the
log volume is $(n_g-1)\log\det DF(0)$; centroid-pair updates retain the offset
and use $\log\det DF(0)$. A linear solve gives the active-coordinate inverse.
Both families pass complete-context Jacobian, inverse and gradient checks.

Both fixed1,000-update affine controls use16,512 queries, matching their
convex partners. On the shared2,048-row confirmation, affine-context
$\Delta\mathrm{KL}$ is $-0.16118\pm0.02692$ and $-0.15405\pm0.02655$ nat.
The paired convex-minus-affine differences are respectively
$-0.01332\pm0.00252$ and $-0.01259\pm0.00215$ nat (row SEM).
Thus a smaller active-map benefit has the same sign in both training pairs
under this fixed recipe. These are one-condition comparisons with shared
evaluation noise, not independent cross-molecule confirmation. The second
affine model still has path ESS1.0014/2,048. The point-map ablation does not
resolve weight degeneracy or establish superiority to external flow methods.
Both affine confirmations reuse stored base and convex energies; their own
training, smoke and confirmation cost37,184 additional oracle queries.
