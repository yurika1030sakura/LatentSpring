\section{Independent-backbone comparison}\label{app:matched-generators}
This experiment tests the source without pretraining on a second architecture.
All methods use the official EDM equivariant graph neural network (EGNN)
\citep{edm}, with four message-passing layers, 256 hidden features, and
2,381,566 parameters. We repeat training from two independent initializations.
Within each repetition, all methods start from identical weights and receive the
same minibatches. Atom types are fixed inputs throughout training and generation.

\paragraph{Data.}
The shared data contain 20,000 OMol25 training structures from 8,323 compositions
and 512 internal validation structures from 299 other compositions. Both sets
contain neutral organic singlets with 8--64 atoms. A hash of composition assigns
structures to training or internal validation. Selection requires the geometric
and chemical-graph checks, but not the additional scaffold-tree filter used in
the pretrained-model experiments. None of the 64 evaluation compositions occurs
in either selected subset or either full processed corpus.

\paragraph{Optimization.}
Each model receives 30,000 AdamW updates at learning rate $10^{-4}$ and batch
size 32, for 960,000 training presentations. Each batch contains molecules with
the same atom count. We sample atom-count groups in proportion to their sizes,
so each training row has the same marginal sampling probability. We clip the
gradient norm at one. Generation uses an exponential moving average of weights
at the final update, with decay $\min(0.999,(1+s)/(10+s))$ at optimizer step
$s$. Internal validation at 5,000, 15,000, and 30,000 updates monitors learning;
it is not used to choose a checkpoint.

\paragraph{Flow-matching models.}
Gaussian and harmonic FM share the linear interpolation, atom matching, and
rotation alignment. Both predict velocity in one EGNN pass and use 64 midpoint
steps, giving 128 network evaluations. Neither uses self-conditioning. The
harmonic distribution is the same as in the main model, but its factorized edge
weights permit a different exact tree sampler. We draw the $N-2$ entries of a
Pr\"ufer sequence independently with probabilities $u_i/\sum_j u_j$ and decode
the sequence into a tree. This samples the same tree law used to place atoms.

\paragraph{Diffusion models.}
EDM predicts Gaussian coordinate noise under its original variance-preserving
schedule. Position-conditional GAGA \citep{qu2026gaga} truncates this schedule at
650 of 1,000 steps, using the published GEOM setting. Its initial Gaussian has
variance $\alpha_t^2 v+\sigma_t^2$, where $\alpha_t$ and $\sigma_t$ are the
signal and noise coefficients. The training-only estimate
$v=3.0232257866$\,\AA$^2$ is the mean squared centered coordinate per independent
coordinate. We transfer the truncation setting from GEOM without optimizing it
on OMol25 evaluation results.

\paragraph{Generation and compute.}
Every model generates 32 raw structures per evaluation composition at 128 network
evaluations. We also evaluate the complete EDM and GAGA reverse schedules at
1,001 and 651 calls, including their final observation steps. This experiment
uses no physical energy labels, bond supervision, energy-based selection, or
geometry optimization. Scheduler metadata record the A100, H200, and A100 MIG
allocations. Equal training presentations and network-call counts do not imply
equal wall-clock time.
