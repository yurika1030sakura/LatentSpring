\section{Learning a physical correction along the flow}\label{app:force-correction}
The correction network learns physical changes at provisional endpoints while
keeping every parent parameter fixed. Its regression target is separate from
the backbone's flow-matching objective.

\paragraph{States and targets.}
The parent generates two trajectories for each of 128 training compositions,
with 32 compositions in each of the 8--16, 17--24, 25--32, and 33--40 atom ranges.
We record the second velocity evaluation at midpoint steps 23 and 29, corresponding
to flow times 0.734375 and 0.921875. These 256 trajectories yield 512 states.
At a recorded state, the parent velocity defines the provisional endpoint
$H_t=X_t+(1-t)v_{\rm base}(X_t,t;c)$. This is an extrapolation from the current
prediction after both parent self-conditioning passes; later changes in velocity
can change the final generated coordinates.

We evaluate the mirror-averaged eSEN force at $H_t$, subtract its mean across
atoms, and construct the force shift defined in Equation~\ref{eq:local-force-shift}.
The Gaussian width is at most 0.03\,\AA\ and the molecular displacement norm is
at most 0.1\,\AA. The regression target is
$\delta(H_t)/(1-t)$, a velocity correction whose linear endpoint extrapolation
has displacement $\delta(H_t)$. All recorded states are retained, including
states whose provisional geometry fails the chemical-graph check. Compositions
are separated by metadata hashes: 96 train the head, and 32 provide internal
validation, with four recorded states per composition. These are subsets of the
parent's training compositions, separate from generator confirmation conditions.

\paragraph{Equivariant pair correction.}
The small model has 8,178 parameters. For each unordered atom pair, it computes
symmetric scalar features from atomic embeddings, the two pair distances in
$X_t$ and $H_t$, their directional alignment, time, atom count, and covalent
radii. A two-hidden-layer network, each layer containing 64 units with SiLU
activations, predicts two coefficients $\alpha_{ij}$ and $\beta_{ij}$. A hyperbolic tangent
bounds both coefficients between $-1$ and $1$.

Write $r_{ij}=X_{t,i}-X_{t,j}$ and $\widehat r_{ij}=H_{t,i}-H_{t,j}$. We normalize these
vectors as $\bar r_{ij}=r_{ij}/\sqrt{\|r_{ij}\|^2+\ell_{ij}^2}$ and likewise
for $\overline{\widehat r}_{ij}$, using the covalent-radius sum $\ell_{ij}$. The contact weight
$w_{ij}=\operatorname{sigmoid}((1.25-\|\widehat r_{ij}\|/\ell_{ij})/0.2)$ decreases as the
predicted atoms move apart. It is a geometric weight, not a supplied bond label.
Let $d_i=\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}$ and $d_{\max}=\max(1,\max_i d_i)$. The correction
for atom $i$ is
\begin{equation}
 A_{\phi,i}=v_{\rm cap}\,t^2\sum_{j\ne i}
 \frac{w_{ij}}{2d_{\max}}
 \left(\alpha_{ij}\bar r_{ij}+\beta_{ij}\overline{\widehat r}_{ij}\right),
 \qquad v_{\rm cap}=2\text{ \AA\ per unit flow time}.
\end{equation}
The symmetric coefficients and opposite pair vectors give zero total correction.
Rotating the coordinates rotates the correction, and relabeling atoms relabels
its outputs. These pair constructions follow standard equivariant-network
practice \citep{satorras2021egnn}. The triangle inequality gives
$\|A_{\phi,i}\|\leq v_{\rm cap}t^2$ for every atom. This bounds the instantaneous velocity
addition, not the final displacement after interaction with the parent flow.
The final layer starts at zero, reproducing the parent field before training.

We fit the correction with mean squared velocity error, AdamW at learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, batch size one, and gradient clipping at one. Two seeds use
20,000 updates each. Only the head is optimized. We compare a 37,540-parameter
pair network with wider layers and a 37,586-parameter alternative with two
rounds of neighborhood message passing and invariant angular summaries. All
receive the same training-state sequence and target vectors within a seed.

On the internally withheld compositions, the contextual model has about 15\%
lower target-prediction error than the small pair model and about 11\% lower
error than the nearly parameter-matched wide model at 20,000 updates. The
confirmation experiment below tests whether this predictive difference improves
actual generation. Physics-based residual correction of frozen flow fields has
also been studied for video generation \citep{wang2026pila}; here supervision is
provided by molecular forces at predicted atomic geometries.

\paragraph{Sampling and preparation cost.}
Generation integrates $v_{\rm base}+A_\phi$ with the same 32 midpoint steps.
Each attempted structure uses 128 backbone evaluations and 64 head evaluations,
with no terminal noise, energy call, output filtering, or geometry optimization.
The parent control carries a zero-output head so its sampling operations match.
The training-state collection uses 256 parent trajectories. Mirror-paired force
labels for the 512 recorded endpoints require 1,024 eSEN queries. The study also
queries eight translated Gaussian particles per state for a complete-work
control, bringing the actual preparation expenditure to 9,216 queries. The
force-target heads do not use those particle energies. The local-work teacher
has median particle effective sample size 1.37 out of eight; its earlier
2,000-update student comparison did not establish an additional generation gain.
