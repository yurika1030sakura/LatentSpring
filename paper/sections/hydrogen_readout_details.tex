\subsection{A conditional flow for hydrogen coordinates}\label{app:hydrogen-readout}
Hydrogen fragmentation can invalidate an otherwise connected heavy-atom skeleton. Among 512 cached training outputs per generator, FM and GAGA produce 346 and 314 connected heavy skeletons, but 60 and 18 outputs have hydrogen-only fragmentation. We therefore train a conditional flow to arrange hydrogens around fixed heavy atoms. Hydrogen decoration is also used by generators such as Quetzal \citep{cheng2025quetzal}; here it is a selective readout of an existing generated structure.

\paragraph{Training.}
The network takes all coordinates, atomic numbers, and completion time $s\in[0,1]$, and predicts a velocity for each hydrogen. We perturb reference hydrogens with Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.4\,\AA, holding heavy atoms fixed. Half of examples perturb every hydrogen; the other half perturb each independently with probability one half. Minimum squared-distance assignment pairs perturbed coordinates $Y_0$ with reference coordinates $Y_1$. The path and loss are
\begin{equation}
 Y_s=(1-s)Y_0+sY_1,\qquad
 \mathcal L_H=\EE\!\left[\frac{1}{3N_H}
 \left\|u_\psi(X_{\rm heavy},Y_s,s;z)-(Y_1-Y_0)\right\|_F^2\right].
\end{equation}
Here $X_{\rm heavy}$ contains heavy-atom coordinates, $z$ denotes atomic numbers, $N_H$ is the hydrogen count, and $u_\psi$ is the hydrogen velocity. A common translation centers the input. Training uses only coordinates and elements.

The four-layer, width-64 EGNN has 153,790 parameters, including 1,001 fixed schedule entries. Two independently initialized models each receive 10,000 updates at batch size 32 on the shared 20,000 OMol25 references, with learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$ and EMA decay 0.999. We use the final EMA. These fits require 640,000 example-forwards in total and serve both FM and GAGA.

\paragraph{Readout and controls.}
The parents use fixed strength-four physical corrections. Readout activates if any hydrogen is farther from every heavy atom than 1.25 times the summed covalent radii; otherwise coordinates are unchanged. We integrate from $s=0$ to $1$ for four midpoint steps, moving all hydrogens and capping each velocity at 2\,\AA\ per unit completion time. Eight small-network calls are required per activated output. Heavy atoms remain fixed, and a final common translation restores the original centroid. No physical evaluator is used.

The radial control moves each detached hydrogen toward its nearest heavy atom to the summed-radius distance, preserving all other relative positions. A second control applies the learned flow only to detached hydrogens. Both start from the same parent coordinates, allowing us to separate simple attachment from joint hydrogen placement.

\paragraph{Selection and confirmation.}
On 16 development compositions, we compare the radial rule with both learned modes starting at time zero or one half. Moving all hydrogens from zero gives the largest FM joint count, 96/256 versus 95/256 for the radial rule, but not an improvement in both fits. Its lower energy motivates a separate prespecified test: reduced mean energy per atom with retained pooled graph and joint yield. We freeze this hypothesis and model before evaluating 30 new compositions, 15 each with 17--28 and 29--40 atoms. Sixteen draws per composition and fit give 960 attempts per setting. Composition intervals retain both fitted models.

For FM, the learned readout lowers energy by 15.21 meV/atom relative to the radial rule (95\% interval $[10.10,21.71]$), with both fits improving. Graph and joint yield differ by only $+0.10$ points ($[-0.21,0.42]$). Relative to the unchanged parent, graph validity increases by 2.08 points ($[1.15,3.13]$) and joint yield by 1.88 ($[1.04,2.71]$), while every previously graph-valid output is preserved. Moving all rather than only detached hydrogens lowers energy by a further 10.25 meV/atom ($[6.96,14.72]$) with identical validity counts. GAGA likewise gains 3.10 meV/atom over the radial rule ($[1.04,6.34]$).

With the shared readout, FM exceeds GAGA in graph validity by 3.33 points ($[0.52,6.15]$); the joint-yield difference is $+2.29$ ($[-0.63,5.21]$). Energy averages include all outputs. Among 261 FM pairs valid under both readouts, 18 are newly repaired; on this descriptive subset the learned flow lowers energy by 83.81 meV/atom. These 18 pairs are not used for selection or the primary interval.
