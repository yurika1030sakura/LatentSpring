\subsection{New compositions and independent physical evaluation}\label{sec:fresh-physics}
We evaluate four frozen models on 24 new compositions: Gaussian, harmonic,
harmonic with the force-only update, and harmonic with the complete-work update.
The force-only model was selected as the primary physical model before this test.
Eight compositions fall in each of the 17--20, 21--24, and 25--28 atom ranges;
none appears in either processed corpus. There are 6,144 raw outputs in total.
Appendix~\ref{app:data-evaluation} describes the fixed metadata-based selection.

The harmonic source improves graph validity over Gaussian by 8.20 percentage
points (95\% CI $[5.08,11.33]$), with gains in both runs. Adding the force update
changes harmonic validity by $+0.72$ points, with interval $[-0.98,2.34]$.
Thus the measured validity rate remains similar after physical fine-tuning.
Every accepted output has a distinct inferred connectivity within its composition
in these draws.

\paragraph{Energy and force scores.}
We evaluate every output at its saved coordinates using eSEN and gas-phase
GFN2-xTB single-point energies and gradients \citep{gfn2xtb}. GFN2 is used only
for evaluation, independently of the eSEN teacher. Four of the 6,144 generated
structures have failed GFN2 calculations; all four are graph-invalid. Calculations
succeed for every graph-valid output, and reference inversion checks pass.
eSEN uses the mirror averages described in Section~\ref{sec:flow}.
Force RMS is the root mean squared magnitude of the atomic force vectors.

The force update lowers energy and force RMS among paired, jointly valid outputs
under both potentials; every composition contributes pairs in both runs. The
directions also hold across all eSEN outputs and all paired successful GFN2
calculations, regardless of graph validity. Without physical updates, harmonic
also lowers aggregate energy relative to Gaussian, predominantly in one run.

Complete-work and force-only updates have similar measured scores. On paired,
jointly valid outputs, the complete-work-minus-force energy difference is
$-0.00413$ eV/atom under eSEN (interval $[-0.00903,0.00078]$) and $-0.00363$
under GFN2 ($[-0.00792,0.00075]$). Force and graph-validity intervals also include
zero. We therefore use the cheaper force-only teacher as the primary method;
these comparisons do not resolve an additional benefit from work weighting.
