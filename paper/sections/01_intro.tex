\begin{abstract}
Energy values from universal molecular potentials offer an auxiliary training signal for bond-free
3D generators, but an improvement in a density-based diagnostic need not establish improved sampling.
We study group-wise log-variance regularisation of a FlowMol3 backbone trained on OMol25.
The archived implementation improves a local scalar-readout correlation with independent GFN2-xTB
energies from $0.200$ to $0.369$ on $93$ filtered held-out parents, and top-1 retrieval from
$20.2\%$ to $29.6\%$. An implementation audit finds that this readout integrates the network's
endpoint prediction as a velocity and omits trajectory and prior gradients; it is therefore not a
validated likelihood of the generator. On a fixed-checkpoint, fixed-geometry resolution sweep, the
value-minus-FM correlation gap decreases from $0.175$ at $12$ steps to $0.042$ at $48$ steps.
An independent generation-strain evaluation provides no evidence of improved sample quality, and raw
energy calibration does not improve. We distinguish these observations from the ideal grouped
objective's identifiability properties, provide a corrected clamped-flow implementation with analytic
and interface tests, and specify the remaining experiments needed to establish a sampling claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

De novo molecular generators jointly produce atom identities and three-dimensional coordinates
\citep{edm,geoldm,semlaflow,flowmol3}. Universal molecular potentials such as those trained on OMol25
\citep{omol25,esen} provide energy labels across diverse compositions. This suggests combining a
flow-matching data objective with a penalty that encourages the learned log-density to track negative
energy. The underlying log-variance divergence and its off-policy estimation are established ideas
\citep{vargrad,nusken2021pathspace,richter2024improvedsampling,sendera2024offpolicy,ldr2026}; our
question is whether this intervention works reliably inside a mixed discrete--continuous molecular
flow.

There are three separate requirements. The scalar used in the energy loss must correspond to a
specified normalised density; its numerical estimate must be accurate enough for the claimed
comparison; and improving that comparison must transfer to the generated structures. Grouped
correlation alone does not check any of these. Even a valid composition-clamped positional density
need not equal the conditional law of a sampler that also evolves discrete labels, maintains
self-conditioning history, and applies geometric retractions.

We therefore report the archived results together with an implementation and evidence audit. The
positive observation is local ordering and retrieval under a fixed legacy readout. Its limitations
are substantive: an endpoint/velocity mismatch, a frozen-trajectory gradient, solver-sensitive
contrasts, poor calibration, incomplete training runs, and no demonstrated generation benefit.
These are unresolved scientific issues, not changes in terminology. Corrected code passes analytic and real FlowMol3 interface tests; no archived result used that
path. Appendix~\ref{app:newdevelopment} reports preliminary conditional-flow experiments.

The study contributes a reproducible accounting of the archived intervention, a distinction between

generators, or a general advantage of energy values over forces.
