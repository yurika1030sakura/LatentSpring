\paragraph{Direct-distillation outcome.}
Both continuations finish all three 1,000-update student fits. The 512 generated
FIT anchors yield 351 graph-supported anchors, of which 349 have at least one
supported escorted particle. All original draws and unsupported particles remain
in the records. Mean local particle ESS by composition is about 1.27--1.87 out of
8, indicating substantial weight concentration and limited finite-particle
resolution of the local target.

On paired common-graph support, complete work versus the unweighted escort reduces
mean energy by 0.00758 eV/atom (conditional paired 95\% interval
$[-0.01424,-0.00113]$ for the signed difference) and force RMS by 0.16174 eV/\AA\
(interval $[-0.27660,-0.05590]$). Both continuation signs favor complete work.
All-output readouts agree in direction. Thus an incremental physical-quality
signal is present relative to the matched escort student.

However, all direct continuations harm the frozen model. Complete work has graph
support 55.16\%, versus 62.11\% for frozen, and common-supported energy is
\emph{higher} by 0.10587 eV/atom (interval $[0.09009,0.12168]$).
The prespecified adoption gate therefore fails. The matched physics increment does
not establish an overall generator improvement. The replay control shows that
plain continuation on these mixed targets already produces substantial regression;
it does not uniquely identify the cause of that regression.

All 512 FIT source draws, 5,120 evaluation outputs, local work values, support
masks, selections, checkpoints and physical readouts are audited. Teacher
preparation uses 6,232 raw energy/force queries: 702 at anchors and 5,530 at
supported particles. An unweighted escort could omit the particle-energy queries;
replay could omit all physical queries. Evaluating the four generated-output arms
uses another 10,240 queries, reported separately from inference cost. No global
Boltzmann, output-ESS, work-law novelty or successful direct-distillation claim
follows from this result.
