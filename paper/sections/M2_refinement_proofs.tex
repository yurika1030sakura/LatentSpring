\section{Constrained volume and entropy identities}
\label{app:refinement-proofs}

\paragraph{Point-map derivative bound.}
Writing $s_j=\operatorname{sigmoid}(b_j^\top u+c_j)$, differentiation gives
\[
 DF(u)=\lambda I+\sum_j a_j s_j(1-s_j)b_jb_j^\top
 +a_r\left(\frac{I}{\sqrt{\ell^2+\|u\|^2}}
 -\frac{uu^\top}{(\ell^2+\|u\|^2)^{3/2}}\right).
\]
The sigmoid derivative is at most $1/4$. The norm bounds give at most
$\beta\lambda/2$ for the summed sigmoid term. The radial matrix is positive
semidefinite with norm at most $1/\ell$, giving the same bound for its signed
contribution. All eigenvalues of $DF$ therefore lie between
$(1-\beta)\lambda$ and $(1+\beta)\lambda$. This establishes strong convexity
of the total potential, even with signed component coefficients.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:centered-volume}.}
The restriction of $\sum_i\Phi(u_i)$ to $H_g$, where $\nabla\Phi=F$, is strongly
convex. Its constrained gradient is $P_gF(u)$ and is globally invertible.
Alternatively, writing $F(u)=\lambda u+G(u)$, the residual map $G$ has Lipschitz
constant at most $\beta\lambda$. Projection has operator norm one, so
$u\mapsto\lambda^{-1}[y-P_gG(u)]$ is a contraction on $H_g$. Its unique fixed
point reconstructs the preimage for every $y\in H_g$.

For the determinant, let $D=\operatorname{diag}(A_1,\ldots,A_{n_g})$,
let $B$ be an orthonormal basis of $H_g$, and let
$C=\mathbf1_{n_g}\otimes I_3/\sqrt{n_g}$ span the translation complement.
The concatenation $[B,C]$ is orthogonal and the constrained derivative is
$B^\top DB$. The Schur-complement identity and block inversion give
\[
 \det(B^\top DB)=\det(D)\det(C^\top D^{-1}C)
 =\prod_i\det(A_i)\det\left(\frac1{n_g}\sum_iA_i^{-1}\right).
\]
All factors are positive. This proves \eqref{eq:centered-volume}.

\paragraph{Full-layer and symmetry accounting.}
In internal/passive coordinates, each layer keeps the passive coordinates
fixed and changes only the active coordinates. Its full Jacobian is therefore
block triangular. Cross-context derivatives enter gradients of the complete
map, but do not change its block-diagonal determinant. Centroid-pair coordinate
changes are fixed linear conjugations, so their count-dependent volume factors
cancel. Composition adds the layer log volumes. Group definitions, centering
and schedules commute with joint coordinate/element permutations. Invariant
scalar coefficients and equivariant context vectors make the point maps and
centroid shifts commute with orthogonal spatial transformations. Numerical
checks include every context dependency and independent batch rows, rather
than only a frozen-context partial Jacobian.

\paragraph{Uniform volume bound for a sweep.}
Active derivative eigenvalues have the same lower and upper bounds on each
constrained block. The sum of active dimensions in one sweep is
$3\sum_g(n_g-1)+3(G-1)=3(n-1)=d$. Thus
\[
 d[-0.25+\log(1-\beta)]\leq\log\det_HDT
 \leq d[0.25+\log(1+\beta)].
\]
For multiple sweeps these bounds add. This is a stability property of the
bounded parameterization, not a coverage or expressivity guarantee.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:relative-kl}.}
On the declared measure, $q_T(Tx)=q_0(x)/\det_HDT(x)$. Substitution in
$\KL(q_T\Vert\pi)=\mathbb E_{q_T}[\log q_T+U/(kT)+\log Z]$ and subtraction
of the corresponding source expression yield \eqref{eq:relative-kl}.
The assumptions ensure that subtraction involves well-defined finite
quantities. The entropy identity is
$h(q_T)-h(q_0)=\mathbb E_{q_0}\log\det_HDT$.
Per-sample volume is exact for the specified map; its expectation is estimated
from samples and carries statistical uncertainty.

\paragraph{Why an augmented-objective comparison needs care.}
For an augmented map with source $q_0(x)r(a\mid x)$ and target
$\pi(x)r(a\mid x)$, the initial conditional KL gap is zero. The standard
chain rule implies $\Delta\KL_{\rm joint}=\Delta\KL_x+\mathrm{Gap}_{\rm final}$,
where the last term is nonnegative. A negative joint change certifies a
marginal improvement, but ordering it against an exact marginal change does
not in general order the physical marginal errors. EACF explicitly discusses
the corresponding joint/marginal objectives \citep{midgley2023eacf}.
This distinction is retained in the external-baseline comparison plan.
