\subsection{Matched experiment on sources and physical adaptation}\label{sec:factorial-details}
To complete the four-setting comparison, we construct physical targets from the
Gaussian model's own outputs using the same procedure as for harmonic. In each
run, the frozen Gaussian model generates 32 anchors for each of the same eight
training compositions. We record all 256 attempts and discard invalid anchors
without replacing them. We retain the eight-candidate force-escort procedure,
temperature, perturbation bounds, source seeds, and 1,000-update training schedule.
For each physical student, we train a matched replay student from the same
Gaussian base and add their parameter difference to that base with coefficient
one. Within each physical/replay pair, reference examples, source randomness,
training times, and teacher-selection draws are paired.

The two Gaussian runs retain 160 and 138 anchors after candidate-support checks.
Teacher preparation uses 5,276 raw eSEN queries, including complete-work
diagnostics stored by the original procedure. The students themselves sample
uniformly from the allowed force-shifted candidates. We preserve the original
maximum preparation budget, but actual valid-anchor counts and query counts can
differ by source. Four new student fits of 1,000 updates produce the two paired
Gaussian models. The harmonic physical model and the two unadapted models reuse
their previously audited checkpoints and outputs.

The Gaussian physical models generate 1,536 unoptimized outputs on the existing
24-composition set at 128 network calls per attempt. eSEN evaluation adds
3,072 queries, and GFN2 adds 1,632 attempts including reference inversion checks.
One GFN2 calculation fails on a graph-invalid output; every graph-valid output
and reference calculation succeeds. The experiment's audit reconstructs target
proposals and graph restrictions, replays uniform candidate draws and parameter
arithmetic, and reparses the structural and physical scores. This is a fixed
follow-up on a previously evaluated set, not a new held-out composition test.

For Gaussian, physical adaptation changes energy on paired, jointly valid outputs
by $-0.01596$ eV/atom under eSEN (95\% interval [-0.02182,-0.01021]) and
$-0.01465$ under GFN2 ([-0.02041,-0.00929]). The corresponding force-RMS changes
are $-0.3795$ eV/\AA\ ([-0.4618,-0.3045]) and $-0.4618$
([-0.6195,-0.3370]). The paired physical update therefore also benefits the
Gaussian-source generator.

At a force-RMS threshold of 5 eV/\AA, GFN2 joint-yield improvements in runs 1
and 2 are 4.56 and 5.34 percentage points for Gaussian and 3.13 and 2.99 for
harmonic. After physical adaptation, harmonic minus Gaussian is 16.28 points
in run 1 and $-2.34$ points in run 2, despite a positive pooled difference.
Thus the results show both separate source/update benefits and variability
between runs. They do not establish a positive statistical interaction between
the source and physical update.
