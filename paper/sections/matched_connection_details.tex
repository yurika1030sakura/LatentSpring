\section{Physical correction on a shared backbone}\label{app:matched-connection}
This initial physical-head-only study precedes geometric recovery.
We test whether the learned physical correction transfers to the independent
EGNN, and give GAGA the same correction architecture and physical-supervision
budget. Both parents have 2,381,566 parameters and use the same 20,000 OMol25
training rows. The FM parent uses distance self-conditioning. Its 15,000
two-pass updates match the 960,000 training-example forward passes of GAGA's
30,000 one-pass updates, as described in Appendix~\ref{app:feedback-transfer}.
Two independent parent initializations each receive a separately fitted head.

\paragraph{A common predicted endpoint change.}
FM predicts a velocity, whereas GAGA predicts noise. We express the head's
action through the change it induces in the parent's provisional final
geometry. Write $p$ for the fraction of the sampling schedule already completed.
For FM, $p$ is the flow time; for GAGA, $p=1-t/t_{\max}$, where $t$ is diffusion
noise time and $t_{\max}=0.65$ is the selected truncation. These are normalized
schedule positions, not a shared physical time.

The head $A_\phi$ takes the current coordinates $X$, provisional final geometry
$H$, atom identities, and progress $p$. It predicts the same bounded vector
correction as in Appendix~\ref{app:force-correction}. A strength $\eta$ scales
its implied endpoint displacement to $\eta(1-p)A_\phi$. The corresponding
updates to the two parent outputs are
\begin{align}
 H=X+(1-p)v,
 &\qquad v^{\rm corr}=v+\eta A_\phi,
 \label{eq:matched-fm-correction}\\
 H=\frac{X-\sigma_t\epsilon}{\alpha_t},
 &\qquad \epsilon^{\rm corr}=\epsilon
       -\eta\frac{\alpha_t(1-p)}{\sigma_t}A_\phi.
 \label{eq:matched-gaga-correction}
\end{align}
Here $v$ is the FM velocity, $\epsilon$ is GAGA's predicted noise, and
$\alpha_t,\sigma_t$ are the signal and noise scales of its fixed schedule.
Substitution shows that both equations change the provisional endpoint by
$\eta(1-p)A_\phi$. The native GAGA transitions and final observation noise are
preserved. Every parent tensor, including its noise schedule, remains frozen.
With the common 16-element embedding vocabulary, each head has 7,106
parameters; its hidden pair network is the same as the small FlowMol correction.

\paragraph{Physical supervision and selection.}
Each parent generates two trajectories for each of the same 128 TRAIN
compositions, balanced across the four atom-count ranges in
Appendix~\ref{app:force-correction}. Two late field evaluations per trajectory
give 512 provisional endpoints. FM records progress 0.734375 and 0.921875;
GAGA records the nearest reverse-sampling evaluations, at progress
$1-174/650$ and $1-51/650$. All states are retained. Mirror-paired eSEN queries
provide centered forces, and the same bounded displacement rule gives the
regression target $\delta(H)/(1-p)$ for $A_\phi$. No target requires capping by
the per-atom velocity bound. Each head uses 20,000 AdamW updates on 96 TRAIN
compositions; the other 32 measure target-prediction transfer. The four fits
reduce this internal prediction error by 29.7--32.1\% relative to zero correction.

Eight separate validation compositions select one strength per algorithm
across both fits from $\{0,1/4,1/2,1\}$. Selection maximizes graph-valid yield
with GFN2 force RMS at most 5 eV/\AA, including every attempt in the denominator;
ties choose the smaller strength. Both algorithms select $\eta=1$.
Only after this choice is audited and frozen do we generate the new test panel:
16 compositions, eight each with 17--28 and 29--40 atoms, with 16 outputs per
composition and fit. None occurs in the prepared training or validation corpus,
or in earlier generator evaluation panels.

\paragraph{Cost and verification.}
The study uses 1,024 TRAIN trajectories, 4,096 eSEN teacher queries, and 80,000
head updates in total. Validation and test each contribute 2,048 raw outputs
and the same number of fixed-coordinate GFN2 calculations. Each output uses
128 backbone calls; FM uses 64 additional head calls and GAGA uses 128.
The zero-strength controls execute the same head operations as their corrected
counterparts. Supervision and head-update budgets match; total FLOPs and wall
time are not equated. Generation uses no physical queries, geometry optimization,
or energy-based selection. Zero heads and passive trajectory observers reproduce
the native samplers exactly. Auditing reconstructs the targets, checks fitted
head errors, re-evaluates all raw graphs, and independently parses xTB gradients.
