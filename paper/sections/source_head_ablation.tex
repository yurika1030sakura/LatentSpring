\subsection{Ablation of the source and endpoint corrections}\label{sec:source-head-ablation}
We compare Gaussian and harmonic sources with the geometric--physical correction block switched off or on. Both one-pass EGNN models receive the same frozen correction weights within each fit, trained with the self-conditioned FM. This holds correction fitting fixed while testing source choice.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../research/figures/final_complete_v1/figures/components.pdf}
\caption{Source and correction ablation. (A) Mean graph-and-force yield, with both fits shown as faint lines. (B) Harmonic-minus-Gaussian gains with nominal crossed fit/composition 95\% intervals. Each setting contains 2,048 outputs on 64 compositions.}
\label{fig:source-head-ablation}\end{figure}

The corrections increase joint yield from 6.25 to 25.20\% for Gaussian FM and from 7.23 to 29.79\% for harmonic FM. With correction, the harmonic source adds 4.59 points (crossed 95\% interval $[2.00,7.13]$); without it, the gain is 0.98 points ($[-0.68,2.64]$).

Geometry-and-force yield increases from 1.51 to 6.40\% for Gaussian FM and from 2.10 to 8.11\% for harmonic FM. The corrected harmonic--Gaussian difference is 1.71 points, with interval $[-1.46,4.69]$. Thus the source advantage is resolved for graph-and-force yield but remains uncertain under the stricter geometry criterion. Appendix~\ref{app:main-implementation} reports both fits, and Appendix~\ref{app:geometric-recovery} separates geometric and physical feedback.
