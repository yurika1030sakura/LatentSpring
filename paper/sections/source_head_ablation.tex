\subsection{Ablation of the source and physical correction}\label{sec:source-head-ablation}
We separate the two design choices in a four-setting comparison: a Gaussian
or harmonic source, each with or without physical correction. These experiments
use EGNN flow models with one network pass per velocity prediction. Within each training
run, the same frozen correction head is applied to both source variants.
This keeps the learned correction fixed while changing the source.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{../research/figures/round2_results_v4/components.pdf}
\caption{Contributions of the source and physical correction. (A) Mean joint yield
for the four configurations; faint lines retain both training fits. (B)
Harmonic-minus-Gaussian joint-yield gains, with nominal 95\% intervals from
resampling fits and compositions. Each configuration uses 2,048 outputs on the
same 64 compositions and one network evaluation per velocity prediction.}
\label{fig:source-head-ablation}\end{figure}

Physical correction improves joint yield with either source: from
6.25 to 19.92\% for Gaussian FM and from 7.23 to 24.27\% for harmonic FM.
The harmonic source alone gives smaller changes. Its contribution is clearer
when combined with physical correction: graph validity rises from
20.65 to 25.59\%, and joint yield rises from 19.92 to 24.27\%.
The 4.35-point joint-yield gain has a 95\% interval of $[1.56,7.13]$
when resampling both training runs and compositions. Both runs favor the
combination over either component alone. Appendix~\ref{app:main-implementation}
reports the individual runs and the four-setting analysis.
