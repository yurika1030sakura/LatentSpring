\section{Paired mobility diagnostic after the screening experiments}
\label{app:mobility-diagnostic}

To distinguish restricted proposal geometry from generic relaxation of imperfect
initial states, we optimize both the source and exchanged destination of each
recorded pair. Hash selection chooses one supported pair from each of 32 distinct
fitting parents, eight in each of four compositions, without energy or acceptance
selection. Thirty pairs change canonical connectivity; all 32 are retained.
Each endpoint retains its own original perceived bond-order graph, charge and
spin. The declared restrained, inversion-averaged potential is unchanged.

Each source and destination has two optimization arms: moving the two exchanged
terminal atoms in the COM subspace while preserving passive relative coordinates,
or moving all COM degrees of freedom. Both use orthonormal charts, L-BFGS with
Armijo backtracking, a 0.05\,\AA\ per-atom step cap and at most 32 candidate energy
evaluations per arm. The projected force stopping threshold is
0.05\,eV/\AA. Geometry and graph rejections, energy rejections and early stops are
retained. Initial energies and forces are freshly evaluated, shared across the
two mobility arms and repeated to check numerical consistency.

For mobility choice $m$, define the bounded observed gap
$\Delta_m=U(y_m)-U(x_m)$, using each arm's best feasible queried point, including
its initial point. The contrast $\Delta_{\mathrm{collective}}-
\Delta_{\mathrm{roots}}$ removes the source's generic relaxation improvement.
It does not compare converged minima unless all contributing arms converge.

Intervals resample the eight parents within each of the four fixed compositions
5,000 times; they are descriptive internal evidence, not generalization intervals
over chemical space. Collective motion lowers the destination an additional
1.1028\,eV on average, but also lowers the source an additional 1.0256\,eV.
Ignoring the source would substantially overstate the evidence for a limitation
specific to the exchange. The paired contrast does not establish such a benefit.

Convergence is incomplete: 59/64 root-only arms converge, four exhaust their
query cap and one stops at the minimum step. Only 5/64 collective arms converge;
49 exhaust the cap and ten stop at the minimum step. All four arms converge for
only 2/32 pairs. Equal query caps therefore do not imply equal actual costs or
comparable accuracy of the apparent minima. Optimization uses 1,638 raw calls
for root-only and 3,670 for collective arms, plus 256 shared initialization and
repeatability calls: 5,564 total, below the frozen 8,448 cap. Both inversion
orientations count as raw calls. Four audits replay every arm and independently
reconstruct all 2,654 queried optimization trials, physical forces, constraints
and Armijo decisions, without additional physical queries.

This bounded diagnostic neither establishes intrinsic chemical minimum-energy
gaps nor rules out useful collective geometry. It motivates resolving optimization
convergence and constraint stops before choosing another learned transport.
Optimization alone is not a valid transition proposal: a subsequent sampler
would require its actual proposal density and Jacobian or a fully accounted
reversible auxiliary path. No learned sampling or equilibrium advantage is
claimed from these endpoints.

\paragraph{Frozen continuation.}
We subsequently extend all 53 budget-exhausted arms to at most 128 cumulative
candidate evaluations each. The other 75 arms remain unchanged. Cached physical
queries are replayed, retaining optimizer histories and partial searches. The
continuation uses 3,768 additional raw calls, for 9,332 cumulative calls; all
4,538 cumulative queried optimization trials pass full replay and independent
checks. Root-only convergence rises to 63/64 and collective convergence to
46/64; all four arms converge for 20/32 pairs. Five collective arms remain
budget-exhausted and 14 arms stop at the minimum step. The all-pair root-only
and collective gaps are now 0.59453 and 0.50666\,eV. Their paired difference is
$-0.08786$\,eV with descriptive interval $[-0.23315,0.07213]$. More optimization
therefore improves convergence without establishing an exchange-specific
collective advantage. Original shorter-budget results and costs are retained.

\paragraph{Boundary stops are not convergence.}
Saved rejected probes identify nine disconnection boundaries, four frozen-graph
changes and one chemical-perception failure. We compare two deterministic
directions at all 14 stops: projected COM force, and the same force with outward
components removed at the observed active pair-distance boundaries. A small
inward component and geometry-only step halving retain the original graph. This
construction supplies candidate directions, not a new sampler or a stationarity
certificate. Both directions and their exact coordinates are frozen before
physical evaluation; no energy-based backtracking is used in this diagnostic.

The distance-adjusted direction has a feasible candidate at all 14 stops, versus
five for unadjusted force; nine infeasible rows remain in the denominator.
Fresh and repeated evaluation of the 14 source states costs 56 raw calls, and
the 19 feasible candidate evaluations cost 38, for 94 total. All four independent
audits replay the queries and reconstruct every physical state. Using the frozen
$10^{-5}$\,eV resolution threshold, 12/14 distance-adjusted candidates decrease
the potential and two increase it. Unadjusted force has zero resolved decreases,
one resolved increase and four changes within the tolerance. Thus 12 stops admit
nearby feasible points with lower potential, while first-order force prediction alone is
insufficient for finite steps. The two increases require preservation and actual
energy backtracking in any subsequent optimizer. These classical diagnostic
repairs do not establish useful AI novelty, converged chemical minima or a
sampling advantage.
