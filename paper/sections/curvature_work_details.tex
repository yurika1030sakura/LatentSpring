\section{Curvature-aware local work sampling}\label{app:curvature-work}
Force translation changes the proposal mean but leaves its shape fixed. To account for stiff and soft molecular directions, we fit local curvature and adjust the proposal covariance. The target distribution and maximum candidate-query budget remain unchanged.

\paragraph{Curvature fit.}
Let $Q_N\in\mathbb R^{3N\times d}$ have orthonormal columns spanning centered coordinate space, with $d=3(N-1)$. A displacement $u\in\mathbb R^d$ in angstroms corresponds to $Q_Nu$ in atomic coordinates, and $f_A$ is the anchor force in this basis. Eight independent pilot displacements form the columns of $D$; their negative force changes form $G$. We fit a symmetric $B_A$ to $G\approx B_A D$:
\begin{equation}
 \|BD-G\|_F^2+\lambda\|B-\kappa I\|_F^2,
 \qquad
 \kappa=\max\!\left(0,\frac{\langle D,G\rangle_F}{\|D\|_F^2}\right),
 \qquad \lambda=0.01\,\|D\|_F^2/K.
\end{equation}
Here $K$ counts admissible pilot columns. With none, or with exactly zero displacements, we set $B_A=0$ and recover force translation. Otherwise, $\|D\|_F^2$ is floored at $10^{-20}$ in scalar and ridge calculations. The nonnegative stiffness $\kappa$ provides an isotropic shrinkage target. We clip negative fitted eigenvalues to zero; an isotropic control uses $B_A=\kappa I$. The map is fixed before drawing production candidates.

\paragraph{Proposal transformation.}
The fitted energy is $E_+(A+Q_Nu)-E_+(A)\approx-f_A^\top u+u^\top B_Au/2$. Reweighting the original Gaussian by this quadratic Boltzmann factor changes both its mean and covariance. With dimensionless precision $P_A=I+\sigma_A^2B_A/(k_B T_{\rm phys})$, define
\begin{equation}
 v=P_A^{-1/2}u+b_A,\qquad
 b_A=P_A^{-1}\frac{\sigma_A^2f_A}{k_B T_{\rm phys}},\qquad
 \log J_A=-\tfrac12\log\det P_A.
\end{equation}
The factor $P_A^{-1/2}$ contracts stiff directions, $b_A$ adjusts the mean shift, and $J_A$ is the intrinsic volume factor. Reference width and temperature stay fixed. For $X=A+Q_Nu$ and $Y=A+Q_Nv$, the work is
\begin{equation}
 W_A=E_+(Y)-E_+(A)+k_B T_{\rm phys}
 [\log q_A(X)-\log q_A(Y)-\log J_A].
\end{equation}
Candidates outside the original same-graph support receive zero weight. This applies targeted and escorted reweighting to the local proposal \citep{jarzynski2002targeted,escorted2008}.

\paragraph{Weight variation.}
Writing the true energy as the fitted quadratic plus remainder $R_A(v)$ gives $W_A=-k_B T_{\rm phys}\log Z_{\rm quad}+R_A(v)$, where $Z_{\rm quad}$ integrates the reference Gaussian times the quadratic Boltzmann factor. Only the remainder varies across allowed candidates. An exact quadratic therefore gives equal weights. Tests verify this identity for anisotropic quadratic energy and check rotation equivariance of the fitted map.

\paragraph{Equal-query comparison.}
In each run, we select the first four eligible anchors from each of eight training compositions, without energy or ESS ranking. Translation uses 24 fresh production particles; curvature methods use at most eight pilot queries and 16 fresh production particles. Common anchor queries and reflection evaluations are counted separately. All production energies are evaluated, including graph-invalid candidates, and pilot samples are not reused in production.

\paragraph{Effect on generation.}
We train students using uniform sampling of 24 translated candidates, complete-work weighting of those same 24, or complete-work weighting of 16 curvature-adjusted candidates. All use the original 1,000-update schedule, reference/anchor selection, replay model, and coefficient-one parameter subtraction. All 349 anchors remain eligible. Work-weighted methods have equal maximum query budgets; uniform targets can omit candidate-energy queries. These larger pools form a follow-up, while the primary model retains its preselected eight-candidate force teacher.
