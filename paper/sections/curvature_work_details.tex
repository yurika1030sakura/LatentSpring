\section{Curvature-aware local work sampling}\label{app:curvature-work}
The local force escort shifts every candidate in the same direction. Molecular
energies also change more sharply along some displacement directions than
others, such as bond stretching versus softer collective motion. We test whether
estimating this local curvature improves the finite-particle work teacher while
keeping its target distribution fixed.

\paragraph{Fitting the local energy shape.}
For this derivation, we flatten an anchor into a vector of $3N$ coordinates.
The columns of $Q_N\in\mathbb R^{3N\times d}$ form an orthonormal basis of the
centered coordinate space, where $d=3(N-1)$. A vector $u\in\mathbb R^d$
specifies an independent displacement in angstroms, and $Q_Nu$ expresses it in
the original atom coordinates. The vector $f_A$
is the anchor force in the same basis. From eight independent pilot candidates,
we collect displacement columns in $D$ and corresponding negative force-change
columns in $G$. A matrix $B_A$ describes how force changes with displacement:
$G\approx B_A D$. We fit a symmetric matrix by minimizing
\begin{equation}
 \|BD-G\|_F^2+\lambda\|B-\kappa I\|_F^2,
 \qquad
 \kappa=\max\!\left(0,\frac{\langle D,G\rangle_F}{\|D\|_F^2}\right),
 \qquad \lambda=0.01\,\|D\|_F^2/K.
\end{equation}
Here $K$ is the number of graph-supported pilot columns, $\kappa$ is their
average positive stiffness, and the second term regularizes directions poorly
resolved by the pilot. Projecting the fitted matrix onto the positive
semidefinite cone replaces its negative eigenvalues by zero and defines $B_A$.
The isotropic control instead uses $B_A=\kappa I$, giving every direction the
same fitted stiffness. The pilot is held fixed before drawing production
candidates.

\paragraph{An affine escort and its complete work.}
The local quadratic approximation is
$E_+(A+Q_Nu)-E_+(A)\approx-f_A^\top u+u^\top B_Au/2$.
Multiplying its Boltzmann factor by the original Gaussian reference gives a
Gaussian with adjusted mean and covariance. Define the dimensionless precision
matrix $P_A=I+\sigma_A^2B_A/(k_B T_{\rm phys})$. Its eigenvalues are positive.
For a source displacement $u$, the new displacement is
\begin{equation}
 v=P_A^{-1/2}u+b_A,\qquad
 b_A=P_A^{-1}\frac{\sigma_A^2f_A}{k_B T_{\rm phys}},\qquad
 \log J_A=-\tfrac12\log\det P_A.
\end{equation}
The inverse square root reduces perturbations more strongly in stiff directions;
the mean shift also accounts for this stiffness. The Jacobian $J_A$ measures
the map's volume change in the $d$ independent coordinates. We use the original
reference width and temperature, so the restrained target is unchanged.
With $X=A+Q_Nu$ and $Y=A+Q_Nv$, the complete work is
\begin{equation}
 W_A=E_+(Y)-E_+(A)+k_B T_{\rm phys}
 [\log q_A(X)-\log q_A(Y)-\log J_A].
\end{equation}
Candidates outside the original same-graph support receive zero weight.
This is a local application of targeted and escorted work reweighting
\citep{jarzynski2002targeted,escorted2008}.

To see why the map helps, write the actual energy as the fitted quadratic plus
a remainder $R_A(v)$. Substitution into the work formula gives
$W_A=-k_B T_{\rm phys}\log Z_{\rm quad}+R_A(v)$, where $Z_{\rm quad}$ is the
normalizing integral of the Gaussian reference times the quadratic Boltzmann
factor. The fitted quadratic term cancels from the work; only the remaining
energy mismatch varies across supported particles. If the energy equals the
fitted quadratic, all supported particles have equal weights. Tests verify the
constant-work identity for an anisotropic quadratic energy and verify rotation
equivariance of the fitted escort.

\paragraph{Equal-budget molecular pilot.}
We select the first four eligible anchors from each of eight existing training
compositions in each continuation, without energy or ESS ranking. The
translation control uses 24 fresh production candidates per anchor. Each fitted
escort uses at most eight pilot energy/force queries and 16 fresh production
candidates. Thus its maximum particle-query budget equals that of the
translation control; common anchor queries and reflection evaluations are
accounted for separately. All production energies are evaluated, including
graph-invalid candidates. Pilot samples are not reused as production samples
for the fitted maps.

The directional fit improves ESS over translation on 61 of 64 anchors.
Its mean gain is 4.66 effective samples, with a composition-bootstrap 95\%
interval of $[3.81,5.46]$. Same-graph retention is 99.51\%, versus 99.54\%
for translation. Independent multivariate-Gaussian density calculations reproduce
the stored complete work weights. A subsequent full teacher construction uses
all 349 original eligible anchors with fresh production draws; mean ESS is
2.32 versus 6.71 in the first continuation and 2.33 versus 6.37 in the second.
These are local teacher diagnostics; the separate student comparison tests their
effect on neural generation.

\paragraph{Transfer into the molecular flow.}
We train three physical students on these full teacher pools: uniform weighting
of 24 force-translated candidates, complete-work weighting of the same 24
candidates, and complete-work weighting of 16 curvature-adjusted candidates.
Every student follows the same 1,000-update schedule and reference/anchor choices
as the original physical continuation. We reuse its matched replay model and
transfer the physical-minus-replay update with coefficient one. All 349 original
anchors remain eligible. The two work-weighted methods have the same maximum
particle-query budget; uniform force targets can omit particle-energy queries.
The main comparison uses the original preselected eight-particle force model;
this follow-up separately tests the larger candidate pools and curvature fit.
