\section{Molecular rotor work protocol}\label{app:rotor-work}
The three FIT molecules are \texttt{C=C(N)N(C)C(N)=O},
\texttt{CCOC(C)(C)N}, and \texttt{CC(=O)NCC=O}. Selection examines the first five
rows of an existing training subset, accepts the first three distinct compositions
with a methyl rotor preserving the labeled perceived graph on a 64-angle grid,
and queries no energy during selection. Only methyl hydrogens rotate around the
carbon--anchor axis; the remaining internal coordinates stay fixed. Overall
translation is removed. Density is defined with respect to the periodic angle
measure $d\phi$ on $[-\pi,\pi)$, not full Cartesian molecular volume.

For $q_0(\phi)=\exp(\cos\phi)/(2\pi I_0(1))$, define reference Hamiltonian
$H_0=-kT\log q_0$ and target Hamiltonian $H_1=H_0+U-U(0)$. The escort has
$|a|<1$, so it is a periodic diffeomorphism. Its complete work is
\begin{equation}
 W_a(\phi)=U(\psi_a(\phi))-U(0)+kT\left[
 \log q_0(\phi)-\log q_0(\psi_a(\phi))-\log J_a(\phi)\right].
\end{equation}
Change of variables gives
\begin{equation}
 \EE_{q_0}e^{-W_a/kT}
 =\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}q_0(y)e^{-[U(y)-U(0)]/kT}\,dy=Z_1/Z_0,
 \qquad Z_0=1.
\end{equation}
The generalized work includes the volume correction for the imposed escort;
the map is not asserted to be a trajectory of unforced molecular dynamics.

All 1,536 single-point calculations succeed and their logs, gradients, atom
identities and coordinates are independently replayed. The reference potential
is explicitly the periodic cubic interpolant of 512 GFN2 values per rotor.
Its 256-knot interpolation error at the additional midpoints is at most
$10^{-8}$ eV in this study. Increasing the reference quadrature from
32,768 to 65,536 points changes the free energy by less than
$10^{-10}$ eV. These numbers describe numerical consistency of this
interpolated approximate potential, not electronic-structure accuracy.

Every molecule, escort, sample budget and repetition is retained. Total variation
uses 64 equal angular bins; reference bin probabilities come from dense
quadrature. Normalized finite-sample importance weights are biased estimates of
the normalized target, while their unnormalized mean estimates its normalizer.
The reported free energy is $-kT\log\widehat Z$, with its finite-sample bias included
in RMSE. At 2,048 draws, complete-work ESS ranges from 788 to 1,892 across the six
nonidentity cases. Some incomplete weights have higher ESS despite their biased
target, demonstrating that ESS alone does not validate work accounting.

These controlled one-dimensional results validate the correction and its finite
sample behavior on the specified targets. They do not establish unrestricted
three-dimensional Boltzmann generation or an additional neural-training benefit
of work weighting over the matched force-update control.
