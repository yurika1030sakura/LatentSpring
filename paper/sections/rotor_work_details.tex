\section{Molecular rotor work protocol}\label{app:rotor-work}
The three training molecules are \texttt{C=C(N)N(C)C(N)=O},
\texttt{CCOC(C)(C)N}, and \texttt{CC(=O)NCC=O}. Selection examines the first five
rows of an existing training subset, accepts the first three distinct compositions
with a methyl rotor preserving the labeled perceived graph on a 64-angle grid,
and queries no energy during selection. Only methyl hydrogens rotate around the
carbon--anchor axis; the remaining internal coordinates stay fixed. Overall
translation is removed. The reference and target densities are defined with
respect to the periodic angle measure $d\phi$ on $[-\pi,\pi)$.

The starting angular density is $q_0(\phi)=\exp(\cos\phi)/(2\pi I_0(1))$,
where $2\pi I_0(1)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\exp(\cos u)\,du$ is its normalizing
integral. Define the reference Hamiltonian (energy function)
$H_0=-kT\log q_0$ and target Hamiltonian $H_1=H_0+U-U(0)$. The imposed map is $\psi_a(\phi)=\phi+a\sin\phi$, with
$a\in\{-0.5,0,0.5\}$ controlling its strength. Its local volume factor is
$J_a(\phi)=1+a\cos\phi$. The condition $|a|<1$ holds, so it is a smooth, invertible transformation of the periodic angle. Its complete work is
\begin{equation}
 W_a(\phi)=U(\psi_a(\phi))-U(0)+kT\left[
 \log q_0(\phi)-\log q_0(\psi_a(\phi))-\log J_a(\phi)\right].
\end{equation}
Change of variables gives
\begin{equation}
 \EE_{q_0}e^{-W_a/kT}
 =\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}q_0(y)e^{-[U(y)-U(0)]/kT}\,dy=Z_1/Z_0,
 \qquad Z_0=1.
\end{equation}
The logarithmic Jacobian accounts for the compression or expansion caused by
the imposed escort.

All 1,536 single-point calculations succeed. The reference potential is a
periodic cubic interpolant of 512 GFN2 values per rotor.
Its 256-knot interpolation error at the additional midpoints is at most
$10^{-8}$ eV in this study. Increasing the reference quadrature from
32,768 to 65,536 points changes the free energy by less than
$10^{-10}$ eV. These checks quantify interpolation and quadrature error for the GFN2-based
reference potential.

Total variation uses 64 equal angular bins, with reference probabilities obtained
by dense quadrature. Self-normalized estimates of target observables have
finite-sample bias; the mean unnormalized weight is an unbiased normalizer
estimator.
The reported free energy is $-kT\log\widehat Z$, with its finite-sample bias included
in RMSE. At 2,048 draws, complete-work ESS ranges from 788 to 1,892 across the six
nonidentity cases. Some incomplete weights have higher ESS despite their biased
target. Correct target recovery therefore depends on the work definition as well
as the concentration of the weights.
