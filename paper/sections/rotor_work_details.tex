\section{Molecular rotor work protocol}\label{app:rotor-work}
We use a controlled one-dimensional motion to test the work correction on
molecular energy curves. The three training molecules are
\texttt{C=C(N)N(C)C(N)=O}, \texttt{CCOC(C)(C)N}, and \texttt{CC(=O)NCC=O}.
We inspect the first five rows of an existing training subset and take the first
three distinct compositions with a methyl rotor that preserves the inferred
labeled graph on a 64-angle grid. Selection uses no energy queries. Only the
methyl hydrogens rotate around the carbon--anchor axis; other internal
coordinates remain fixed. We remove overall translation and define densities
with respect to the periodic angle measure $d\phi$ on $[-\pi,\pi)$.

\paragraph{Reference, target, and coordinate map.}
The starting density is $q_0(\phi)=\exp(\cos\phi)/(2\pi I_0(1))$, with
normalizing integral $2\pi I_0(1)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\exp(\cos u)\,du$.
This is a circular reference distribution, distinct from the coordinate source
used by the neural generator. Its reference Hamiltonian is $H_0=-kT\log q_0$,
and the target Hamiltonian is $H_1=H_0+U-U(0)$. We transform angles with
$\psi_a(\phi)=\phi+a\sin\phi$ for $a\in\{-0.5,0,0.5\}$. The derivative,
$J_a(\phi)=1+a\cos\phi$, is the local volume-change factor. Since $|a|<1$,
this map is smooth and invertible on the circle. Its complete work is
\begin{equation}
 W_a(\phi)=U(\psi_a(\phi))-U(0)+kT\left[
 \log q_0(\phi)-\log q_0(\psi_a(\phi))-\log J_a(\phi)\right].
\end{equation}
The density terms correct the changed angular sampling distribution, and the
Jacobian term corrects expansion or compression of angular intervals. Changing
variables gives
\begin{equation}
 \EE_{q_0}e^{-W_a/kT}
 =\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}q_0(y)e^{-[U(y)-U(0)]/kT}\,dy=Z_1/Z_0,
 \qquad Z_0=1.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Numerical reference.}
All 1,536 single-point calculations succeed. For each rotor, we construct a
periodic cubic interpolation of 512 GFN2 energy values. The 256-knot interpolation
has error at most $10^{-8}$ eV at the additional midpoint samples in this study.
Increasing the reference quadrature from 32,768 to 65,536 points changes free
energy by less than $10^{-10}$ eV. These checks assess interpolation and numerical
integration errors for the GFN2-based reference curve.

\paragraph{Estimators and finite-sample effects.}
We compute total variation on 64 equal angular bins, using dense quadrature for
the target probabilities. Self-normalized estimates of observables have
finite-sample bias, whereas the average unnormalized weight is an unbiased
estimator of the normalizing constant. The free-energy estimate is
$-kT\log\widehat Z$; its RMSE includes the bias introduced at finite sample
size. At 2,048 draws, complete-work ESS ranges from 788 to 1,892 across the six
nonidentity cases. Some incomplete weights have higher ESS despite targeting the
wrong distribution. ESS alone therefore does not establish correct target recovery.
