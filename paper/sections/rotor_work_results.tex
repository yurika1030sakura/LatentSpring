\subsection{Complete-work correction on molecular rotors}\label{sec:rotor-work}
For this test, one angle $\phi$ specifies a family of three-dimensional structures:
the three hydrogens of a methyl group rotate around its bond axis while the other
internal coordinates remain fixed. We select three such rotors from training
molecules in fixed data order using geometry alone. A periodic interpolation of
512 GFN2 energies defines $U(\phi)$ for each rotor, with $U(0)$ as the reference
energy. The target is
$\rho(\phi)\propto q_0(\phi)\exp[-(U(\phi)-U(0))/kT]$, where $q_0$ is a
normalized von Mises distribution with concentration one. It favors angles near
zero; the energy factor reweights those angles while retaining this restraint.
Here $kT=k_B T_{\rm phys}$ at 300 K.

We apply three fixed angle maps, including the identity map, to the starting
samples. For each map, we compare complete-work weighting, energy-only weighting,
weighting without the Jacobian correction, and no weighting. Complete work
accounts for both the reference-density change and the expansion or compression
of angle intervals. Each setting uses 128 repetitions at sample counts from
8 to 2,048. The maps and work formula are specified above in
Appendix~\ref{app:rotor-work}.

Total variation measures the discrepancy between estimated and target angular
probabilities; zero indicates agreement. Free-energy RMSE measures error in the
logarithm of the target normalizer, expressed in energy units.

At 2,048 samples, complete work has lower mean total-variation error than both
incomplete-weight controls in all six nonidentity cases. The average error is
0.0636 for complete work, 0.1532 for energy-only weights, and 0.1134 without the
Jacobian. Complete work therefore reduces this error by 58.5\% relative to
energy-only weighting. Its free-energy RMSE ranges from 0.158 to 0.734 meV.
Independent density-ratio quadrature also recovers the target normalizer in all
nine molecule/map cases. With the identity map, the three weighted methods
coincide. These results test the full work correction on restrained molecular
energy curves, rather than equilibrium sampling by the neural generator.
