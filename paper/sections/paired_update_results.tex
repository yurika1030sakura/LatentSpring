\subsection{Why subtract the replay update}\label{sec:update-ablation}
Does replay subtraction help because it changes the update direction, or simply
because it reduces the update size? We compare the paired update with direct
physical fine-tuning and a direct update rescaled to the same parameter norm.
The rescaled control is
$\theta_{\rm control}=\theta_{\rm base}+\alpha(\theta_{\rm physical}-\theta_{\rm base})$,
where $\alpha$ is the ratio of paired to direct update norms. Each norm is the
square root of the summed squared changes in model parameters; buffers are excluded.
The coefficients are 0.2536 and 0.2564 in the two
runs, determined from the trained weights before generating evaluation samples.

\centering
\caption{Update controls on 10 compositions, with 1,280 generation attempts per

paired outputs that are graph-valid under both models.}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Model & Graph validity (\%) & Energy change (eV/atom)\\
\midrule
Base &62.11&0\\
Direct physical update &54.77&$+0.11080$\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\label{tab:update-ablation}
\end{table}

The rescaled direct update largely preserves graph validity but increases energy
relative to the base model. The paired update lowers energy instead.
Paired minus same-norm direct gives $-0.03232$ eV/atom in energy
([-0.03981,-0.02485]) and $-0.4170$ eV/\AA\ in force RMS
([-0.5529,-0.2859]). Both runs favor the paired direction, and comparisons using
all outputs have the same signs. The graph-validity difference is $+0.39$
percentage points, with interval [-1.95,2.73]. These results support a benefit
from the replay-subtracted direction, rather than update size alone.
