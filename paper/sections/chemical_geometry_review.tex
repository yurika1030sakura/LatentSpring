\section{Chemical geometry and illustrative structures}\label{app:chemical-geometry}
We supplement graph inference and the GFN2 force criterion with geometric checks
from PoseBusters 0.4.4 \citep{buttenschoen2024posebusters}. The checks use distance
bounds for heavy-atom bond lengths and angles, internal steric distances, planar
aromatic rings and double bonds, and nonplanarity of saturated six-membered
rings. We use the package's molecule-configuration tolerances: 25\% for bond and
angle bounds, 30\% for internal distances, 0.25\,\AA\ for planar groups, and
0.05\,\AA\ for nonplanar rings. A structure must also have zero assigned radical
electrons. The check is a geometric subset of PoseBusters; it omits conformer
ensemble energy ratios and reference-identity comparisons.

Every generated coordinate remains fixed. We evaluate all 5,120 attempts per
setting, using the same inferred graph as the original benchmark. This includes
outputs with failed graph inference in the denominator. Table~\ref{tab:chemical-geometry}
reports the geometric subset alone and its conjunction with the original
GFN2 force threshold of 5 eV/\AA.

For the initial FM, physical correction reduces contact-disconnected outputs from 3,092 to 2,702,
heavy-atom-disconnected outputs from 2,527 to 2,325, and severe overlaps from
126 to nine. Among the 1,273 graph-valid corrected outputs, 674 fail a planarity
check, 340 fail a bond-angle check, 237 fail a bond-length check, and 296 fail
the internal-distance check. These categories overlap. On all 512 prepared
validation references, 470 pass the same closed-shell geometric screen.
The comparison identifies connectivity and detailed geometry as remaining
generation errors even after the force criterion improves.

\paragraph{Inferred charge patterns.}
Geometric and force checks do not characterize every aspect of chemical
plausibility. Among the 494 geometry-passing LatentSpring outputs on the main
panel, 466 contain assigned formal charges and 378 contain charged carbon
atoms. The corresponding counts among the 64 panel reference graphs are eight
and two. Legitimate functional groups can carry formal charges, but this large
difference identifies charge assignment and connectivity as remaining errors.
We therefore also examine common-element compositions and report charge filters
as descriptive diagnostics, separately from the declared geometry metric.

\paragraph{Common-element organic compositions.}
We select validation references with 17--28 atoms, elements in CHONF, zero
assigned formal charges, and passing geometric checks. A fixed hash order gives
12 distinct compositions, excluding the 24 development compositions and the
four earlier illustration compositions. Thirteen compositions met the rule;
we fixed the first 12 before generating outputs. They are disjoint from the
20,000 training structures and belong to the existing validation corpus.
The panel characterizes this selected application domain.

Five unchanged generators each produce 16 outputs per composition from each
of two fitted models: 384 attempts per method and 1,920 in total. The full
model gives 136 geometry-and-force successes, compared with 79 for initial
FM with physical correction, 68 for GAGA, 57 for EDM and 63 for Gaussian FM.
The paired full-versus-initial FM gain is 14.84 percentage points, with crossed
fit/composition interval $[7.81,25.52]$.

Requiring zero assigned formal charges as well gives 54 successes for the full
model and 33 for the initial FM, or 14.06\% versus 8.59\% of all attempts.
Requiring no charged carbon atoms gives 20.31\% versus 13.02\%. Charge filters
are not comprehensive validity rules: they may exclude reasonable ionic or
charge-separated functional groups.

\paragraph{The molecular illustration.}
Figure~\ref{fig:method} uses the first full-model output in fit/composition/draw
order passing geometry and force checks with zero assigned atom charges.
It is fit 1, composition 1, draw 1 (one-based), with formula
$\mathrm{C_7H_{15}N}$, inferred connectivity \texttt{CCC(C)CC=NC}, and GFN2 force
RMS 1.856 eV/\AA. This is a raw output from the application panel above.

We replay its source random numbers exactly and use the stored output coordinates.
The 3D source and output share camera and scale. The 2D inset depicts the inferred
connectivity, with hydrogens implicit in the usual chemical notation. Bond
orders are rendered explicitly, and the PyMOL round-trip check verifies atom
identities, bond orders and coordinate precision. No geometry optimization or
alteration of the generation outcomes is used in preparing the illustration.
