\begin{abstract}
Energy-aware molecular sampling requires both an explicit statistical target and
proposals that respect its geometric support. A flow-matching generator can supply
initial states while leaving its finite-step density unknown, and correct
Metropolis acceptance does not by itself make local moves efficient. We study
learned geometry proposals for reversible molecular graph exchanges. A masked
graph network learns equivariant directional distributions from physical
angular-force examples. Fixed-geometry angular updates can cancel unknown
support normalizers but leave graph/geometry barriers unresolved. Our joint update
instead regenerates two radii and directions after an attachment exchange, using
explicitly normalized autoregressive directional mixtures and the correct
Cartesian volume factors. Complete reverse densities permit Metropolis correction
without a source likelihood or equilibrium training coordinates. In a development
study, both frozen tensor models reach a diagnostic reference connectivity from
all four generated starts, while matched short physical controls retain failures.
Continuing the physical controls to the same total cost shows that a simple
coordination-site prior reaches the diagnostic graph earlier once learning
preparation is counted. We preserve these adverse controls, all rejected proposals
and a failed transfer screen. Equilibrium mixing, a total-cost learned advantage
and calibrated molecular equilibrium ensembles remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and scope}
Implicit molecular generators and equilibrium samplers solve different problems.
Our FlowMol3-based initializer~\citep{flowmol3}, trained on an OMol25
subset~\citep{omol25}, supplies coordinates, but its implemented finite-step
law need not have an available likelihood. Metropolis correction can instead use
the probability ratio of a subsequent conditional proposal. This avoids assigning
a fictitious source density, while leaving the practical problem of choosing
efficient molecular moves.

Our development evidence identifies separate failures. Cartesian local moves can
be rejected by a hard structural domain. Large unstructured rotations can satisfy
the domain yet have negligible physical acceptance. A learned selector can improve
the first connectivity transition without improving total-cost sampling. These
distinctions motivate a conditional angular guide and, after its graph-transition
failure, a joint graph/geometry update. Target correctness and empirical
efficiency are evaluated separately.

The present study fixes atom identities, total charge and spin multiplicity.
The target may contain different constitutional isomers; it is not an unconditional
distribution over chemical compositions or a fixed-stereoisomer ensemble. Perceived
bonds define a stated algorithmic support and proposal context. They are not supplied
OMol bond-supervision labels, and their successful assignment does not certify a
quantum electronic state. We retain a bond-free flow-matching initializer and make
no claim that this preliminary study solves broad molecular generation.

\section{Target and internal-coordinate update}
For $N$ labelled atoms, let $\HH=\{x\in\mathbb R^{N\times 3}:\sum_i x_i=0\}$,
with its Euclidean volume in dimension $3(N-1)$. We use
\begin{equation}
 \pi(x)\propto {\bf 1}_{D}(x)\exp[-U(x)/(kT)],\qquad
 U(x)=\tfrac 12[E_{\rm raw}(x)+E_{\rm raw}(-x)]+\tfrac\gamma 2\|x\|^2.
 \label{eq:angular-target}
\end{equation}
The raw potential is a fixed OMol25 eSEN energy model~\citep{esen}. Both inversion orientations are
evaluated; the conservative averaged force is
$[F_{\rm raw}(x)-F_{\rm raw}(-x)]/2$. A single raw evaluation cannot replace this
average inside an exponential weight or acceptance ratio. In the current pilot,
$kT=0.025851999786435$ eV and $\gamma=0.1$ eV/\AA$^2$.

The domain $D$ requires connected, nonoverlapping geometry and a successful
deterministic bond assignment and sanitization at the requested total charge.
This changes the target relative to unconstrained energy sampling. Our original
unconstrained experiments retain their original interpretation. The graph criterion
has material radical and metal limitations, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:angular-limits}.

Select a single-bonded terminal atom $i$ and its passive anchor $k$.
Write $x_i-x_k=r u$, with $u\in\sphere$. Hold $r$ and all passive relative
coordinates fixed, replace $u$, and recenter the coordinates. A linear chart on
$\HH$ consists of the passive relative coordinates and $x_i-x_k$; its determinant
is constant. The angular part of volume is $r^2\,dr\,d\Omega(u)$, so the radial
factor cancels between the forward and reverse update. The proposal density in
this section is conditional on this angular subspace, not a density on all of $\HH$.

\section{Masked equivariant angular guide}
\paragraph{Invariant context.}
The retained context $c$ contains the passive coordinates relative to $k$, radius
$r$, atom identities, electronic condition and the perceived graph $G$. Before
any geometric features or message passing, the moved atom's coordinate is replaced
by the anchor. Leaf and anchor roles are marked without assigning learned meaning
to absolute atom indices. An angular candidate is eligible only if its perceived
bond matrix equals $G$. Consequently the context is identical before and after
the update. Retaining the moved atom's old direction in the network would generally
invalidate the normalizer cancellation below.

\paragraph{Vector and tensor outputs.}
Two scalar message-passing blocks use atom embeddings, role embeddings, radial
features of the masked coordinates and the observed bond matrix. Invariant scalar
weights combine passive vectors and their traceless dyadic products to produce
a polar vector $\eta_\theta(c)$ and symmetric traceless matrix $A_\theta(c)$.
The angular score is
\begin{equation}
 s_\theta(u;c)=\eta_\theta(c)^\top u+u^\top A_\theta(c)u.
 \label{eq:angular-score}
\end{equation}
This is a linear--quadratic directional model. The tensor can represent symmetric
polar or planar structure without selecting an arbitrary oriented normal. A
vector-only ablation sets $A=0$. Joint atom permutations and orthogonal coordinate
transformations preserve the scalar score. We bound the combined coefficient norms
and initialize both outputs to zero.

\subsection{Capped support-conditioned proposals}
Let $D(c)\subset\sphere$ be the same-graph angular support. First consider uniform
directions accepted by an internal rejection step with probability
\begin{equation}
 a_c(u)={\bf 1}_{D(c)}(u)\exp[s_\theta(u;c)-M(c)],
 \qquad M(c)\ge\sup_{u\in\sphere}s_\theta(u;c).
\end{equation}
Stop at the first success, or retain the old state after $K$ failed trials. Let
$Z(c)$ be the one-trial success probability and
$b_K(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}(1-Z)^j$. The non-self generated density is
\begin{equation}
 Q_K(v\mid u,c)=\frac{b_K(Z(c))}{4\pi}
 {\bf 1}_{D(c)}(v)\exp[s_\theta(v;c)-M(c)].
\end{equation}
The unknown $Z$, bound $M$ and cap factor are identical in the reverse update.
They therefore cancel without estimation.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional capped update]
For a frozen guide with invariant context, an eligible angular proposal $y$ is
accepted using
\begin{equation}
 \log R=-\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{kT}
 +s_\theta(u_x;c)-s_\theta(u_y;c)
 +\log\frac{p(i,k\mid y)}{p(i,k\mid x)}.
 \label{eq:angular-mh}
\end{equation}
The resulting update satisfies detailed balance with the declared target.
Exhausted searches contribute self-transitions.
\end{proposition}
We begin with uniform terminal selection. This supplies an exact proposal
\emph{ratio}; it does not supply a normalized conditional likelihood, source
likelihood or partition-function estimate. Composing the update with separately
corrected local and chemical-exchange kernels preserves the target, even when
the deterministic scan as a whole is not reversible.

\subsection{A more efficient rejection envelope}
Uniform score rejection can be inefficient for concentrated guides. Let
$\lambda_3\ge\lambda_2$ be the two largest eigenvalues of $A$, let $v$ be a top
eigenvector, and set $d=\lambda_3-\lambda_2$. The base proposal is a mixture of
von Mises--Fisher distributions with natural parameters $\eta\pm d v$ and weights
proportional to the reciprocals of their normalizers. Its density is proportional
to $\exp(\eta^\top u)\cosh(d v^\top u)$. A valid rejection bound is
\begin{equation}
 B=\lambda_3-\log\cosh d,\qquad
 \log a(u)=u^\top A u-\log\cosh(d v^\top u)-B\le 0.
\end{equation}
After support rejection the generated density remains proportional to
$\exp s_\theta$. All additional constants depend only on $c$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular-mh} remains valid under the cap. Eigenvector sign changes
exchange mixture components; when $d=0$, the axis disappears. We treat this as
an exact implementation improvement, not an established new distributional theorem.

\section{Joint graph and geometry proposal}
The fixed-radius angular kernel cannot directly repair a coupling between
connectivity and bond strain. We therefore implement a separate, normalized
proposal that exchanges two unlike terminal leaves at distinct anchors while
regenerating both bond lengths and directions. It retains the same target in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular-target}. The desired graph $G'$ exchanges the two attachments;
the final perceived bond matrix must equal $G'$, otherwise the proposal is rejected
before querying the physical oracle.

For each new attachment, draw $\ell=\log r\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$, with
$\mu$ the log sum of the corresponding covalent radii. A deterministic template
uses these radii and directions transported from the source's partner leaves.
Choose one of the two root orders $o$ with equal probability and then generate
directions sequentially, conditioning the second decoder on the first output.
The first decoder sees the second leaf's template, not its eventual output.
In this experiment $\sigma=0.05$, frozen before evaluation.

Unlike the invariant-context angular update, this proposal changes its graph and
radial context. Its directional density must therefore be explicitly normalized.
We use the two-vMF mixture itself, without rejection to the quadratic score:
\begin{equation}
 \log h_\theta(u;c)=\eta^\top u+\log\cosh(dv^\top u)-\log H(c).
\end{equation}
Uniform directions and a fixed vMF coordination-site prior are physical controls.
The latter points away from the sum of the other bonded neighbors' unit vectors,
with concentration 10, or is uniform when that sum is zero. Vector and tensor
decoders reuse the frozen angular guides without new fitting.

Writing $c_m$ for the sequential template context, the coordinate density is,
up to a constant common COM chart factor,
\begin{equation}
 \log q_o(y\mid x,a)=\sum_{m=1}^2
 \left[\log\phi_\sigma(\ell_m-\mu_m)-3\ell_m
       +\log h_\theta(u_m;c_m)\right].
 \label{eq:joint-q}
\end{equation}
The factor $r^3$ combines spherical volume with $dr=r\,d\ell$. It is not the
Jacobian of the earlier deterministic site exchange. Reverse evaluation rebuilds
the reverse template from $y$, injects the observed radii and directions from $x$
in the same augmented order, and uses the inverse graph action $\bar a$.
The corrected log acceptance is
\begin{equation}
 \log R=-\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{kT}
 +\log\frac{q_o(x\mid y,\bar a)}{q_o(y\mid x,a)}
 +\log\frac{p(\bar a\mid y)}{p(a\mid x)}.
 \label{eq:joint-mh}
\end{equation}
The equal order probabilities cancel. The deterministic control uses the same
distinct-anchor action list, so restricting that list cannot be credited to
learned geometry. This is an evaluated development extension, not a new claim
that generic autoregressive densities or Metropolis correction are novel.

\subsection{Learning the actual normalized directional law}
The quadratic-score guide and its normalized envelope have different surface
scores. We therefore also implement a guide that directly models the normalized
directional distribution. Continuous atomic-number and covalent-radius features
replace element-specific embedding rows. Two masked message-passing blocks predict
an equivariant correction $\delta_\theta$ to the physical-site center and paired
offsets associated with passive atoms:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 h_\theta(u;c)&=\sum_j\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 w_{\theta,j,\epsilon}(c) C(\|\eta_{\theta,j,\epsilon}\|)
 \exp(\eta_{\theta,j,\epsilon}^{\top}u),\\
 \eta_{\theta,j,\epsilon}&=\eta_{\rm site}+\delta_\theta+
 \epsilon a_{\theta,j}\widetilde v_j.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\widetilde v_j$ are bounded masked passive vectors, $a_{\theta,j}>0$ are
bounded scales and paired component probabilities share invariant node logits.
These probabilities sum to one; they are not inverse-normalizer weights of an
unnormalized quadratic score. The moving leaf is excluded from component weights.
A vector ablation uses just the corrected center. The mixture has 4,531 parameters;
the ablation uses 4,497 active parameters in the same implementation.

If $\rho_k(u;c)$ denotes a component's posterior responsibility, the surface
score fitted to physical angular forces is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_{\sphere}\log h_\theta(u;c)
 =(I-uu^\top)\sum_k\rho_k(u;c)\eta_k(c).
\end{equation}
Thus fitting and evaluation use the same learned directional law. This remains
pointwise force regression on generated training states, not a claim that the
finite-dimensional model recovers a supported equilibrium conditional exactly.

For molecular use, we mix the \emph{whole} joint learned proposal with the physical
site proposal: $q_{\rm mix}=\tfrac12 q_{\rm site}+\tfrac12 q_\theta$. A coin chooses
the generating component, while MH uses the full logsumexp of both component
densities in each direction. This retains an explicit physical component under
transfer. For the same paired action and root order, its off-diagonal accepted
probability flow is at least half the physical proposal's flow. This standard
mixture bound does not establish a speedup or finite-time equilibrium accuracy.

\section{Training and experimental contract}
The physical angular score at a supported point is
\begin{equation}
 g=r(I-u u^\top)F_{\HH,i}/(kT),\qquad
 \nabla_{\sphere}s_\theta=(I-u u^\top)(\eta+2 Au),
\end{equation}
where $F_\HH$ includes the restraint and COM projection. We regress the latter
against a norm-capped version of $g$. This is supervised pointwise force fitting,
not an equilibrium score-matching theorem. For the normalized guide we instead
use its responsibility-weighted surface score above, including the explicit
vMF normalizers. The original quadratic-score fit requires no angular normalizer;
neither fit requires oracle Hessians. Our finite-time training states need not be
equilibrium samples for this regression objective; generalization and useful
sampling nevertheless require separate evidence.

The current table contains 2,782 supported scored states and 22,256 eligible
state/leaf examples from 34 generated training parents. Parents are sampled
uniformly, followed by an example within the parent, retaining parent/time
correlation in the provenance. Only generated training coordinates and their
audited physical forces are used. The four generated development starts are
separate. A reference connectivity is used as a diagnostic, not as a known
equilibrium point mass or a training label.

The original two-layer quadratic-score guide has 6,082 parameters. We fit its tensor and vector variants at
two seeds for 1,000 updates of 64 examples. The in-sample MSE of the norm-capped,
dimensionless angular-score labels is
439.7/475.0 for the tensor variant and 639.8/623.7 for the vector ablation, versus
730.0 for zero prediction. These numbers measure training fit only.

Every chain uses the schedule local/angle/exchange/local. Local MALA uses a fixed
mixture of three scales; chemical moves are uniform, invertible terminal exchanges
with their actual intrinsic Jacobian and reverse-action probability. Comparisons
retain a zero-score model with the same candidate cap and a force-informed vMF
control with explicit forward/reverse angular densities.

\paragraph{Completed comparison including preparation.}
We continued all six physical-control chains with unchanged states, kernels and
random streams to the corresponding tensor arm's cumulative query budget. All
original prefixes and complete continuations replay. The final budgets differ
from the tensor targets by at most six raw queries, with every residual reported.
The site prior reaches all four reference graphs at total costs 6,380/2,994;
the tensor models require 11,296/11,076 including preparation
(Figure~\ref{fig:joint-cost}). Deterministic controls still fail the difficult
parent and uniform geometry reaches none of the reference graphs. Thus learning
improves this particular fixed-step pilot but loses to the strong physical prior
on total first-passage cost. The continuation adds 58,946 raw evaluations to the
pilot's 17,014. A claim based on amortized reuse would require additional independent
parent batches; extrapolation from these four starts is insufficient.

First passage also does not resolve distributional convergence. In a secondary
diagnostic of the final half of each history, the maximum rank/folded split
$\widehat R$ for restrained potential is 1.802/1.906 for the tensor models and
1.126/1.208 for the site controls. Radius-of-gyration diagnostics also retain
substantial disagreement. These short histories do not qualify molecular
equilibrium ensembles, even after all four chains reach the reference graph.

The tensor coefficients were fitted to the quadratic score, whereas joint proposals
use the normalized mixture. Its actual training angular-score MSE is 575.2/602.7,
compared with 439.7/475.0 for the fitted quadratic score. The vector densities coincide
and their errors are unchanged. This diagnostic prevents interpreting the earlier

In the first-envelope study the tensor guide and force-informed control each
repair the difficult start in one replica, while the other remains unresolved.
The improved-envelope study does not make the learned success repeatable. Source
preparation is substantial: only 34/4096 training and
4/512 development parents pass the current support. Learning inherits 5,564
training-preparation queries and the training-source cost; physical controls do
not inherit unused training data. Each scored geometry requires both inversion
orientations. Neural evaluation, geometry searches and wall time are additional
costs, not interchangeable with raw oracle calls.

The broader eight-condition census finds eligible terminal exchanges only in two
conditions. The old bond-assignment mode rejects neutral radicals and encounters
algorithm errors for some metals. An experimental radical fallback increases graph
assignability but does not certify chemistry or spin, and is not adopted here.
Independent molecular generalization, reference-distribution convergence, practical
scaling and an anonymous reproducibility package remain outstanding. The reserved
evaluation conditions remain unused.

A geometry-only screen on the next eligible, 17-atom composition uses the first
32 supported training parents and four proposals per parent. It introduces no
new physical queries or fitting. Tensor models pass the specified graph check in
44/128 and 0/128 attempts, versus 101/128 and 96/128 for the site prior. This
composition includes O, P and Cl, absent from the guide's training composition.
The screen identifies a transfer limitation; it measures neither Metropolis
acceptance nor the energy distribution of that composition.

\paragraph{Normalized site-guide follow-up.}
We fit both new variants on the same training artifact for two fixed seeds,
using no additional physical queries. Training MSE is 558.8/545.6 for the vector
model and 571.6/549.6 for the mixture, versus 708.1 for the physical site prior.
The more complex directional mixture does not improve this training comparison.
Eight frozen sampling arms add 14,820 raw physical evaluations. Both learned
variants reach all four diagnostic graphs in both 256-step pilots; the site
control reaches three/two and the untrained mixture two/three. All complete
trajectories, physical pairs, random streams and marginal-mixture MH ratios replay.
Total costs remain 12,446/12,456 for vector and 12,460/12,452 for mixture, versus
2,734/2,756 for the short site controls. These endpoints have different cumulative
costs; the earlier full-cost physical continuation remains relevant adverse evidence.
Final-half restrained-potential rank/folded split $\widehat R$ is 1.950/1.658 for
the vector models and 1.677/1.808 for the mixtures. The new first passages also
leave molecular equilibrium unqualified.

On the identical 17-atom geometry screen, learned mixtures pass 99/128 and 86/128
proposals; vectors pass 92/128 and 85/128. The untrained mixture passes 93/128 and
94/128, and the unchanged physical site prior 101/128 and 96/128. The previous
zero-validity failure is absent. The physical component and initialization account
for much of this support, so the result does not establish learned transfer
superiority or an energy-distribution guarantee.

\section{Related work and position}
Learned molecular proposals with Metropolis correction already appear in
Timewarp~\citep{klein2023timewarp}, and flow-based MCMC acceleration is addressed
by Markovian flow matching~\citep{cabezas2024markovian}. Masked conditionals used
as MH proposals are also established outside molecular sampling, including
\citet{goyal2022maskedmh}. We do not claim masking, conditional MH, directional
distributions, tensor covariance or force regression as new general ideas.
Graph-editing MCMC with learned proposals is established by MARS~\citep{xie2021mars};
MOSAiCS applies evolutionary Monte Carlo to quantum-property optimization over
chemical space~\citep{karandashev2023mosaics}. Here we evaluate coordinate-space
physical sampling with deterministic graph perception and joint geometry
regeneration after attachment exchanges. The implementation and limited
development evidence qualify this proposed kernel. A competitive learned sampler,
broad generalization and useful equilibrium ensembles remain to be demonstrated.
VonMisesNet already uses graph-predicted von Mises mixtures for molecular torsions
and Boltzmann-oriented conformation generation~\citep{swanson2023vonmises}.
Equivariant VFM studies molecular generation and post-hoc conditional control
within variational flow matching~\citep{eijkelboom2025controlled}. Neither directional
mixtures nor generic symmetry-aware post-hoc control constitute novelty here.
