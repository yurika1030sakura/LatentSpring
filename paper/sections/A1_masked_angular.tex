\begin{abstract}
Energy-aware molecular sampling requires both an explicit statistical target and
proposals that respect its geometric support. A generator can supply useful initial
states while leaving its finite-step density unknown, and correct Metropolis
acceptance does not by itself make Cartesian moves efficient. We study learned
angular proposals that retain a terminal bond length and mask the moved atom's
direction from their conditioning context. A graph network predicts an equivariant
vector and traceless tensor defining an unnormalized angular score. Because the
context is unchanged by an accepted angular update, the unknown normalization
introduced by chemical-support rejection cancels in the Metropolis ratio, including
when candidate search has a finite trial cap. We fit the guide to physical angular
forces without requiring equilibrium training samples or oracle Hessians. A
development study compares the guide with uniform and force-informed angular
controls, retaining every failed search and preparation cost. Current results
establish implementation consistency and improve angular proposal acceptance
after an exact envelope correction. They do not yet establish repeatable
cross-isomer mixing, a total-cost learned advantage or calibrated molecular
equilibrium ensembles.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and scope}
Implicit molecular generators and equilibrium samplers solve different problems.
Our FlowMol3-based initializer~\citep{flowmol3}, trained on an OMol25
subset~\citep{omol25}, supplies coordinates, but its implemented finite-step
law need not have an available likelihood. Metropolis correction can instead use
the probability ratio of a subsequent conditional proposal. This avoids assigning
a fictitious source density, while leaving the practical problem of choosing
efficient molecular moves.

Our development evidence identifies separate failures. Cartesian local moves can
be rejected by a hard structural domain. Large unstructured rotations can satisfy
the domain yet have negligible physical acceptance. A learned selector can improve
the first connectivity transition without improving total-cost sampling. These
distinctions motivate a conditional angular guide, with target correctness and
empirical efficiency evaluated separately.

The present study fixes atom identities, total charge and spin multiplicity.
The target may contain different constitutional isomers; it is not an unconditional
distribution over chemical compositions or a fixed-stereoisomer ensemble. Perceived
bonds define a stated algorithmic support and proposal context. They are not supplied
OMol bond-supervision labels, and their successful assignment does not certify a
quantum electronic state. We retain a bond-free flow-matching initializer and make
no claim that this preliminary study solves broad molecular generation.

\section{Target and internal-coordinate update}
For $N$ labelled atoms, let $\HH=\{x\in\mathbb R^{N\times3}:\sum_i x_i=0\}$,
with its Euclidean volume in dimension $3(N-1)$. We use
\begin{equation}
 \pi(x)\propto {\bf1}_{D}(x)\exp[-U(x)/(kT)],\qquad
 U(x)=\tfrac12[E_{\rm raw}(x)+E_{\rm raw}(-x)]+\tfrac\gamma2\|x\|^2.
 \label{eq:angular-target}
\end{equation}
The raw potential is a fixed OMol25 eSEN energy model~\citep{esen}. Both inversion orientations are
evaluated; the conservative averaged force is
$[F_{\rm raw}(x)-F_{\rm raw}(-x)]/2$. A single raw evaluation cannot replace this
average inside an exponential weight or acceptance ratio. In the current pilot,
$kT=0.025851999786435$ eV and $\gamma=0.1$ eV/\AA$^2$.

The domain $D$ requires connected, nonoverlapping geometry and a successful
deterministic bond assignment and sanitization at the requested total charge.
This changes the target relative to unconstrained energy sampling. Our original
unconstrained experiments retain their original interpretation. The graph criterion
has material radical and metal limitations, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:angular-limits}.

Select a single-bonded terminal atom $i$ and its passive anchor $k$.
Write $x_i-x_k=r u$, with $u\in\sphere$. Hold $r$ and all passive relative
coordinates fixed, replace $u$, and recenter the coordinates. A linear chart on
$\HH$ consists of the passive relative coordinates and $x_i-x_k$; its determinant
is constant. The angular part of volume is $r^2\,dr\,d\Omega(u)$, so the radial
factor cancels between the forward and reverse update. The proposal density in
this section is conditional on this angular subspace, not a density on all of $\HH$.

\section{Masked equivariant angular guide}
\paragraph{Invariant context.}
The retained context $c$ contains the passive coordinates relative to $k$, radius
$r$, atom identities, electronic condition and the perceived graph $G$. Before
any geometric features or message passing, the moved atom's coordinate is replaced
by the anchor. Leaf and anchor roles are marked without assigning learned meaning
to absolute atom indices. An angular candidate is eligible only if its perceived
bond matrix equals $G$. Consequently the context is identical before and after
the update. Retaining the moved atom's old direction in the network would generally
invalidate the normalizer cancellation below.

\paragraph{Vector and tensor outputs.}
Two scalar message-passing blocks use atom embeddings, role embeddings, radial
features of the masked coordinates and the observed bond matrix. Invariant scalar
weights combine passive vectors and their traceless dyadic products to produce
a polar vector $\eta_\theta(c)$ and symmetric traceless matrix $A_\theta(c)$.
The angular score is
\begin{equation}
 s_\theta(u;c)=\eta_\theta(c)^\top u+u^\top A_\theta(c)u.
 \label{eq:angular-score}
\end{equation}
This is a linear--quadratic directional model. The tensor can represent symmetric
polar or planar structure without selecting an arbitrary oriented normal. A
vector-only ablation sets $A=0$. Joint atom permutations and orthogonal coordinate
transformations preserve the scalar score. We bound the combined coefficient norms
and initialize both outputs to zero.

\subsection{Capped support-conditioned proposals}
Let $D(c)\subset\sphere$ be the same-graph angular support. First consider uniform
directions accepted by an internal rejection step with probability
\begin{equation}
 a_c(u)={\bf1}_{D(c)}(u)\exp[s_\theta(u;c)-M(c)],
 \qquad M(c)\ge\sup_{u\in\sphere}s_\theta(u;c).
\end{equation}
Stop at the first success, or retain the old state after $K$ failed trials. Let
$Z(c)$ be the one-trial success probability and
$b_K(Z)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}(1-Z)^j$. The non-self generated density is
\begin{equation}
 Q_K(v\mid u,c)=\frac{b_K(Z(c))}{4\pi}
 {\bf1}_{D(c)}(v)\exp[s_\theta(v;c)-M(c)].
\end{equation}
The unknown $Z$, bound $M$ and cap factor are identical in the reverse update.
They therefore cancel without estimation.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional capped update]
For a frozen guide with invariant context, an eligible angular proposal $y$ is
accepted using
\begin{equation}
 \log R=-\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{kT}
 +s_\theta(u_x;c)-s_\theta(u_y;c)
 +\log\frac{p(i,k\mid y)}{p(i,k\mid x)}.
 \label{eq:angular-mh}
\end{equation}
The resulting update satisfies detailed balance with the declared target.
Exhausted searches contribute self-transitions.
\end{proposition}
We begin with uniform terminal selection. This supplies an exact proposal
\emph{ratio}; it does not supply a normalized conditional likelihood, source
likelihood or partition-function estimate. Composing the update with separately
corrected local and chemical-exchange kernels preserves the target, even when
the deterministic scan as a whole is not reversible.

\subsection{A more efficient rejection envelope}
Uniform score rejection can be inefficient for concentrated guides. Let
$\lambda_3\ge\lambda_2$ be the two largest eigenvalues of $A$, let $v$ be a top
eigenvector, and set $d=\lambda_3-\lambda_2$. The base proposal is a mixture of
von Mises--Fisher distributions with natural parameters $\eta\pm d v$ and weights
proportional to the reciprocals of their normalizers. Its density is proportional
to $\exp(\eta^\top u)\cosh(d v^\top u)$. A valid rejection bound is
\begin{equation}
 B=\lambda_3-\log\cosh d,\qquad
 \log a(u)=u^\top A u-\log\cosh(d v^\top u)-B\le0.
\end{equation}
After support rejection the generated density remains proportional to
$\exp s_\theta$. All additional constants depend only on $c$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular-mh} remains valid under the cap. Eigenvector sign changes
exchange mixture components; when $d=0$, the axis disappears. We treat this as
an exact implementation improvement, not an established new distributional theorem.

\section{Training and experimental contract}
The physical angular score at a supported point is
\begin{equation}
 g=r(I-u u^\top)F_{\HH,i}/(kT),\qquad
 \nabla_{\sphere}s_\theta=(I-u u^\top)(\eta+2Au),
\end{equation}
where $F_\HH$ includes the restraint and COM projection. We regress the latter
against a norm-capped version of $g$. This is supervised pointwise force fitting,
not an equilibrium score-matching theorem. It requires neither oracle Hessians
nor normalized guide densities. Our finite-time training states need not be
equilibrium samples for this regression objective; generalization and useful
sampling nevertheless require separate evidence.

The current table contains 2,782 supported scored states and 22,256 eligible
state/leaf examples from 34 generated training parents. Parents are sampled
uniformly, followed by an example within the parent, retaining parent/time
correlation in the provenance. Only generated training coordinates and their
audited physical forces are used. The four generated development starts are
separate. A reference connectivity is used as a diagnostic, not as a known
equilibrium point mass or a training label.

The two-layer guide has 6,082 parameters. We fit tensor and vector variants at
two seeds for 1,000 updates of 64 examples. The in-sample MSE of the norm-capped,
dimensionless angular-score labels is
439.7/475.0 for the tensor variant and 639.8/623.7 for the vector ablation, versus
730.0 for zero prediction. These numbers measure training fit only.

Every chain uses the schedule local/angle/exchange/local. Local MALA uses a fixed
mixture of three scales; chemical moves are uniform, invertible terminal exchanges
with their actual intrinsic Jacobian and reverse-action probability. Comparisons
retain a zero-score model with the same candidate cap and a force-informed vMF
control with explicit forward/reverse angular densities.

The efficient-envelope experiment is completed and fully replayed separately;
the original outcomes remain unchanged.

With the improved envelope, neither tensor model exhausts any of its256 searches;
they accept75/256 and58/256 angular updates. The vector ablations accept10/256 and
29/256, and zero guides4/256 and6/256. This repairs proposal-generation inefficiency
and improves local angular acceptance. It does not resolve the difficult parent's
connectivity in either tensor replica; the zero and vector controls each resolve
it in one replica. Total raw-query costs are12462/12456 for tensor,12484/12632 for
vector, and2972/2812 for zero guides. The improved implementation therefore does
not establish a full-cost learned sampling advantage.

In the first-envelope study the tensor guide and force-informed control each
repair the difficult start in one replica, while the other remains unresolved.
The improved-envelope study does not make the learned success repeatable. Source
preparation is substantial: only 34/4096 training and
4/512 development parents pass the current support. Learning inherits 5,564
training-preparation queries and the training-source cost; physical controls do
not inherit unused training data. Each scored geometry requires both inversion
orientations. Neural evaluation, geometry searches and wall time are additional
costs, not interchangeable with raw oracle calls.

The broader eight-condition census finds eligible terminal exchanges only in two
conditions. The old bond-assignment mode rejects neutral radicals and encounters
algorithm errors for some metals. An experimental radical fallback increases graph
assignability but does not certify chemistry or spin, and is not adopted here.
Independent molecular generalization, reference-distribution convergence, practical
scaling and an anonymous reproducibility package remain outstanding. The reserved
evaluation conditions remain unused.

\section{Related work and position}
Learned molecular proposals with Metropolis correction already appear in
Timewarp~\citep{klein2023timewarp}, and flow-based MCMC acceleration is addressed
by Markovian flow matching~\citep{cabezas2024markovian}. Masked conditionals used
as MH proposals are also established outside molecular sampling, including
\citet{goyal2022maskedmh}. We do not claim masking, conditional MH, directional
distributions, tensor covariance or force regression as new general ideas.
The candidate contribution is their concrete combination for invariant-context,
validity-conditioned molecular angular updates, together with a demonstrated
cost/quality advantage if subsequent experiments establish one. The current
development evidence is insufficient for that final empirical claim.
