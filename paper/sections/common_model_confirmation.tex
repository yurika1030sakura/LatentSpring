\section{Complete generators on common compositions}\label{app:common-models}
We evaluate the complete systems on the same 16 new compositions, eight each with
17--28 and 29--40 atoms. The panel is fixed by metadata hashes, excludes prior
confirmation panels, and is absent from both processed generator corpora. All
conditions are neutral organic singlets within the GAGA adaptation's element
vocabulary. Each system generates 16 raw structures per composition and run,
for 512 scored outputs per row of Table~\ref{tab:common-models}.

This comparison answers a different question from the shared-EGNN experiment.
The FlowMol variants use a 5.90-million-parameter inherited backbone; the GAGA
models use the 2.38-million-parameter EGNN trained from scratch on 20,000 OMol25
structures. Their architectures, training histories, physical supervision, and
costs differ. GAGA uses its validation-selected truncation at 650, evaluated at
both 128 calls and the complete 651-call schedule. The paired-update model keeps
its two original continuations. The correction-head study uses two head-fitting
seeds on one shared parent. Intervals condition on these fitted systems and
resample the 16 compositions.

The contextual head was the prespecified primary challenger. It improves joint
yield over its parent by 7.81 percentage points, with a 95\% interval of
$[4.88,10.94]$; the run-specific gains are 7.03 and 8.59 points. Its differences
from the small and wide 20,000-update heads are $-0.20$ points
($[-1.95,1.56]$) and $+0.59$ points ($[-1.17,2.34]$). Thus the better internal
force-target prediction of the contextual architecture does not produce an
established generation advantage over either simpler control.

Relative to GAGA at 128 and 651 calls, the contextual complete system gains
36.91 points ($[26.56,46.09]$) and 34.57 points ($[24.80,43.95]$). The original
paired-update system also exceeds both GAGA settings, by 28.32 points
($[20.70,35.74]$) and 25.98 points ($[18.75,33.40]$). These are complete-system
results on common conditions. The shared-backbone results in
Appendices~\ref{app:feedback-transfer} and~\ref{app:matched-connection} control
architecture and training exposure. Learned correction improves both EGNN
generators under equal physical supervision, with uncertain ordering between them.

\paragraph{Training duration and terminal noise.}
The published-model and terminal-noise controls were designated before reading
the new GFN2 comparison. The zero-noise output is captured from the same neural
trajectory as the published sampler, before its final 0.025\,\AA\ perturbation.
Removing that noise increases joint yield by 1.95 points ($[0.39,3.71]$).
The small learned correction exceeds the paired-update model by 8.79 points
($[3.91,13.67]$), and its zero-noise version by 6.84 points ($[1.95,11.72]$).
Their physical-training sets and parent histories differ, so this comparison
combines those choices with the adaptation mechanism.

After inspecting the main confirmation, we add a training-duration ablation
using the already-saved 2,000-update prefix of each small-head fit. All targets,
initial weights, training-state choices, and generation settings match the
20,000-update model. Longer fitting adds 3.71 points ($[1.76,5.86]$), with
positive changes in both runs. This supplemental ablation is a later diagnostic,
not an independently chosen confirmation panel.

Across all unoptimized outputs, the small 20,000-update head also lowers GFN2
energy by a mean 0.0951 eV/atom relative to its matched parent. The signed-change
95\% interval is $[-0.2022,-0.0364]$ eV/atom, and both runs decrease. This readout
includes invalid outputs and measures the change across paired generator
attempts, rather than equilibrium energy differences between isomers.

\paragraph{Additional graph diagnostics.}
Requiring RDKit to assign zero radical electrons gives joint yields of 46.29\%
for the small correction, 46.09\% for the contextual correction, and 9.57\% and
11.52\% for the two GAGA budgets. The relative result therefore also appears
under this stricter graph-assignment check. Within each composition and run,
all graph-valid outputs have distinct inferred labeled connectivity graphs.
This 16-draw diagnostic measures observed duplication, not coverage of the
possible isomers or their equilibrium probabilities.

The six-system confirmation contributes 3,072 neural trajectories and GFN2
readouts. Its parameter-matched control adds 40,000 head updates; the preceding
small/contextual capacity study used 80,000 updates. The supplemental published
and short-fit study adds 1,024 neural trajectories and 512 further outputs
obtained by sharing the published model's trajectory across the two noise
settings, with 1,536 GFN2 readouts. The physical teacher is reused throughout.
No generated coordinates are optimized.
