\section{Grouped Energy Supervision and the Implemented Readout}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
De novo generators including EDM, GeoLDM, SemlaFlow and FlowMol3
\citep{edm,geoldm,semlaflow,flowmol3} learn molecular composition and geometry jointly. Conditional
conformer and energy-targeted samplers address a different, fixed-system problem
\citep{torsionaldiff,noe2019boltzmann,fab,adjointsampling,idem,ewfm}.
Adjoint Sampling uses neural energy models across molecular systems
\citep{adjointsampling}. Log-variance objectives, partition-constant cancellation and off-policy
reference measures are prior work \citep{vargrad,nusken2021pathspace,richter2024improvedsampling,sendera2024offpolicy}.
In particular, off-policy log-dispersion regularisation already combines data-based training with
energy-labelled shape regularisation without additional on-policy samples \citep{ldr2026}.
We claim neither the divergence nor that combination as new. The question here is its implementation
and empirical behaviour in a bond-free molecular FlowMol3 model; broader related work is in
\Cref{app:related}.

\subsection{Backbone and the ideal density}
\label{sec:method:flow}
A composition $c$ specifies atom types and total charge, and coordinates lie in the zero-centroid
subspace $H$ of dimension $3(N-1)$. The backbone is FlowMol3 on OMol25 with no bond supervision
and at most $200$ atoms. The energy teacher supplies values in eV, with $kT=1$ eV a numerical
scale. Spin is fixed to a singlet and charge information is restricted by preprocessing; the
reported energy evaluations concern main-group organic parents.

FlowMol3's position head predicts an endpoint $D_\theta(x,t)$, not a velocity
\citep{flowmol3}. For the position schedule $\alpha$, the smooth position velocity is
\begin{equation}
 v_\theta(x,t)=\frac{\alpha'(t)}{1-\alpha(t)}\bigl(D_\theta(x,t)-x\bigr).
 \label{eq:headvelocity}
\end{equation}
For a deterministic field projected as $v_H(x,t)=P v_\theta(Px,t)$, fixed discrete labels, and an
interval where the flow is well-defined and invertible, change of variables gives
\begin{equation}
 \log q_T(x\mid c)=\log p_0(x_0)-\int_0^T\nabla_H\!\cdot v_H(x_t,t)\,\mathrm{d}t,
 \qquad x_0=\phi_T^{-1}(x).
 \label{eq:ffjord}
\end{equation}
Here $p_0$ is a normalised Gaussian on $H$. This is an ideal clamped-flow density; it is not the
joint CTMC sampler's conditional likelihood. History-dependent self-conditioning, guidance and
nonsmooth retractions require separate treatment.

\subsection{The grouped objective}
\label{sec:method:obj}
The BGFM loss family is $\Ls_{\rm FM}+\lambda_1\Ls_{\rm force}+\lambda_2\Ls_{\rm energy}$
with an optional anchor; the value-only experiments set $\lambda_1=\lambda_3=0$ and
$\lambda_E=\lambda_2=3\times10^{-5}$. The intended energy objective is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad\Ls_{\rm ours}=\Ls_{\rm FM}+\lambda_E\Ls_{\rm value},\qquad
 \Ls_{\rm value}=\frac1M\sum_m\Var_k[\ell_\theta(x_{m,k}\mid c_m)+E_{m,k}/kT].\quad}
 \label{eq:lvalue}
\end{equation}
Ideally $\ell_\theta=\log q_T$; the archived runs instead use the scalar
$\widehat\ell_\theta$ defined below. The labels are pre-computed on $K$ perturbed geometries per
parent. Parent-specific normalisers cancel because a variance is invariant to additive constants;
a variance pooled over different compositions would retain their different offsets. This explains
grouping, but does not identify cross-group mass.

\subsection{What the archived experiments actually optimise}
\label{sec:method:cancel}
\label{sec:method:impl}
The archived estimator takes $u_\theta(x,t)=D_\theta(x,t)$ directly, uses reverse Euler steps
$x\leftarrow x-\Delta t\,u_\theta(x,t_k)$ at midpoint \emph{times}, and subtracts the accumulated
ambient divergence from a prior formula with dimension $3(N-1)$. It neither performs
\eqref{eq:headvelocity} nor projects the differentiated head onto $H$. We call its scalar output
$\widehat\ell_\theta$. A midpoint time with a start-of-step state is not a second-order midpoint
solver. Replacing the step count therefore tests the legacy readout's sensitivity, not the
correctness of its interpretation as a generator likelihood.

Training additionally detaches each reverse state and the final prior argument. Parameters receive
gradients only through divergence evaluations, a surrogate that omits the state and prior paths
(\Cref{app:asimplemented}). Random self-conditioning in training can also change the field between
calls. No theorem below establishes convergence of this update. Non-finite-loss guards and a
non-finite-parameter abort are retained; $6/20$ value-containing runs diverged in the matched grid,
versus $0/10$ without a value term. Reported value-cell means are conditional on completion.

The corrected implementation converts the head with its actual schedule, centers both states and
differentiated fields, uses deterministic module behaviour, advances state and divergence at
midpoint stages, and retains the complete parameter-gradient graph. Its default $T=0.95$ explicitly
defines $q_{0.95}$, not an endpoint likelihood. Analytic and real-interface checks validate the
implementation; molecular retraining and a convergence study remain necessary.

\subsection{The archived force comparator}
\label{sec:forcebaseline}
For an independent Gaussian linear interpolant and its population velocity $v^*$,
$s^*(x,t)=[tv^*(x,t)-x]/[(1-t)\sigma^2]$. The archived force cells instead substitute the endpoint
head, giving
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s_\theta(x,t)=\frac{tD_\theta(x,t)-x}{(1-t)\sigma^2},\qquad
 \Ls_{\rm force}=\E\left[N^{-1}\sum_i\ell\bigl(\widetilde s_{\theta,i}(x_t,t)-F_i(x_1)/kT\bigr)\right].
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
This scales the endpoint-based readout by $(1-t)$ before clipping. The probes
$t\in\{0.85,0.92,0.97\}$ additionally compare a finite-time path readout with an endpoint force.
Alignment and retraction alter the independent-Gaussian path assumption. These cells test this
particular implemented comparator; their failure is not evidence against correctly specified force
or potential-score matching. \Cref{app:score,app:trap} gives the ideal identity and diagnostics.
