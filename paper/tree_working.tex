\begin{abstract}
Generating molecular structures from atomic composition requires spatial organization
to emerge without a supplied chemical graph. We introduce LatentSpring, a molecular
flow-matching framework with a harmonic source that marginalizes over latent spatial
dependence trees. Atom-dependent Gaussian edge displacements provide molecular length
scales, while a matrix-tree ratio gives the normalized source density on centered
coordinates. The resulting source integrates with an equivariant molecular flow
without bond labels or an additional learned source network. On neutral-organic
OMol25 compositions, matched continuations improve raw chemical-graph support over
an isotropic Gaussian source by 5.86 percentage points on 12 development compositions,
8.83 points on 10 additional compositions, and 8.20 points on a subsequent frozen
24-composition test. A calibrated covariance-Gaussian comparison supports a contribution
from mixture structure beyond the tested second-moment approximation. We further
incorporate physical feedback during training: a paired parameter update reduces
raw energy and force in both continuations under eSEN and independent GFN2-xTB
readouts. Generation uses 128 primitive denoiser calls and requires no per-output
energy selection or geometry optimization. These results establish latent spatial
dependence as a useful source design for composition-conditioned molecular flows
and demonstrate a complementary route for incorporating physical feedback into
the learned generator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Molecular generators must turn an unstructured coordinate distribution into a
configuration compatible with its atoms. The source distribution determines part
of this learning problem: independent Gaussian coordinates carry little molecular
organization, while graph-conditioned harmonic priors encode relationships that
must already be known. This distinction matters when the input specifies atomic
composition and electronic conditions without a chemical graph, as in the
bond-free OMol25 setting considered here.

LatentSpring introduces spatial dependence before learning the transport, while
leaving the final chemical graph unspecified. We sample a latent tree over atoms,
draw Gaussian displacements along its edges at atom-dependent scales, integrate
them into coordinates, and remove translation. Marginalizing the tree defines a
mixture of harmonic networks. The tree is an auxiliary source variable: the neural
field receives coordinates and molecular conditions, and can generate chemical
connectivities different from the sampled source tree. A matrix-tree expression
makes the mixture density and its posterior-averaged elastic coupling explicit.

We integrate this source with equivariant flow matching and geometry
self-conditioning. Controlled comparisons hold the backbone, initialization,
training data, optimization and inference budget fixed while changing the source.
The harmonic mixture improves raw graph support in both continuations and repeats
its advantage on two subsequent composition panels. Comparing with both a narrower
shell source and an approximate covariance-matched Gaussian distinguishes the
useful source structure from simply imposing near-fixed distances or increasing
second moments.

Physical feedback supplies a complementary training signal. Local force-escorted
FIT samples define paired training targets, whose parameter update is transferred
relative to a matched replay continuation. The final generator retains network-only
inference, and a frozen new-composition test confirms energy and force improvements
under two approximate potentials. Jarzynski-type complete-work weights provide a
specified local energy-reweighting extension with explicit reference-density terms.

Our contributions are: (i) a normalized molecular harmonic-mixture source with
latent spatial connectivity and no supplied bond graph; (ii) its integration with
molecular flow matching and training-only physical feedback; and (iii) controlled
source comparisons and new-composition, cross-potential evidence for raw generation
quality. The construction builds on established tree-distribution, flow-matching
and escorted-work principles. Our empirical target is raw coordinate generation;
thermal-equilibrium sampling is a distinct claim with additional requirements.

\section{Related work and scope}
\paragraph{Sources and molecular flows.}
FlowMol3 combines joint topology/coordinate generation with self-conditioning,
fake atoms and geometry distortion \citep{flowmol3}. We reuse its equivariant
vector-field implementation; self-conditioning is established prior art.
ET-Flow constructs harmonic sources for graph-conditioned conformers \citep{etflow}.
Its molecular input graph and benchmark differ from our composition-conditioned
coordinate task. Source-Guided Flow Matching studies guidance by changing sources
under a frozen field \citep{wang2026sourceguided}; condition-dependent source
learning is also established \citep{kim2026bettersource}. Thus neither changing
noise nor learning a source in general is our novelty claim.

\paragraph{Latent trees.}
Factored distributions over trees and tractable tree learning are classical
\citep{meila2000trees,duan2023spanning}. Random-spanning-tree difference priors,
including Gaussian mixtures and conjugate tree posteriors, have also been studied
for imaging inverse problems \citep{everink2026random}. The molecular instantiation
here uses three-dimensional isotropic edge displacements, an intrinsic centered
coordinate measure, species-dependent scales, and no supplied molecular graph.
Our experiments isolate the utility of this source structure through matched
Gaussian, shell and covariance-Gaussian comparisons.

\paragraph{Target distribution and physics.}
OMol25 provides calculated structures and properties across electronic conditions
\citep{omol25}; its empirical geometry distribution is not a specified-temperature
canonical ensemble. Harmonic source energies encode spatial fluctuations, whereas
an electronic potential describes different physics. Jarzynski's relation and its
single-molecule pulling applications connect nonequilibrium work to equilibrium
free energies \citep{jarzynski1997master,hummerszabo2001}. An arbitrary learned
transport or higher graph-validity rate does not by itself supply the required
work weights. We therefore separate our measured generation task from the optional
energy-correction construction in Appendix~\ref{app:jarzynski}.

\section{A harmonic source with latent connectivity}
Let $c=(z_1,\ldots,z_N,Q,S)$ contain atomic numbers, total charge and spin
multiplicity. Coordinates lie in
$\HH_N=\{X\in\RR^{N\times3}:\sum_i x_i=0\}$, with intrinsic Lebesgue measure
and dimension $3(N-1)$. For positive symmetric affinities $a_{ij}$, define
\begin{equation}
 p(T\mid c)=\frac{\prod_{(i,j)\in T}a_{ij}}{\tau(a)},\qquad
 \tau(a)=\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree}}\prod_{(i,j)\in T}a_{ij}.
 \label{eq:tree-law}
\end{equation}
The selected fixed source uses $a_{ij}=u_i u_j$. We set $u_i=0.05$ for H and
F/Cl/Br/I, and otherwise $u_i=\max(1,\text{default valence}(z_i)-1)$.
This species heuristic discourages terminal atoms from becoming high-degree
source hubs; it neither imposes nor predicts chemical bond orders. The fixed
source uses species but not $Q,S$ beyond validating their admissibility.

\paragraph{Gaussian edge laws and sampling.}
Let $\ell_{ij}$ be the sum of covalent radii and
$\sigma_{ij}^2=\ell_{ij}^2\exp(s^2)/3$, with $s=0.2$ fixed throughout.
The edge-displacement law is
\begin{equation}
 h_{ij}(d)=(2\pi\sigma_{ij}^2)^{-3/2}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\|d\|^2}{2\sigma_{ij}^2}\right].
 \label{eq:edge-law}
\end{equation}
The scale convention matches the covariance of the previously tested lognormal
shell edges; it was not optimized on the confirmation outputs. Sample a tree
using weighted Wilson sampling, draw its independent edge vectors, integrate
along the tree, and subtract the coordinate mean. The resulting nominal source
is invariant under rotations and permutations of identical species.

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic mixture density]
For normalized isotropic edge densities,
\begin{equation}
 q_0(X\mid c)=N^{3/2}
 \frac{\tau\big(a_{ij}h_{ij}(x_i-x_j)\big)}{\tau(a)}
 \label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
is normalized on $\HH_N$. For a log affinity $\alpha_e=\log a_e$,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_{\alpha_e}\log q_0(X\mid c)
 =\Pr(e\in T\mid X,c)-\Pr(e\in T\mid c).
 \label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Appendix~\ref{app:proof} applies standard incidence-matrix and tree-ensemble
identities. Two grounded Laplacian determinants evaluate the density in cubic
time; the implementation uses positive log-space elimination for numerical
stability. This is a density marginalized over trees, not conditional on the
particular tree used to sample $X_0$.

\paragraph{What physics is applied?}
For a fixed tree, define the dimensionless spring energy
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal U_T(X)=\frac12\sum_{(i,j)\in T}
 \frac{\|x_i-x_j\|^2}{\sigma_{ij}^2}.
\end{equation}
The component is a normalized Gaussian proportional to $e^{-\mathcal U_T}$.
The marginal $-\log q_0$ is a soft mixture of these spring energies, with the
component normalization constants retained. Its coordinate score is
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_{x_i}\log q_0(X\mid c)=
 -\sum_{j\ne i}\Pr((i,j)\in T\mid X,c)
 \frac{x_i-x_j}{\sigma_{ij}^2}.
 \label{eq:source-score}
\end{equation}
Differentiating Equation~\eqref{eq:source} gives this posterior-averaged elastic
coupling, which describes how the marginalized source organizes atomic positions.
The springs have zero rest displacement; covalent radii set their fluctuation
scale, not an excluded-volume guarantee or physical bond-equilibrium potential.
The evaluated sampler draws this source; it does not use Equation~\eqref{eq:source-score}
as an additional learned force or guidance term.

\section{Integration with molecular flow matching}
Sample $X_0\sim q_0(\cdot\mid c)$ and a reference $X_1$ from the training corpus.
A type-preserving assignment and proper rotation couple the pair, followed by a
shared independent Haar augmentation. This preserves the invariant source
marginal while allowing dependence between paired endpoints
(Appendix~\ref{app:symmetry}). We use the linear path
$X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1$ and target $V^*=X_1-X_0$.

The FlowMol coordinate updater returns $D_\theta(X_t,t)$ and the research
parameterization interprets $v_\theta=D_\theta-X_t$ as velocity. This explicitly
trained displacement interpretation differs from a native endpoint head.
A first pass yields $v^{(0)}$ and the endpoint estimate
$\widehat X_1=X_t+(1-t)v^{(0)}$. A second pass recycles that estimate through the
existing geometry self-conditioning modules to obtain $v^{(1)}$. Both passes
remain differentiable, with objective
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm FM}=\frac12\EE\left[
 \|v^{(0)}-V^*\|^2+\|v^{(1)}-V^*\|^2\right].
\end{equation}
Atomic categories and the declared charge interface remain fixed; edge inputs
carry the constant no-bond interface. No bond-classification loss is introduced.
The sampled tree is not supplied to the vector field. Self-conditioning and the
endpoint-estimation identity are existing techniques shared by all primary arms.

Generation integrates $v^{(1)}$ with 32 midpoint steps and two passes per field
call, totaling 128 primitive denoiser evaluations per attempted sample, followed
by 0.025\,\AA\ centered Gaussian noise. We retain this numerical sampler exactly
for comparisons. Source normalization alone does not give the finite integrator
plus noise a qualified final density. Source-comparison training uses FM only; existing
force/energy training interfaces are not evidence for a thermal law, and the
independent-Gaussian velocity-to-score proxy is invalid for this source/coupling.

\subsection{Physical feedback through paired training targets}
A frozen harmonic generator supplies FIT anchors $A$ on compositions excluded from
evaluation. For graph-supported anchors, a bounded force displacement defines local
escorted samples $Y=X+\delta_A$, with $X\sim q_A=\mathcal N_{\HH_N}(A,\sigma_A^2I)$.
The local scale and displacement are fixed from the anchor force before drawing
particles (Appendix~\ref{app:local-work}). We train a physical-target continuation
and a replay continuation from the same weights and matched training draws, then
transfer their parameter difference:
\begin{equation}
 \theta_* = \theta_0 + (\theta_{\rm physical}-\theta_{\rm replay}).
 \label{eq:physical-transfer}
\end{equation}
This applies established parameter arithmetic \citep{ilharco2023arithmetic} with
coefficient one and fixed model buffers. The update changes the learned generator,
so inference requires no force calls or structural relaxation.

Complete escorted-work weighting \citep{escorted2008} defines a local physical
extension. On its specified structural support, let
\begin{equation}
 W_A=E_+(Y)-E_+(A)+kT[\log q_A(X)-\log q_A(Y)].
 \label{eq:main-local-work}
\end{equation}
The full weight includes the reference-density correction for the escort, rather
than only an energy difference. Its expectation gives the normalizer of the
Gaussian-restrained local energy tilt. We evaluate both uniform and complete-work
weights; the simpler force update is the prespecified candidate for the fresh
cross-potential test. Appendix~\ref{app:local-work} provides the derivation,
finite-particle implementation and matched training controls.

\section{Experimental design}
\paragraph{Task and training.}
We evaluate coordinate generation for neutral-singlet organic compositions,
containing C and H and elements from H/B/C/N/O/F/Si/P/S/Cl/Br/I.
The implementation supports200 atoms; experiments are limited to8--40.
Two continuations share one pretrained warm checkpoint. Each uses 3,000 distinct
OMol25 training rows and 3,000 optimizer updates, with identical rows and initial
weights across source arms within that continuation. The source is fixed and
adds no fitted parameters. The vector-field parameter count is 5,904,369.
Backbone learning rate is $2\cdot10^{-5}$; activated self-conditioning modules use
$3\cdot10^{-4}$. Two differentiable passes are used for every primary source arm.

The training subset is deliberately narrower than the full processed corpus:
neutral-singlet organic references must pass the unchanged graph assay and admit
the declared bounded-edge, degree-capped geometric scaffold used in an earlier
training selection. The source-SC models reuse these coordinates but ignore the
stored scaffold. All arms share this selection. It is derived geometric selection,
not supplied bond supervision, and limits claims about unrestricted OMol25.
The data selection and training parameters are retained in the frozen protocols.

\paragraph{Panels and separation.}
Original reference qualification identified31 accepted compositions among 78
metadata-eligible candidates. An earlier21-composition panel used up to8 per size
bin; the present12-composition development panel is a fixed subset of that panel.
After comparing all three source arms, we selected harmonic sources jointly
across the two continuations and froze both harmonic and Gaussian models.
We then evaluated \emph{all} remaining10 qualified compositions. They happen to
have 17--28 atoms; no generator output was used to filter them. Exact count-vector
checks find no matching composition in 3,902,107 processed training rows or39,415
validation rows. References qualify the assay and do not initialize the generator
or enter fitting. Separation from these corpora does not certify unrecorded
pretraining or all trajectory relations. The additional panel reuses the same
trained models, not two further independent training replicates.

\paragraph{Metrics and uncertainty.}
The first two panels use 64 samples per composition; the fresh cross-potential
panel uses 32. Geometry support requires
contact connectivity and no short-distance overlap using 1.25 and 0.6 times summed
covalent radii. Graph support additionally requires successful chemical-graph
perception. This assay does not certify quantum stability, the complete electronic
state or synthetic accessibility. Failed outputs and validator exceptions stay in
the denominator. We also record distinct perceived connectivity within each
composition and actual generation time. Paired bootstrap intervals condition on
the fitted models and fixed compositions; a composition bootstrap separately
summarizes heterogeneity. Neither is a broad population-generalization certificate.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
LatentSpring shows that a fixed, structured source can improve a molecular flow
without a supplied graph or an additional learned source head. Marginalizing
spatial dependence trees provides a tractable probabilistic construction, and the
source comparisons support its practical value across the reported composition
panels. Training-only physical feedback further improves raw energy and force
readouts while preserving the observed graph-support rate in the fresh test.

The evaluated domain is a selected neutral-organic subset of OMol25; models share
a warm start, and the tests reuse two fitted continuations. Conditional intervals
quantify sampling uncertainty on these compositions, rather than uncertainty over
all possible retrained models. The measured advantage at this continuation budget
does not establish an asymptotic advantage after converged large-scale training.
Graph support is a structural assay, and the two energy models are approximate:
these readouts do not imply proximity to converged quantum minima, synthetic
accessibility, or a canonical energy distribution. The local escorted-work identity
in Appendix~\ref{app:local-work} and global connection in
Appendix~\ref{app:jarzynski} specify their respective targets and assumptions.
The current evidence supports the source and physical-feedback construction for
raw generation, with broader chemical coverage and equilibrium sampling as
separate directions for development.

\section{Normalization and affinity-gradient proof}\label{app:proof}
For a tree $T$, let $B_T\in\RR^{(N-1)\times N}$ be an oriented incidence matrix
and $U\in\RR^{N\times(N-1)}$ an orthonormal basis of the centered subspace.
Because $B_T\mathbf1=0$, $(B_TU)(B_TU)^\top=B_TB_T^\top$. Cauchy--Binet expresses
$\det(B_TB_T^\top)$ as the sum of squares of its $N$ maximal minors. Each minor
of a tree incidence matrix is $\pm1$, so the determinant is $N$. The absolute
Jacobian in one spatial dimension is therefore $\sqrt N$, and in three dimensions
it is $N^{3/2}$. Consequently the component density is
$q(X\mid T,c)=N^{3/2}\prod_{e\in T}h_e(B_TX)$ and integrates to one. Averaging
with Equation~\eqref{eq:tree-law} yields Equation~\eqref{eq:source} and preserves
normalization. Differentiating the log of each finite positive tree partition
with respect to $\alpha_e$ gives its edge-inclusion probability, proving
Equation~\eqref{eq:gradient}. These are applications of standard incidence and
tree-ensemble identities.

\section{Source invariance under symmetry coupling}\label{app:symmetry}
Let $G$ be the compact group of proper rotations and permutations of identical
conditioning labels. Suppose $q_0$ is $G$-invariant, $X\sim q_0$, and a pairing
algorithm selects $g(X,Y)\in G$ using a target $Y$. If $H$ is an independent Haar
draw, then conditional on the orbit of $X$ and on the selected $g$, $HgX$ has
the uniform orbit distribution of $HX$, by Haar invariance. Integrating over the
unchanged source-orbit distribution recovers $q_0$. This argument permits a
non-Gaussian source and explains the shared augmentation used in the pairing.
It does not imply independence of source and target after pairing or justify
the independent-Gaussian velocity-to-score formula.

\section{Covariance controls}
For $R=\ell\exp(sZ-s^2/2)$ with $Z\sim N(0,1)$, $\EE R^2=\ell^2\exp(s^2)$.
A uniform direction has second moment $I/3$, so each edge has covariance
$vI=\ell^2\exp(s^2)I/3$. Let $A_T$ map edge coordinates to centered node
coordinates. Independence gives $C_T=A_T\operatorname{diag}(v_e)A_T^\top$.
Replacing the edge vectors by independent Gaussians of covariance $v_eI$ leaves
$C_T$ unchanged and, since all component means are zero, leaves $\EE_TC_T$
unchanged. The single-Gaussian control uses a finite, composition-seeded average
of these matrices. Averaging this matrix over within-species permutations preserves
positive definiteness on the centered subspace and yields species invariance.
The Gaussian is exactly normalized for its chosen covariance; equality to the
infinite tree-mixture covariance remains approximate.



\paragraph{Additional source-adaptation prior art.}
Source-Guided Flow Matching already obtains guidance by changing the source under
a frozen vector field, including source-space importance and MCMC sampling
\citep{wang2026sourceguided}. Conditional source learning within flow matching
also appears in \citet{kim2026bettersource}. Random-tree continuous difference
priors have imaging applications \citep{everink2026random}, and molecular diffusion
fine-tuning with physical rewards appears in \citet{zhou2025rlpf}. A positive pilot
would therefore establish only an internal molecular mechanism result. Comparative
utility and cost would still be needed to substantiate the specific structured
source adaptation contribution.

\section{Connection to Jarzynski and escorted transport}\label{app:jarzynski}
The motivating connection to nonequilibrium force measurements is retained, but
is separate from the evaluated validity experiment. Jarzynski relates exponential
work averages to equilibrium free-energy differences under a qualified
nonequilibrium protocol; Hummer--Szabo reconstruction applies this principle to
single-molecule pulling \citep{jarzynski1997master,hummerszabo2001}.
Artificial escort flows generalize the transport protocol while retaining the
appropriate work accounting \citep{escorted2008}. None of these identities is a
new result of this paper.

A change-of-variables version clarifies what our normalized source could supply.
Fix a composition and intrinsic coordinate measure. Let
$\rho_1(x)=\mathbf1_\Omega(x)\exp[-\beta E(x)]$ be a target with finite, nonzero
$Z_1=\int_{\HH_N}\rho_1(x)\,dx$, for example a declared confined molecular ensemble.
Normalizability must not be assumed for dissociating fragments in unbounded space.
Let $q_0>0$ be the normalized source in Equation~\eqref{eq:source}, and let
$\Phi:\HH_N\to\HH_N$ be a differentiable bijection with nonzero intrinsic Jacobian.
For $\Phi(x_0)\in\Omega$, define the generalized work
\begin{equation}
 W_\Phi(x_0)=E(\Phi(x_0))+
 \beta^{-1}\log q_0(x_0)-
 \beta^{-1}\log|\det D\Phi(x_0)|,
\end{equation}
and set $W_\Phi=+\infty$ otherwise. Then
\begin{align}
 \EE_{q_0}[e^{-\beta W_\Phi}]
 &=\int_{\HH_N}\rho_1(\Phi(x_0))|\det D\Phi(x_0)|\,dx_0\\
 &=Z_1.
\end{align}
Equivalently, the auxiliary source energy $E_0=-\beta^{-1}\log q_0$ has $Z_0=1$
in the declared measure, yielding a Jarzynski-type normalizer identity.
The Jacobian term accounts for compression by the escort map; the work cannot
be replaced by an endpoint energy difference alone. Weighted observables require
the same complete weight. Even a correct identity need not give an efficient
finite-sample estimator when the weights have poor overlap.

For a sufficiently regular exact ODE flow, the intrinsic log determinant is the
integral of the intrinsic velocity divergence. Our measured midpoint sampler
followed by terminal Gaussian noise is not that exact diffeomorphism. A valid
implementation would need either its own qualified map/Jacobian or a specified
forward/reverse stochastic path law that includes the noise and every correction.
We have not performed this global reweighting for the source-SC results. A
separate local anchored work teacher is defined and tested in
Appendix~\ref{app:local-work}. No global work-variance,
free-energy, effective-sample-size or Boltzmann-distribution claim is inferred
from their graph or geometry pass rates.
