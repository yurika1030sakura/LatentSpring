\begin{abstract}
Molecular flow matching often starts from isotropic noise or a spatial prior
constructed from a supplied chemical graph. We study a learnable coordinate
source when composition and electronic conditions are given but bonds are
unavailable. A latent dependence tree connects normalized isotropic edge laws;
a matrix-tree ratio gives the marginal coordinate density on the translation-free
subspace. Small networks learn affinities directly from coordinates without bond
targets or gradients through sampled trees. We integrate this source with molecular
flow matching and distinguish source fit from generation utility. Tree sources
repeatedly improve geometric support in bounded OMol25 studies, while chemical
graph improvements vary across continuations. Broad-panel reference checks expose
limited graph-assay coverage. A prospective test therefore selects21 neutral
organic monomers whose original references all pass the unchanged assay and whose
compositions are absent from two verified processed corpora. On10,752 generated
outputs, learned node/pair sources still do not establish an increment over the
fixed shell source; a separate static tree-context adapter also fails its matched
control. The framework makes the source probability law explicit, but these
results do not establish competitive superiority or equilibrium sampling. They
motivate testing source learning against terminal generation utility rather than
assuming that improved coordinate likelihood is sufficient.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The starting distribution of a molecular generator specifies more than its noise
scale: it determines which spatial dependencies the learned transport must create.
Flow matching offers a flexible way to learn this transport
\citep{lipman2023flowmatching}. Existing molecular implementations include joint
generation of atom types, bonds and coordinates \citep{flowmol3}, and conformer
generation conditioned on a supplied molecular graph \citep{etflow}. Here we
consider a different, narrower task: generate coordinates conditional on a
composition, total charge and spin multiplicity, without supplied bond labels.

A graph-informed harmonic source is useful when the graph is known. When bonds
are unavailable, replacing them by one inferred graph commits to a discrete
structure and can obscure the probability law of the coordinate source. Our
construction instead averages over latent dependence trees. Every component has
a normalized density on the translation-free coordinate space, and the full
mixture admits tractable normalization and learning. The latent tree supplies a
coordinate dependence pattern; it need not represent the final chemical bonds.

This construction combines established tree-ensemble identities with a
finite-length, non-Gaussian spatial edge law. We do not claim a new matrix-tree
theorem, tree-mixture principle, or physical work identity. The proposed method
contribution is an explicit, learnable spatial source without supplied bonds and
its integration into molecular flow matching. Whether this combination provides
competitive practical value is the empirical question, rather than a consequence
of normalization. Our present evidence supports a source-structure effect beyond
the tested single-Gaussian controls, while an extra benefit of affinity learning
remains unestablished.

\section{Related work and task boundary}
\paragraph{Molecular flow matching.}
FlowMol3 studies joint molecular topology and geometry generation and reports
improvements from self-conditioning, fake atoms and training geometry distortion
\citep{flowmol3}. We reuse its vector-field implementation with the repository's
electronic conditioning and coordinate-only research path. Published native
FlowMol3 validity on its own benchmark is not directly comparable to our
bond-free, charged/open-shell OMol25 development panel. ET-Flow uses a harmonic
prior for conformer generation with a supplied molecular graph \citep{etflow}.
Our harmonic control is a controlled source ablation in the same backbone, not
a reproduction of ET-Flow's training or benchmark performance.

\paragraph{Tree distributions.}
Tractable factored distributions and learning over trees have a long history
\citep{meila2000trees}. Spanning-tree likelihoods can characterize dependence
without requiring the true population dependence graph to be a tree
\citep{duan2023spanning}. These are direct antecedents of our marginalization and
affinity learning. Our application uses an intrinsic coordinate measure and
normalized isotropic edge kernels, with no observed chemical graph.

\paragraph{Latent conditioning.}
Latent-CFM conditions the transport on features extracted by pretrained
latent-variable models to exploit target clustering \citep{samaddar2026latentcfm}.
Latent or mixture-component conditioning is therefore not itself our proposed
innovation. The additional experiment here retains the explicit source tree as
context, without a target encoder, and tracks its node labels through molecular
symmetry coupling. Its usefulness must be measured against parameter-matched
independent-tree conditioning.

\paragraph{Physical meaning.}
OMol25 provides molecular geometries with electronic conditions and calculated
properties \citep{omol25}. Its empirical geometry distribution is not automatically
a canonical ensemble for a specified temperature. We use an eSEN potential
\citep{esen} for a separate output readout. Neither low potential energy nor a
normalized source proves that the final generator follows a Boltzmann law.

\section{A normalized latent-tree spatial source}
Let $c=(z_1,\ldots,z_N,Q,S)$ denote atomic numbers, integer total charge and spin
multiplicity. We remove unweighted translation and use intrinsic Lebesgue measure
on $\HH_N=\{X\in\RR^{N\times3}:\sum_i x_i=0\}$, of dimension $3(N-1)$.
For positive symmetric affinities $a_{ij}(c)$, define
\begin{equation}
 p_\eta(T\mid c)=\frac{\prod_{(i,j)\in T}a_{ij}(c)}{\tau(a(c))},\qquad
 \tau(a)=\sum_{T\text{ spanning tree}}\prod_{(i,j)\in T}a_{ij}.
 \label{eq:tree-law}
\end{equation}
We sample $T$, draw independent edge displacements, integrate them on the tree,
and subtract the coordinate mean. Weighted Wilson sampling supplies the tree law.

\paragraph{Finite-length edge law.}
Let $\ell_{ij}$ be the sum of the two covalent radii. Draw a uniform direction
and a lognormal radius of mean $\ell_{ij}$ and log standard deviation $s$.
The resulting normalized density against three-dimensional displacement measure is
\begin{equation}
 h_{ij}(d)=\frac{\exp[-(\log r-\mu_{ij})^2/(2s^2)]}
 {4\pi s\sqrt{2\pi}\,r^3},\quad
 r=\|d\|>0,\quad \mu_{ij}=\log\ell_{ij}-s^2/2.
 \label{eq:edge-law}
\end{equation}
The $r^{-3}$ factor includes both the lognormal and solid-angle Jacobians. We
fix $s=0.2$ in these studies. This radial distribution is a source heuristic,
not an electronic interaction potential. Exact coincidences have zero probability;
the current differentiable density implementation rejects them as numerical inputs.

\begin{proposition}[Intrinsic tree-mixture density]
If every $h_{ij}$ is a normalized displacement density, then
\begin{equation}
 q_\eta(X\mid c)=N^{3/2}
 \frac{\tau\big(a_{ij}(c)h_{ij}(x_i-x_j)\big)}{\tau(a(c))}
 \label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
is normalized on $\HH_N$. For an edge log affinity $\alpha_e=\log a_e$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial\log q_\eta(X\mid c)}{\partial\alpha_e}
 =\Pr(e\in T\mid X,c)-\Pr(e\in T\mid c).
 \label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}. Equation~\eqref{eq:source} is the
marginal over all trees, rather than a density conditional on one sampled tree.
Two grounded Laplacian determinants evaluate it in cubic time. Our implementation
uses positive elimination in log space to avoid cancellation when edge weights
span a large dynamic range.

\paragraph{Learning affinities.}
The fixed reference has $a_{ij}=u_i u_j$, where $u_i=0.05$ for H and the common
terminal halogens and otherwise uses a simple default-valence-derived propensity.
This is a heuristic and does not enforce chemical valence. A node network adds
$b_i+b_j$ to $\log a_{ij}$; a pair network adds a symmetric pair score. Inputs are
species embeddings, pooled composition, $\log N$, $Q/N$ and $\log S$. No input
coordinates or chemical bonds are provided to the affinity head at generation.
Coordinate data fit its parameters through
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm source}(\eta)=
 \EE_{(X,c)\sim\mathcal D_{\rm train}}
 \left[-\frac{\log q_\eta(X\mid c)}{3(N-1)}\right].
\end{equation}
The node scores and pair score each use $2\tanh$ bounding. Consequently the summed
node correction spans $[-4,4]$ and the pair correction $[-2,2]$; these are not
strictly nested equal-range capacity ablations. Both learned priors retain the
same fixed radial edge law.

\section{Integration with flow matching}
After fitting the source, sample $X_0\sim q_\eta(\cdot\mid c)$ and a training
endpoint $X_1$, apply the same type-preserving assignment/rotation coupling in all
arms, and interpolate coordinates. Shared Haar augmentation preserves the
rotation- and identical-label-permutation-invariant source law (Appendix~\ref{app:symmetry}).
The equivariant vector field learns the conditional path displacement. The pilot
uses coordinate flow matching alone and no chemical-bond loss. Existing force/
energy training interfaces remain available, but their independent-Gaussian
velocity-to-score proxy is not valid for this correlated non-Gaussian pairing.

At generation, composition, charge and spin are fixed inputs. We integrate the
displacement parameterization with64 midpoint steps to $t=1$ and add0.025\,\AA
centered Gaussian noise. This finite numerical sampler is the evaluated law.
The continuous-flow density interface can use Equation~\eqref{eq:source} at an
integrated preimage with its divergence correction. That fact does not provide
the exact density of the evaluated midpoint-plus-noise sampler. We therefore
make no absolute final-likelihood or importance-ESS claim.

\paragraph{Tree-conditioned transport variant.}
The source-only flow receives coordinates sampled from a tree but does not receive
the tree itself. We additionally implement a small invariant adapter for that
information. For each atom pair, its inputs are the source-tree edge indicator,
$\log(1+\text{tree-path hops})$, and the mean of the two $\log(1+\text{degree})$
values. A $3\rightarrow32\rightarrow d_{\rm edge}$ SiLU network adds a residual
to the existing equivariant field's edge embedding. A zero final layer preserves
the warm field at initialization. The adapter sees no observed chemical bonds.

An actual-tree arm retains the tree that generated $X_0$. An independent-tree arm
uses a separate draw from the same tree law. Both have identical initialization,
parameter counts and learning rates: $2\cdot10^{-5}$ for the backbone and
$3\cdot10^{-4}$ for the adapter. A no-context reference uses the same fixed shell
source and continuation budget. Context follows both axes of the source atom
permutation during pairing and remains fixed throughout generation. Conditioning
on $T$ changes the probability object to a joint/conditional flow, so the existing
marginal-density interface explicitly rejects this variant. No final-density or
thermal claim is made. The complete paired comparison retains every output.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and remaining evidence.}
The paired draw intervals condition on fitted models and the respective fixed
composition panels, without multiplicity adjustment. The additional panel also
has a descriptive stratified composition bootstrap. These summaries do not
quantify new-composition or pretraining-seed uncertainty. Broad held-composition
evaluation, comparisons with independently trained learned generators and a
replicated useful learned-source increment remain outstanding. A favorable source
likelihood or one conditional energy interval cannot replace those experiments.

\section{Discussion}
The current framework makes a bond-free source probability law explicit and
learnable. The new controls support a useful source structure beyond a single
Gaussian approximation, while affinity learning's downstream value remains
unresolved. Narrow finite-length edges are not consistently better than a harmonic
tree mixture. One concrete open question is whether the flow should retain the
sampled tree as conditioning, instead of receiving its coordinates alone. The
implemented static adapter does not establish a useful increment in the matched
actual/sham/no-context test. The32-composition comparison supports geometric gains
but not a repeated shell-tree graph advantage. Poor reference coverage shows why
a primary single-molecule benchmark must align its input domain and assay before
further architectural claims. The prospective neutral-monomer panel supplies that
controlled test, and its completed results still do not establish extra learned-
source utility. One focused next hypothesis is to fit source probabilities to
terminal generation utility with a frozen decoder, rather than to reference
coordinate likelihood. Exact source importance ratios support such a test
without assigning a marginal density to the noisy decoder. A bounded pilot is now
implemented (Appendix~\ref{app:source-utility}), but no performance result is yet
claimed. Frozen-decoder initial-noise learning already appears in Noise PPO
\citep{miao2025noiseppo}, and inference-time noise optimization in DNO
\citep{tang2024dno}; the generic adaptation idea is not our novelty. The present results do not
establish a calibrated energy distribution or a completed competitive benchmark.

\section{Normalization and affinity-gradient proof}\label{app:proof}
For a tree $T$, let $B_T\in\RR^{(N-1)\times N}$ be an oriented incidence matrix
and $U\in\RR^{N\times(N-1)}$ an orthonormal basis of the centered subspace.
Because $B_T\mathbf1=0$, $(B_TU)(B_TU)^\top=B_TB_T^\top$. Cauchy--Binet expresses
$\det(B_TB_T^\top)$ as the sum of squares of its $N$ maximal minors. Each minor
of a tree incidence matrix is $\pm1$, so the determinant is $N$. The absolute
Jacobian in one spatial dimension is therefore $\sqrt N$, and in three dimensions
it is $N^{3/2}$. Consequently the component density is
$q(X\mid T,c)=N^{3/2}\prod_{e\in T}h_e(B_TX)$ and integrates to one. Averaging
with Equation~\eqref{eq:tree-law} yields Equation~\eqref{eq:source} and preserves
normalization. Differentiating the log of each finite positive tree partition
with respect to $\alpha_e$ gives its edge-inclusion probability, proving
Equation~\eqref{eq:gradient}. These are applications of standard incidence and
tree-ensemble identities.

\section{Source invariance under symmetry coupling}\label{app:symmetry}
Let $G$ be the compact group of proper rotations and permutations of identical
conditioning labels. Suppose $q_0$ is $G$-invariant, $X\sim q_0$, and a pairing
algorithm selects $g(X,Y)\in G$ using a target $Y$. If $H$ is an independent Haar
draw, then conditional on the orbit of $X$ and on the selected $g$, $HgX$ has
the uniform orbit distribution of $HX$, by Haar invariance. Integrating over the
unchanged source-orbit distribution recovers $q_0$. This argument permits a
non-Gaussian source and explains the shared augmentation used in the pairing.
It does not imply independence of source and target after pairing or justify
the independent-Gaussian velocity-to-score formula.

\section{Covariance controls}
For $R=\ell\exp(sZ-s^2/2)$ with $Z\sim N(0,1)$, $\EE R^2=\ell^2\exp(s^2)$.
A uniform direction has second moment $I/3$, so each edge has covariance
$vI=\ell^2\exp(s^2)I/3$. Let $A_T$ map edge coordinates to centered node
coordinates. Independence gives $C_T=A_T\operatorname{diag}(v_e)A_T^\top$.
Replacing the edge vectors by independent Gaussians of covariance $v_eI$ leaves
$C_T$ unchanged and, since all component means are zero, leaves $\EE_TC_T$
unchanged. The single-Gaussian control uses a finite, composition-seeded average
of these matrices. Averaging this matrix over within-species permutations preserves
positive definiteness on the centered subspace and yields species invariance.
The Gaussian is exactly normalized for its chosen covariance; equality to the
infinite tree-mixture covariance remains approximate.



\section{Prespecified source-utility pilot}\label{app:source-utility}
For each fixed composition and electronic condition $c$, retain the actual frozen
decoder kernel $K_\theta(y\mid x_0,c)$, including its midpoint integrator and final
noise. Changing only latent-tree affinities gives the standard importance identity
\begin{equation}
 J(\eta,c)=\mathbb{E}_{q_0K_\theta}
 \left[\frac{q_\eta(X_0\mid c)}{q_0(X_0\mid c)}R(Y,c)\right].
\end{equation}
The ratio is a source-space marginal ratio, not the marginal output-density ratio.
For $M$ iid cached draws, the leave-one-out centered estimate
$\frac{M}{M-1}\frac1M\sum_i w_i(R_i-\bar R)$ is unbiased for the improvement over
$q_0$ at a fixed $\eta$. Optimizing it on that same bank can overfit.

With common normalized edge kernels, the exact latent-tree divergence is
\begin{equation}
 D_T=\sum_{e}m_{\eta,e}(\log a_{\eta,e}-\log a_{0,e})
       -\log\tau(a_\eta)+\log\tau(a_0),
\end{equation}
where $m_{\eta,e}$ is the prior tree edge marginal. Deploy the mixture
$p_{\mathrm{mix}}(T)=(1-\epsilon)p_0(T)+\epsilon p_\eta(T)$ with
$\epsilon=\min(1,\delta/D_T)$, taking $\epsilon=1$ at zero divergence. Standard KL
convexity and data processing through $T\to X_0\to Y$ imply
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{\mathrm{mix}}(Y)\Vert p_0(Y))\leq\epsilon D_T\leq\delta.
\end{equation}
This controls the raw attempt distribution for a common decoder. It does not
certify output density, effective sample size, diversity, validity or equilibrium,
and does not apply to earlier experiments that changed decoder weights.

The prospective pilot fixes $\delta=0.25$ nats, unchanged lognormal edge width0.2,
and utility $0.8$ times graph support plus $0.2$ times geometric support, retaining
all failures. Twenty-four new qualified training-corpus compositions supply64
bank draws each. Twelve additional compositions are held out of source-head
fitting, with four per size bin8--16,17--28,29--40. All36 originate from the flow's
training corpus; this is not a global pretraining-disjoint test. The actual-utility,
within-composition shuffled-utility and coordinate-NLL heads have equal capacity
and30 training epochs, Adam learning rate0.003, gradient clipping5 and tree-KL
penalty0.01. Together with the fixed source, all four receive64 fresh draws per
held composition under each of two frozen decoders. The9216 planned attempts count
both bank preparation and validation. No result or useful learned increment is
claimed before completion and audit.
