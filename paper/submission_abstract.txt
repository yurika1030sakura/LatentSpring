LatentSpring: Physics-Informed Molecular Flow Matching from Atomic Composition

Generating three-dimensional molecules from atomic composition requires organizing atoms before their chemical connectivity is known. We introduce LatentSpring, a composition-conditioned flow-matching method with a harmonic source defined by latent spatial trees. The source uses atomic identities and covalent radii to sample correlated starting coordinates; averaging over trees gives an explicitly normalized density. A paired physical-training step then learns from local force-guided targets and transfers the update relative to a matched replay model. On 64 new compositions spanning 17–64 atoms, the harmonic source improves raw chemical-graph validity by 6.91 percentage points over an isotropic Gaussian source. The gain also appears on an independently initialized EGNN backbone. Component controls establish benefits from geometry self-conditioning and the paired physical update, which lowers energy and force under both eSEN and independent GFN2-xTB evaluation. A direct physical update of the same parameter norm raises energy, while replay subtraction lowers it, identifying a useful update direction beyond a change in step size. The final generator uses one network with 128 evaluations per sample, and all reported generation metrics use its unoptimized coordinates.
