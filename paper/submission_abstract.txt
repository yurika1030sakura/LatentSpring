LatentSpring: Harmonic Sources and Physical Corrections for Molecular Generation

Generating a molecule from atomic composition requires determining both its connectivity and three-dimensional coordinates. In this paper, we propose LatentSpring, which improves molecular generation through two designs. A normalized harmonic-mixture source uses random auxiliary trees and atomic length scales to organize the starting coordinates without known chemical bonds. A small equivariant network learns physical corrections to a frozen flow matching (FM) model's predicted endpoints from force feedback. On 64 unseen compositions, LatentSpring reaches 24.00% joint yield, the fraction of outputs that are graph-valid and have GFN2-xTB force RMS at most 5 eV/Å, compared with 8.75% for GAGA and 8.15% for EDM. A component study using one network evaluation per velocity prediction shows that the harmonic source adds 4.35 percentage points to joint yield when combined with physical correction. The correction also improves the flow matching model in each of three new training repetitions. Expressing its action in coordinate space allows a physical correction head trained using FM-generated trajectory states to act directly on diffusion noise predictions: transfer to GAGA improves joint yield by 16.54 points across those three repetitions, without retraining the correction. Generation uses neural evaluations, with no energy calls or quantum geometry optimization.
