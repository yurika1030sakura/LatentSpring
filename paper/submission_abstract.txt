LatentSpring: Physics-Informed Molecular Flow Matching from Atomic Composition

Generating molecular structures from atomic composition requires spatial organization to emerge without a supplied chemical graph. We introduce LatentSpring, a molecular flow-matching framework with a harmonic source that marginalizes over latent spatial dependence trees. Atom-dependent Gaussian edge displacements provide molecular length scales, while a matrix-tree ratio gives the normalized source density on centered coordinates. The resulting source integrates with an equivariant molecular flow without bond labels or an additional learned source network. On neutral-organic OMol25 compositions, matched continuations improve raw chemical-graph support over an isotropic Gaussian source by 5.86 percentage points on 12 development compositions, 8.83 points on 10 additional compositions, and 8.20 points on a subsequent frozen 24-composition test. A calibrated covariance-Gaussian comparison supports a contribution from mixture structure beyond the tested second-moment approximation. We further incorporate physical feedback during training: a paired parameter update reduces raw energy and force in both continuations under eSEN and independent GFN2-xTB readouts. Generation uses 128 primitive denoiser calls and requires no per-output energy selection or geometry optimization. A controlled molecular-rotor experiment also validates complete escorted-work correction of a specified local target. These results establish latent spatial dependence as a useful source design for composition-conditioned molecular flows and demonstrate a complementary route for incorporating physical feedback into the learned generator.

Keywords: molecular generation; flow matching; structured priors; physical feedback; nonequilibrium reweighting.
