LatentSpring: Physics-Informed Molecular Flow Matching from Atomic Composition

Generating a molecule from its atomic composition requires the model to infer spatial organization without a supplied chemical graph. We introduce LatentSpring, a flow-matching framework whose source distribution is a mixture of harmonic networks over latent trees. Gaussian edge displacements encode atomic length scales, and a matrix-tree expression gives the normalized density after the tree is marginalized. The source works with an equivariant molecular flow and requires no bond labels or learned source network. In matched OMol25 fine-tuning experiments, it improves raw chemical-graph support over an isotropic Gaussian source by 5.86, 8.83 and 8.20 percentage points on panels of 12, 10 and 24 neutral organic compositions, respectively. A calibrated Gaussian covariance control supports a role for mixture structure beyond second moments. We also introduce physical feedback through paired training targets, reducing raw energy and force in both model continuations under eSEN and independent GFN2-xTB evaluation. A separate molecular torsion experiment verifies complete escorted-work reweighting against numerically resolved local targets. LatentSpring combines these source and training choices in a single neural generator, using 128 denoiser evaluations per sample without energy-based selection or geometry optimization at inference.

Keywords: molecular generation; flow matching; structured priors; physical feedback; nonequilibrium reweighting.
