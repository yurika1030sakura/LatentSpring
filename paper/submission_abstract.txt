LatentSpring: Physics-Informed Molecular Flow Matching from Atomic Composition

Generating a molecule from its atomic composition requires deciding both how its atoms are arranged in space and which atoms are bonded. LatentSpring combines flow matching with a harmonic-mixture source and learned physical correction. We construct the source without a known bond graph: a random tree organizes Gaussian displacements whose scales depend on atomic radii. Averaging over trees gives a normalized distribution of correlated coordinates. A bounded equivariant head then learns from forces evaluated at the generator's provisional final geometries, while the backbone remains fixed. Expressing the correction as an endpoint change also lets it act on a diffusion model's noise prediction. We additionally study transferring physical feedback into the backbone weights. On 64 unseen compositions with 17–64 atoms, the harmonic source improves chemical-graph validity by 3.89 percentage points on average across five fine-tuning runs, with gains in four runs. On 16 further compositions, an 8,178-parameter force head increases the fraction of outputs that are both graph-valid and have GFN2-xTB force RMS at most 5 eV/Å\ from 38.87% to 46.88%. Under equal physical-supervision budgets on a separate EGNN backbone, the correction improves this joint yield by 7.03 points for FM and 6.64 points for GAGA on another 16 new compositions, with both fits improving in each case. The corrected flow uses 128 backbone and 64 small-head evaluations per output, without energy evaluations or geometry optimization during generation.
