LatentSpring: Transferable Physical Corrections for Molecular Generation from Atomic Composition

Generating a molecule from atomic composition leaves both its connectivity and three-dimensional geometry to be determined. LatentSpring combines a normalized harmonic-mixture source with learned physical correction. A random auxiliary tree organizes Gaussian coordinate displacements using atomic length scales, requiring no known chemical bonds. A small equivariant head learns force-driven changes to a frozen generator's predicted endpoints. Expressing these changes in coordinate space lets the same head act on flow velocities or diffusion noise predictions. On 64 new compositions and five independently trained EGNN fit pairs, physical correction raises the fraction of outputs that are graph-valid and have GFN2-xTB force RMS at most 5 eV/Å\ from 7.21% to 24.00% for FM and from 8.75% to 25.37% for GAGA. Three new fit pairs confirm improvement in every case. Transferring the FM-trained head verbatim to GAGA improves this joint yield by 16.54 percentage points across those three pairs, without head retraining or target-generator force labels. A separate source comparison improves graph validity by 3.89 points on average across five fine-tuning runs. An optional conditional hydrogen flow further improves physical geometry. Generation uses neural evaluations, with no energy calls or quantum geometry optimization.
