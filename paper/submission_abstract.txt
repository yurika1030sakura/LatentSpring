LatentSpring: Physics-Informed Molecular Flow Matching from Atomic Composition

Generating a molecule from its atomic composition requires deciding both how its atoms are arranged in space and which atoms are bonded. We introduce LatentSpring, a flow-matching method that starts from correlated coordinates rather than isotropic Gaussian noise. To construct these coordinates without a known bond graph, we sample a random tree over the atoms and draw Gaussian displacements along its edges, with scales set by atomic radii. Averaging over trees gives a normalized harmonic-mixture source. We then use an energy model's forces to build nearby training targets. Two copies of the generator are fine-tuned on the force-shifted and original generated structures, respectively; their parameter difference is added to the base model. A second implementation learns a compact velocity correction while keeping the backbone frozen. On 64 unseen compositions with 17–64 atoms, the harmonic source improves chemical-graph validity by 3.89 percentage points on average across five fine-tuning runs, with gains in four runs. The improvement also appears on an independently initialized EGNN. On 24 compositions, the paired physical update improves the fraction of outputs that are both graph-valid and below a residual-force threshold for both sources. At 5 eV/Å, this fraction is 47.59% for the updated harmonic model and 40.63% for the updated Gaussian model under independent GFN2-xTB evaluation. On a further 16 compositions, an 8,178-parameter force corrector increases joint yield from 38.87% to 46.88%. The paired update uses 128 backbone calls; the explicit corrector adds 64 small-head calls. Both generate without energy evaluations or geometry optimization.
