LatentSpring: Physics-Informed Molecular Flow Matching from Atomic Composition

Generating three-dimensional molecules from atomic composition requires organizing atoms before their chemical connectivity is known. We introduce LatentSpring, a composition-conditioned flow-matching method with a harmonic source defined by latent spatial trees. The source uses atomic identities and covalent radii to sample correlated starting coordinates; averaging over trees gives an explicitly normalized density. A paired physical-training step then learns from local force-guided targets and transfers the update relative to a matched replay model. On 64 new compositions spanning 17–64 atoms, the harmonic source improves raw chemical-graph validity by 6.91 percentage points over an isotropic Gaussian source. The gain also appears on an independently initialized EGNN backbone. On 24 compositions, paired physical training improves joint structural and force quality for both sources. With the same adaptation recipe, the harmonic source reaches 47.59% graph-valid yield at a 5 eV/Å force-RMS threshold, compared with 40.63% for Gaussian under independent GFN2-xTB evaluation. A same-norm control identifies the benefit of the replay-subtracted update direction. The final generator uses one network with 128 evaluations per sample, and all reported generation metrics use its unoptimized coordinates.
