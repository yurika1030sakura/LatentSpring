\title{BGFM-Native: Energy-Coupled Boltzmann Flow Matching with\\
Universal Neural Potentials for 3D Molecular Generation}

\author{Anonymous Authors\\
Under double-blind review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Fast 3D molecular flow and diffusion models generate diverse de novo
molecules, but their probability geometry is learned from empirical
molecular datasets rather than from a physical energy landscape.
Energy-based molecular generators recover an explicit scalar energy,
but sample with expensive Langevin or parallel-tempering chains and can
trade diversity for physical quality. We propose \textbf{BGFM}, an energy-coupled flow-matching generator for
de novo 3D molecular generation.  Its coordinate velocity is decomposed
into an amortized transport field and a conservative Boltzmann residual
drift distilled from OMol25-derived energies and forces~\citep{omol25}:
$ v_{\rm BGFM}=v_\theta+\alpha(t,T_{\rm drift})\,P_{\rm SE(3)}[-\beta_{\rm drift}\nabla_r\Delta E_\psi] $.
Training couples this vector field to the external energy landscape
through late-time force-score consistency, within-parent density-energy
variance, and a finite-cloud Local Boltzmann bridge.  BGFM also learns a
composition-conditioned residual strain head from the same teacher and
uses it inside a short coordinate-only corrector that is unrolled during
training. The resulting model is designed to combine the speed and
diversity of flow matching with the low-strain sampling behavior of
energy-based models. Because OMol25 labels enter training, we separate
mechanistic OMol25 alignment from external validation: the headline
experiments use standard QM9 and GEOM-Drugs~\citep{geom} generation
metrics, independent
GFN2-xTB~\citep{gfn2xtb}, MMFF~\citep{mmff}, and DFT relaxation oracles,
quality--diversity--compute Pareto curves with the Vendi
score~\citep{friedman2023vendi}, and shuffled-label negative controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Three-dimensional molecular generators are increasingly used as the
front end of simulation and discovery pipelines. A useful generated
structure must not only be chemically valid; it should already be close
to a physically plausible region of an energy landscape, otherwise
substantial relaxation or molecular dynamics is required before the
sample can be used. Current models expose a tradeoff. Flow-matching and
diffusion models amortize sampling and produce diverse molecules, but
standard training matches empirical data distributions and does not, by
construction, enforce Boltzmann relative probabilities under a
quantum-level energy model. Energy-based molecular generators learn an
explicit scalar potential and can rank, filter, and steer samples, but
sampling from a learned energy generally requires many inference-time
steps.

BGFM addresses this gap by treating a universal neural potential as an
external physical teacher for the generative vector field itself.  We
instantiate the amortized transport component with a FlowMol3-style
de novo proposal~\citep{flowmol3} over atom types, charges, and coordinates,
but the coordinate velocity used for training and sampling is energy-coupled:
it adds a learned conservative residual drift calibrated from OMol25.
The first calibration term aligns the flow-implied coordinate score with
neural-potential forces near the data endpoint. The second estimates a
coordinate log-density with a FFJORD integral and enforces that, within a
cloud of perturbations of the same parent molecule,
\begin{equation}
    \log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r \mid c) + \beta_{\rm phys} E_{\rm NP}(r,c)
\end{equation}
varies as little as possible, where $\beta_{\rm phys} = 1/(k_B T_0)$ is the
physical inverse temperature (see ``Temperature conventions'' in
\S\ref{eq:conditional-boltzmann}'s preamble). This is a local
conditional Boltzmann condition: lower-energy perturbations of the same
molecular identity should receive higher probability. BGFM further trains a small scalar
energy head to approximate the external neural-potential energy and
force, then uses this head for a short learned-energy Langevin corrector
that refines coordinates while holding the sampled molecular identity
fixed.

The central comparison is with EBMol-style energy-based generation~\citep{ebmol}.
EBMol learns a persistent scalar energy and samples with Mirror-Langevin
dynamics and parallel tempering. BGFM takes the complementary route:
keep the fast flow proposal, calibrate it with external physical labels,
and use an energy-coupled flow field plus a short learned-energy corrector rather than a long MCMC chain.
Therefore, our primary evaluation is not just validity or an OMol25
self-correlation. We evaluate whether BGFM improves the
physical-quality--diversity--compute frontier under independent
relaxation oracles.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Energy-coupled equivariant molecular vector field.}
    We design an SE(3)-equivariant generative velocity field that
    decomposes into an amortized transport component and a
    conservative late-time Boltzmann drift distilled from a universal
    neural potential,
    $v_{\rm BGFM} = v_\theta + \alpha(t,T_{\rm drift})\,P_{\rm SE(3)}[-\beta_{\rm drift}\nabla_r \Delta E_\psi]$.
    The energy gradient enters the generator's dynamics, not a
    post-processing step.
    \item \textbf{Local Boltzmann bridge with density-energy
    calibration.} We train the position log-density to match a finite-
    cloud Boltzmann target induced by OMol25 perturbation energies via
    an explicit per-parent KL bridge,
    $\mathrm{KL}(w_m\,\|\,q^{(m)}_\theta)$ with
    $w_m^{(k)}\propto e^{-\beta_{\rm bridge}(s)\, E_m^{(k)}}$ and
    $q^{(m,k)}_\theta\propto e^{\log p_\theta(r_m^{(k)}|c_m)}$, in
    parallel with the within-parent density-energy variance form. Here
    $\beta_{\rm bridge}(s) = 1/(k_B T_{\rm bridge}(s))$ is the
    \emph{tempered} training inverse temperature, annealed to the
    physical anchor $\beta_{\rm phys}$ by end of training. Both
    objectives are invariant to the unknown per-composition partition
    function and reuse the same FFJORD log-density evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Corrector-in-the-loop amortized physical sampler.}
    A composition-conditioned residual strain scorer
    $\hat E_\psi(r,c) = b_\phi(c) + \Delta E_\psi(r,c)$ is calibrated to
    OMol25 force/energy. We unroll a random short Langevin corrector
    on this learned energy at training time with a stability penalty,
    so the sampler obtains EBM-like physical refinement without long
    Langevin or parallel-tempering chains and without a separate
    post-processing stage.
    \item \textbf{Quality--diversity--compute evaluation.}
    Headline metrics use independent oracles (GFN2-xTB and MMFF
    relaxation, DFT subset) rather than OMol25 self-scores; we report
    a Pareto frontier across model variants alongside ablations and
    negative controls that isolate the contribution of each module.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{3D molecular flow and diffusion.}
EDM~\citep{edm}, GeoLDM~\citep{geoldm}, MiDi~\citep{midi},
GeoBFN~\citep{geobfn}, SemlaFlow~\citep{semlaflow},
FlowMol~\citep{flowmol}, and FlowMol3~\citep{flowmol3} learn
transport or denoising maps for molecular atom types and coordinates.
GeoDiff~\citep{geodiff} targets conditional conformer generation. The
underlying generative families --- denoising diffusion~\citep{ddpm},
score SDE~\citep{scoresde}, neural ODEs and FFJORD-style flow
density~\citep{neuralode, ffjord}, and flow matching~\citep{flowmatching}
--- supply the velocity / score primitives we reuse. FlowMol3 is the
closest same-family baseline: an open-source multi-modal flow-matching model
for de novo all-atom small-molecule generation that improves FlowMol via
self-conditioning, fake atoms, and train-time geometry distortion without
changing the underlying GNN or flow formulation.  BGFM does not replace
the discrete molecular identity generator and does not enlarge the GNN
backbone; instead, it changes the \emph{coordinate dynamics} into an
energy-coupled flow field. Concretely, the continuous coordinate vector
field is modified into an energy-coupled vector field whose conservative
component is distilled from a universal neural potential, while the
amortized molecular identity proposal over atom types and charges is
inherited from FlowMol3. The change is to the generative dynamics
themselves, not to a post-hoc calibration layer applied to the
backbone's outputs.

\paragraph{Energy-based molecular generation.}
EBMol~\citep{ebmol} learns a time-unconditional atom-additive scalar potential and
samples it with Mirror-Langevin dynamics plus parallel tempering. It is
therefore the direct competitor for physically motivated 3D generation.
BGFM differs in two ways. First, BGFM uses an external universal neural
potential as the physical teacher, whereas EBMol learns an energy from
geometry data without direct energy supervision. Second, BGFM retains an
amortized flow proposal and uses a short corrector, whereas EBMol relies
on inference-time energy sampling.

\paragraph{Flexible conformer ensemble generation.}
FlexiFlow~\citep{flexiflow} extends flow matching to jointly sample molecular graphs and
multiple conformations and reports strong QM9/GEOM-Drugs performance.
FlexiFlow is a required standard-generation baseline, especially for
conformer-ensemble metrics. Its goal is complementary to BGFM:
FlexiFlow focuses on molecular/conformer ensemble coverage, while
BGFM focuses on energy-coupled coordinate dynamics that produce
low-strain de novo geometries without long inference-time energy
chains.

\paragraph{Boltzmann generators and energy-based flow matching.}
Boltzmann Generators~\citep{boltzmanngen},
equivariant flow matching for Boltzmann sampling~\citep{equivariantfm},
and iEFM~\citep{iefm} study sampling from known or learned unnormalized densities,
often for fixed systems or continuous toy/physical potentials. iEFM is
especially relevant theoretically because it trains CNFs from energy
function evaluations, but its evaluation setting is fixed continuous
Boltzmann sampling rather than mixed discrete-continuous de novo molecule
generation.

\paragraph{Universal neural potentials.}
OMol25~\citep{omol25} provides large-scale DFT data and neural-potential models over a
broad chemistry space. BGFM uses such a neural potential not as a
post-hoc relaxer but as a training-time teacher for forces, energies,
and energy-head calibration.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Temperature conventions.}
Throughout this paper we use a strict two-temperature convention.
The \emph{physical} temperature is $T_0 = 298.15\,\mathrm{K}$ and the
corresponding physical Boltzmann constant times temperature is
$k_B T_0 = 0.02569\,\mathrm{eV} = 0.593\,\mathrm{kcal/mol}$,
so $\beta_{\rm phys} = 1/(k_B T_0) \approx 38.93\,\mathrm{eV}^{-1}$.
This is the unique definition of ``room temperature'' used to define the
target Boltzmann law and the reported physical-quality numbers (the
xTB/MMFF/DFT $\Delta E$ tables and BGFM-Native-det).
During training, the Boltzmann-bridge loss uses an \emph{effective}
$k_B T_{\rm bridge}(s)$ that may be tempered or annealed for numerical
stability of the variance / KL objective; whenever
$k_B T_{\rm bridge} > k_B T_0$ we call this configuration the
\emph{tempered Boltzmann bridge} and never ``room temperature''. We
recommend a log-linear anneal $0.25 \to 0.10 \to 0.05 \to 0.02569\,\mathrm{eV}$.
The corrector is reported in two variants (\S\ref{sec:method:corrector}):
a \emph{deterministic} descent at $T_0$ and a \emph{physical-$300\,\mathrm{K}$
noisy Langevin} diagnostic that uses $\beta_{\rm phys}$ for its noise scale.
All ``$kT$'', ``$T_0$'', ``$298$'', and ``tempered'' usage in the paper is
labelled in one of these three categories. Any $kT$ used in training that
differs from $k_B T_0$ is the \emph{effective / tempered training
temperature}, not physical.

Let a molecular configuration be $x=(c,r)$, where $c$ denotes molecular
identity variables (atom count, atom types, and charges) and
$r\in\mathbb R^{3N}$ denotes coordinates. The external neural potential
returns an energy $E_{\rm NP}(r,c)$ and forces
$F_{\rm NP}(r,c)=-\nabla_r E_{\rm NP}(r,c)$. BGFM does not define a
global canonical ensemble over arbitrary molecular identities. Instead,
for a fixed identity $c$, it targets the conditional coordinate
Boltzmann law
\begin{equation}
    p_B(r\mid c,T_0)=\frac{1}{Z_c(T_0)}\exp[-\beta_{\rm phys} E_{\rm NP}(r,c)],
    \qquad \beta_{\rm phys}=1/(k_B T_0)\;[\text{physical}].
    \label{eq:conditional-boltzmann}
\end{equation}
This target is well-defined because $c$ is fixed; cross-composition
probabilities are left to the empirical proposal distribution and are
not interpreted as thermodynamic weights.

\subsection{Energy-coupled flow proposal}

BGFM uses a FlowMol3-style proposal for molecular identity variables, but
the coordinate velocity is an explicitly energy-coupled vector field.  Let
$v_\theta(x_t,t)$ be the amortized transport field from the flow proposal and
let $\Delta E_\psi(r,c)$ be a composition-conditioned residual strain energy
head.  The coordinate velocity used by BGFM is
\begin{equation}
    v_{\rm BGFM}(x_t,t)
    = v_\theta(x_t,t) + \alpha(t, T_{\rm drift})\,P_{\rm SE(3)}\bigl[-\,\beta_{\rm drift}\,\nabla_r\Delta E_\psi(r_t,c)\bigr],
    \qquad \beta_{\rm drift}=1/(k_B T_{\rm drift}),
    \label{eq:energy-coupled-vf}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\rm SE(3)}$ removes per-graph center-of-mass drift and
$\alpha(t, T_{\rm drift})$ is a late-time onset schedule. The explicit
$\beta_{\rm drift}$ makes the role of temperature in the drift unambiguous
and matches Eq.~\eqref{eq:bgfm-velocity}. Atom types and charges still use
the categorical flow/CTMC component of the proposal.  The base flow-matching
loss is applied to the total velocity,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm FM}=\mathbb E\left[\|v_{\rm BGFM}(x_t,t)-u_t\|^2 +
    \mathcal L_{\rm disc}\right].
\end{equation}
This turns the external energy from a post-hoc correction signal into part of
the generative dynamics while retaining amortized flow sampling.  In ablations
we also report the plain-flow proposal $v_\theta$ and a post-hoc-only variant.

\subsection{Late-time force-score consistency}

For the conditional Boltzmann law in Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-boltzmann},
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_r \log p_B(r\mid c,T_0)=\beta_{\rm phys} F_{\rm NP}(r,c)
    \qquad [\text{physical}].
\end{equation}
Under the linear Gaussian interpolant, the coordinate score at time $t$
can be estimated from the flow velocity by
\begin{equation}
    s_\theta(x_t,t)=\frac{t\,v_\theta(x_t,t)-x_t}{(1-t)\sigma_0^2}.
\end{equation}
BGFM evaluates this score at late but finite times
$t\in\mathcal T$ and aligns it with the endpoint neural-potential force:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm force}=\mathbb E_{t\in\mathcal T}
    \left[1-\cos\left(s_\theta(x_t,t),\,\beta_{\rm phys} F_{\rm NP}(r_1,c)\right)\right]
    \qquad [\text{physical}; \cos \text{ is } \beta\text{-invariant}].
    \label{eq:force-loss}
\end{equation}
This is a late-time regularizer, not an exact identity at arbitrary
intermediate $x_t$. It avoids online neural-potential calls during every
training step by using precomputed endpoint forces.

\subsection{Density-energy variance}

The most direct probability calibration is that
$\log p_B(r\mid c,T)+\beta_{\rm bridge}(s) E_{\rm NP}(r,c)$ equals a
constant depending only on $c$ and $T$. BGFM estimates the coordinate
log-density $\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r\mid c)$ with a FFJORD-style
divergence integral through the coordinate velocity field. For each
parent molecule $m$, we precompute a cloud of perturbations
$\{r_m^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^K$ and neural-potential energies. The loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm dens}=\mathbb E_m\operatorname{Var}_{k}
    \left[
        \log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(k)}\mid c_m)
        +\beta_{\rm bridge}(s)\, E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)},c_m)
    \right],
    \label{eq:density-energy}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\rm bridge}(s)$ is the (possibly tempered) training
inverse temperature that anneals to $\beta_{\rm phys}$ by end of
training (see ``Temperature conventions'').
Because the variance is taken within the same parent molecule, the
unknown offset $-\log Z_{c_m}(T)$ cancels. We optionally add a
composition-conditioned scalar offset $b_\phi(c)$ as an anchor, but do
not interpret it as a supervised thermodynamic partition-function
estimator.

\subsection{Calibrated composition-conditioned strain scorer}
\label{sec:method:head}

To obtain an explicit inference-time energy without running OMol25 at
sampling time, BGFM trains a scalar head. Absolute OMol25 energies are
dominated by composition and atom count, so a direct regression head
would mostly learn molecular size rather than coordinate-dependent
strain. We therefore decompose the head into a
\emph{composition baseline} $b_\phi(c)$ and a
\emph{coordinate-dependent strain term} $\Delta E_\psi(r, c)$:
\begin{equation}
    \hat E_\psi(r, c) \,=\, b_\phi(c) \,+\, \Delta E_\psi(r, c),
    \label{eq:head-decomp}
\end{equation}
and train against OMol25 with the residual loss
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm head}
    \,=\, \bigl| \Delta E_\psi(r, c) - \bigl(E_{\rm NP}(r, c) - b_\phi(c)\bigr) \bigr|
    \,+\, \lambda_F\,\bigl\|\!-\nabla_r \Delta E_\psi(r, c) - F_{\rm NP}(r, c)\bigr\|_2^2,
    \label{eq:head-loss}
\end{equation}
where $b_\phi$ is a small invariant network on the molecular
composition vector (atom-type histogram + total charge) and
$\Delta E_\psi$ is the per-graph readout of the head from the
flow-proposal scalar features. We use the head as a
composition-conditioned strain scorer for ranking and refinement; it is
not a free energy and is not a thermodynamic absolute formation energy.

\subsection{Short learned-energy corrector}
\label{sec:method:corrector}

After the flow proposal samples $(c, r^0)$, BGFM optionally refines only
coordinates using the calibrated head. We report two variants:

\paragraph{Deterministic descent corrector (BGFM-Native-det).}
\begin{equation}
    r^{j+1} \,=\, \Pi\bigl[\,r^j + \eta_j\bigl(-\nabla_r \Delta E_\psi(r^j, c)\bigr)\bigr],
    \qquad j = 0, \ldots, J-1.
    \label{eq:corrector-det}
\end{equation}
This is the noise-free ($\epsilon_j = 0$) descent that produces our
\emph{main physical-quality numbers} (xTB/MMFF $\Delta E$). It is not
intended as a Boltzmann sampler; it is a short, learned, deterministic
relaxation, comparable to a few steps of MMFF or xTB local optimization
but using only the in-model strain head.

\paragraph{Room-temperature noisy Langevin corrector (BGFM-Native-300K).}
\begin{equation}
    r^{j+1} \,=\, \Pi\bigl[\,r^j + \eta_j\bigl(-\nabla_r \Delta E_\psi(r^j, c)\bigr)
    + \sqrt{2 \eta_j / \beta_{\rm phys}}\,\epsilon_j\,\bigr],
    \qquad \epsilon_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),
    \label{eq:corrector-langevin}
\end{equation}
with $\beta_{\rm phys} = 1/(k_B T_0)$ at $T_0 = 298.15\,\mathrm{K}$ [physical].
This is the mechanistic Boltzmann-ensemble diagnostic and is what we use
when we wish to test conditional-Boltzmann calibration empirically;
it is \emph{not} the default reported number.
Here $J \in \{0, 20, 50, 100\}$ in our compute sweep, $\Pi$ removes
center-of-mass drift, and the discrete identity $c$ --- atom counts,
types, and charges --- is held fixed throughout refinement. Per sampled
molecule we report flow NFEs, energy-head NFEs, external oracle calls
(zero by default), wall-clock time, and outcome. Samples whose
post-corrector geometry produces invalid OpenBabel/RDKit bond inference,
steric clash above a threshold, or downstream relaxation failure are
counted as \emph{failures} and remain in the population averages of
the all-samples protocol; we never silently drop them. Unless a
Metropolis correction is explicitly enabled and reported, neither
corrector variant is an exact MCMC sampler.

\subsection{Energy-calibrated vector field}
\label{sec:method:drift}

Modules 1--4 above use the energy head only at sampling time. To turn
BGFM into a genuine \emph{energy-calibrated} flow rather than a flow
with a post-hoc relax step, we additionally inject the head's
coordinate gradient into the velocity field itself, with an explicit
inverse-temperature factor $\beta_{\rm drift} = 1/(k_B T_{\rm drift})$
[physical at default]:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\rm BGFM}(x_t, t) \,=\, v_\theta(x_t, t)
    \,+\, \alpha(t, T_{\rm drift})\,P_{\rm SE(3)}\!\bigl[\,-\,\beta_{\rm drift}\,\nabla_r\,\Delta E_\psi(r_t, c)\bigr],
    \label{eq:bgfm-velocity}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha(t, T_{\rm drift})$ is a smooth late-time onset schedule
(zero on $[0, t_{\rm on}]$, growing on $[t_{\rm on}, 1]$ with a quadratic
power) and $P_{\rm SE(3)}$ removes the per-graph centroid drift so the
correction respects translation equivariance; SO(3) equivariance is
inherited from the strain term being a rotation-invariant scalar of
pairwise distances. The drift inverse temperature $\beta_{\rm drift}$
appears \emph{explicitly} in the velocity so the role of temperature in
the generative dynamics is unambiguous; in the main configuration
$k_B T_{\rm drift} = k_B T_0 = 0.02569\,\mathrm{eV}$ [physical], and
the temperature ablation in \S\ref{sec:exp:temperature} sweeps it.
We add a stability regularizer
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm drift} \,=\, \mathbb E \bigl\|\alpha(t, T_{\rm drift})\,P_{\rm SE(3)}\!\bigl[-\beta_{\rm drift}\,\nabla_r\,\Delta E_\psi(r_t, c)\bigr]\bigr\|^2,
    \label{eq:drift-loss}
\end{equation}
so the drift contribution remains the same order of magnitude as the
base velocity. The norm penalty alone, however, does not certify the
\emph{direction} of the drift: a backbone that learned to cancel the
drift contribution would still satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift-loss} at the
optimum. To make the drift a genuinely physical \emph{descent} direction
on the learned strain, we add a hinge \emph{monotonicity} loss. Writing
$d_t = P_{\rm SE(3)}[-\beta_{\rm drift}\,\nabla_r\,\Delta E_\psi(r_t, c)]$
for the conservative drift component, the loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm mono} \,=\, \mathbb E\,\max\!\bigl(0,\;
        \Delta E_\psi(r_t + \epsilon\,d_t,\,c)
        \,-\, \Delta E_\psi(r_t, c)
        \,+\, m\bigr),
    \label{eq:drift-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
with step size $\epsilon$ matched to the late-time velocity scale and
margin $m \ge 0$. The role of $m$ is to interpolate between two
regimes: $m = 0$ asks only that a step along $d_t$ not \emph{raise} the
learned strain (so a flat optimum where $d_t \approx 0$ satisfies the
hinge); $m > 0$ asks that the strain strictly decrease by at least $m$
along $d_t$, which forbids the flat optimum and forces $d_t$ to be a
non-trivial descent direction whenever $\Delta E_\psi$ has a non-zero
gradient at $r_t$. We default to $m = 0$ in the main configuration
because the early-training strain head is noisy and a strict-descent
margin amplifies that noise into instability; we sweep $m \in
\{0, 0.05, 0.1\}\,\mathrm{eV}$ in the ablation table. With either
margin, this hinge certifies that the conservative component, when
actually applied, does not raise the learned strain on average and thus
distinguishes a physical Boltzmann drift from a generic norm-controlled
perturbation. At inference, the sampler can opt in to either the plain
flow $v_\theta$ or the calibrated field $v_{\rm BGFM}$; we report both
in the experimental ablations.

\subsection{Corrector-aware training}
\label{sec:method:corrector-aware}

The post-hoc Langevin corrector (\S\ref{sec:method:corrector}) can be
criticized as a generic post-processing step accessible to any
amortized baseline. To close that gap, we train the energy head and
the flow jointly with a random short unroll of the corrector:
sample $J \sim {\rm Uniform}\{0, 1, \ldots, J_{\rm max}\}$ with
$J_{\rm max} = 3$, run $J$ Langevin steps from the data endpoint
under $\hat E_\psi$, and add a stability penalty
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm stab} \,=\, \mathbb E_{\rm atom}\bigl[\max\!\bigl(0,\,\|r' - r_1\|^2 - \delta_{\max}^2\bigr)\bigr]
    \,+\, \mathbb E_{\rm graph}\bigl[\max\!\bigl(0,\, \hat E_\psi(r', c) - \hat E_\psi(r_1, c)\bigr)\bigr],
    \label{eq:corrector-aware-loss}
\end{equation}
where $r'$ is the unrolled geometry and $\delta_{\max}$ caps the
per-atom displacement. This makes the corrector an in-the-loop
training signal rather than a sampling-only procedure, at a cost of
roughly $J_{\rm max}$ additional energy-head forward and backward
passes per batch.

\subsection{Local Boltzmann bridge}
\label{sec:method:bridge}

The density-energy variance objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-energy}
controls only the second moment of the residual across each parent's
perturbation cloud. To turn that into a direct distribution-matching
objective with a well-defined target, we add a Local Boltzmann bridge
loss. For each parent molecule $m$ with $K$ perturbations, define the
neural-potential-induced cloud-restricted Boltzmann target
\begin{equation}
    w_m^{(k)} \,=\, \frac{\exp\!\bigl[-\beta_{\rm bridge}(s)\, E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)}, c_m)\bigr]}
                         {\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp\!\bigl[-\beta_{\rm bridge}(s)\, E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(j)}, c_m)\bigr]},
    \qquad \beta_{\rm bridge}(s)=1/(k_B T_{\rm bridge}(s))\;[\text{tempered, anneals to } \beta_{\rm phys}],
    \label{eq:bridge-target}
\end{equation}
and the model's cloud-restricted distribution
\begin{equation}
    q^{(m,k)}_\theta \,=\, \frac{\exp\!\bigl[\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(k)}\mid c_m)\bigr]}
                                {\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp\!\bigl[\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(j)}\mid c_m)\bigr]}.
    \label{eq:bridge-model}
\end{equation}
The unknown partition function $-\log Z_{c_m}(T)$ enters both numerator
and denominator equally inside each softmax and cancels exactly; the
bridge loss is therefore well-defined despite $\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}$
being only known up to a per-parent constant in practice. We optimize
the per-parent forward KL,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm bridge}
    \,=\, \mathbb E_m\,\mathrm{KL}\!\bigl(w_m \,\big\|\, q^{(m)}_\theta\bigr)
    \,=\, \mathbb E_m\,\sum_{k=1}^{K} w_m^{(k)}\!\left(\log w_m^{(k)} - \log q^{(m,k)}_\theta\right).
    \label{eq:bridge-loss}
\end{equation}
In the small-fluctuation limit where the cloud residual
$\log p_\theta^{\rm pos} + \beta_{\rm bridge}(s)\, E_{\rm NP}$ is
approximately constant across $k$, this reduces to half the within-parent
variance in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-energy} plus higher-order terms, so the two
objectives are theoretically consistent. In practice the bridge form is
more numerically stable when the cloud is large or when the residual is
not yet calibrated, and it is the form reviewers more readily recognize
as an explicit distribution-matching algorithm. The bridge reuses the
single FFJORD log-density evaluation already consumed by
$\mathcal L_{\rm dens}$ and thus carries no additional integration cost.

\paragraph{Symmetric variant.}
By default, $\mathcal L_{\rm bridge}$ uses the forward
$\mathrm{KL}(w_m\,\|\,q^{(m)}_\theta)$, which is mode-covering: the model
is encouraged to place mass on every low-energy perturbation that
OMol25 marks as probable. An alternative, made available as a config
knob, is the symmetric Jensen-Shannon-style form
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\rm bridge}^{\rm sym}
    \,=\, \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb E_m\bigl[
        \mathrm{KL}(w_m\,\|\,q^{(m)}_\theta) +
        \mathrm{KL}(q^{(m)}_\theta\,\|\,w_m)\bigr],
    \label{eq:bridge-sym}
\end{equation}
which adds a mode-seeking term that penalizes the model for placing
mass on cloud entries that OMol25 considers high-energy. We default to
forward KL because the natural failure mode of an undertrained model is
to assign uniform mass over the cloud (which forward KL strongly
penalizes for low-energy targets); the symmetric variant is reported in
the bridge ablation column of \S\ref{sec:exp:ablation}.

\subsection{Training objective}

The full training loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L \,=\, \mathcal L_{\rm FM}
    \,+\, \lambda_1 \mathcal L_{\rm force}
    \,+\, \lambda_2 \mathcal L_{\rm dens}
    \,+\, \lambda_3 \mathcal L_{\rm anchor}
    \,+\, \lambda_4 \mathcal L_{\rm head}
    \,+\, \lambda_5 \mathcal L_{\rm drift}
    \,+\, \lambda_6 \mathcal L_{\rm stab}
    \,+\, \lambda_7 \mathcal L_{\rm bridge}
    \,+\, \lambda_8 \mathcal L_{\rm mono}.
    \label{eq:total-loss}
\end{equation}
All auxiliary weights use warmup/ramp schedules. OMol25 is not called
in the main training loop: endpoint forces are stored in the dataset
and perturbation energies are precomputed offline.

\subsection{Joint discrete-continuous density}
\label{sec:method:joint}

BGFM exposes an optional \emph{joint density} mode that augments the
coordinate FFJORD with the model's discrete log-probability over atom
counts $N$, atom types $a$, and per-atom formal charges $c$:
\begin{equation}
    \log p_\theta(x \mid T)
    \,=\,
    \underbrace{\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r \mid c, T)}_{\text{FFJORD on coords}}
    \,+\,
    \underbrace{\log p_\theta^N(N) + \sum_{i=1}^{N}\!\bigl[\log p_\theta^a(a_i) + \log p_\theta^c(c_i)\bigr]}_{=\,\log p_\theta(c) \text{ (discrete CTMC)}}.
    \label{eq:joint-density}
\end{equation}
When enabled, $\log p_\theta(x \mid T)$ replaces $\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}$
inside both the density-energy variance loss
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:density-energy}) and the Local Boltzmann bridge target
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge-model}). Because all $K$ perturbations of a parent
molecule $m$ share atom counts, types, and charges, the discrete term
contributes the same per-parent constant to every cloud entry and
therefore drops out of both the within-parent variance and the
per-parent softmax. The joint-density mode is consequently
\emph{invariant} for the parent-restricted objectives that we use as
calibration signals, by construction. Its value is diagnostic: tracking
the discrete component lets us audit whether the discrete generator is
also implicitly Boltzmann-calibrated across compositions (a stricter
condition than this paper claims) and is enabled in the
$\lambda_{\rm joint}$ ablation column of \S\ref{sec:exp:ablation}. The
headline experimental numbers in this paper use the conditional-
coordinate variant ($\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}$ only).


\paragraph{Algorithmic form.}
\textbf{Training.} For each minibatch, sample a flow time $t$ and construct
$x_t$ on the conditional path.  Evaluate $v_{\rm BGFM}$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy-coupled-vf}, compute the base flow-matching loss, the
late-time force-score loss, FFJORD log-densities on perturbation clouds, the
variance and bridge losses, residual strain-head calibration, and a random
$0$--$J_{\max}$ corrector unroll.  Update all parameters with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:total-loss}.  \textbf{Sampling.} Integrate the learned
energy-coupled vector field from the prior.  Optionally run the same learned
coordinate-only corrector for $J\in\{0,20,50,100\}$ steps; no external OMol25
call is used in default sampling.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-cloud Boltzmann ratios]
For a fixed molecular identity $c$, if
$\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(k)}\mid c)+\beta_{\rm phys} E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)},c)$ is
constant over the perturbation cloud (in the limit $T_{\rm bridge} \to T_0$),
then for any perturbations $i,j$,
\begin{equation}
    \log\frac{p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(i)}\mid c)}
    {p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(j)}\mid c)}
    =-\beta_{\rm phys}\left(E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(i)},c)-E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(j)},c)\right).
\end{equation}
Thus, in the limit $T_{\rm bridge} \to T_0$, the variance and bridge
objectives enforce local conditional physical Boltzmann relative
probabilities without requiring $Z_c(T_0)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint force-score condition]
Under the conditional coordinate Boltzmann target in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conditional-boltzmann}, the coordinate score satisfies
$\nabla_r\log p_B(r\mid c,T_0)=\beta_{\rm phys} F_{\rm NP}(r,c)$.
Aligning the flow-implied late-time score of the total energy-coupled
field with $\beta_{\rm phys} F_{\rm NP}$ is therefore a local necessary
condition for physical Boltzmann calibration near the data endpoint.
\end{proposition}

\section{Experiments}

\paragraph{Experimental questions.}
We design experiments to answer six questions. First, does BGFM preserve
standard de novo 3D generation quality? Second, does it improve physical
quality under independent evaluators rather than only under the OMol25
labels used for training? Third, does it achieve a better
quality-diversity-compute frontier than EBMol-style energy sampling?
Fourth, does the proposed objective actually produce local conditional
Boltzmann alignment? Fifth, do the gains require correct physical
labels rather than arbitrary regularization? Sixth, does the calibrated
energy head rank and filter molecular strain better than existing energy
scorers?

\paragraph{Datasets and baselines.}
We evaluate on QM9, GEOM-Drugs, and held-out OMol25 chemistry slices.
We report each baseline along with its post-processing variant, so the
calibrated-corrector contribution of BGFM is not confounded with
generic post-hoc refinement. Specifically, our standard-generation
baselines include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the official FlowMol3~\citep{flowmol3} checkpoint with reported numbers and a re-run on our hardware;
    \item a \emph{same-backbone FM baseline}: an identical FlowMol3-style architecture trained on the same OMol25 split, without any BGFM regularization or corrector;
    \item FlowMol3/FM \emph{plus the same corrector budget} as BGFM, where the corrector uses an external xTB or OMol25 oracle instead of $\hat E_\psi$;
    \item FlowMol3/FM \emph{plus an xTB / OMol25 re-ranking head} that filters and re-orders the same sample pool;
    \item EDM, GeoLDM, GeoBFN, SemlaFlow, FlexiFlow, and EBMol with reported numbers, run under matched NFE budgets where possible.
\end{itemize}
For BGFM we report the full method and its module ablations: flow-only,
force-only, density-only, head-only, BGFM w/o corrector, and full BGFM.
These splits let us answer whether BGFM's gains come from the
calibrated corrector or from post-processing accessible to any
amortized baseline.

\subsection{Quality-diversity-compute Pareto}

We sweep flow NFEs and corrector steps $J\in\{0,20,50,100\}$ and compare
to EBMol's compute-scaled samplers. The key metric is the number of
valid, unique, low-strain samples per GPU-hour:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{VULS}_\tau=\frac{\#\{\text{valid, unique samples with }\Delta E_{\rm xTB}<\tau\}}{\text{GPU-hour}}.
\end{equation}
This evaluation targets the main BGFM hypothesis: a fast amortized flow
proposal plus a short calibrated corrector should dominate long
energy-sampling chains on physical quality, diversity, and cost.

\section{Limitations}

BGFM inherits OMol25's coverage and accuracy limitations. The main
density loss is conditional-coordinate unless the optional discrete
joint-density extension is explicitly enabled. The short corrector is a
learned-energy refinement, not an exact MCMC sampler. The energy head is
a calibrated scorer distilled from neural-potential energies and forces,
not a free-energy estimator. These limitations are evaluated with
independent oracles and shuffled-label controls rather than hidden.

\section{Conclusion}

BGFM is designed to bridge two strengths: amortized de novo flow
sampling and physically meaningful energy landscapes. By calibrating a
fast molecular flow proposal with an external universal neural potential
and using a short learned-energy corrector, BGFM targets low-strain,
diverse, computationally efficient 3D molecule generation. The decisive
empirical question is not whether BGFM improves an OMol25 self-metric,
but whether it improves independent physical quality and the
quality-diversity-compute frontier against strong flow and energy-based
baselines.
