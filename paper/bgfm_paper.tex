\begin{abstract}
Modern de novo 3D molecular generators can produce valid, data-like
structures, but their learned coordinate densities are not explicitly tied
to a physically meaningful energy landscape. As a result, generated
geometries may require substantial downstream relaxation before use in
simulation or screening. We propose \emph{Boltzmann-Regularized Flow
Matching} (BGFM), a training-time physical regularization framework for 3D
molecular flow matching. BGFM keeps a FlowMol3-style de novo generator as
the backbone, but uses energies and forces from a universal neural
potential, OMol25, to align the model's coordinate density with local
conditional Boltzmann relative probabilities. For a fixed molecular
identity $c$ and coordinates $r$, OMol25 induces the target
\[
    p_B(r\mid c,T)=Z_c(T)^{-1}\exp[-E_{\rm NP}(r,c)/kT].
\]
BGFM does not claim to sample an exact global Boltzmann distribution over
arbitrary atom counts, compositions, and charges. Instead, it regularizes
the position-only conditional density $p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r\mid c)$ using
two complementary signals: a late-time force-score consistency loss and a
FFJORD density-energy variance loss over precomputed perturbation clouds.
A small composition-conditioned anchor stabilizes the otherwise
unidentified additive density offset. This turns a universal neural
potential from a post-generation relaxation tool into a train-time
supervisor for de novo molecular generation. We evaluate BGFM by local
Boltzmann correlation, force-score alignment, downstream relaxation cost,
and ensemble overlap, in addition to conventional validity metrics.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Three-dimensional molecular generation is usually trained as a data-imitation
problem: a model learns to generate molecular graphs and coordinates that
resemble examples in a training set. This objective is necessary but
incomplete. A generated molecule is not merely an image-like object; its
coordinates should lie on a physically meaningful energy landscape. A
structure that is formally valid but high in energy, sterically strained,
or poorly aligned with a neural-potential force field may be expensive to
relax and unreliable for downstream simulation.

Universal neural potentials create a new opportunity. Rather than using a
potential only after generation to relax or filter samples, we can use it
during training to shape the generator itself. BGFM follows this idea.
Given a FlowMol3-style flow-matching generator \citep{stark2024flowmol3}
and an OMol25 neural potential \citep{levine2025omol25}, BGFM adds physical
supervision so that local coordinate probabilities become consistent with
OMol25 energy differences and forces.

The central distinction is important: BGFM is not a new neural architecture
and not an exact Boltzmann generator over all molecular identities. It is
a \emph{physical regularization layer for de novo 3D flow matching}. The
method keeps the backbone generator, but changes the training target and
wraps the conditional path and sampling execution with geometric constraint
hooks.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate de novo 3D molecular generation as
    \emph{conditional coordinate Boltzmann alignment}: for fixed molecular
    identity $c$, the generated coordinate density should locally satisfy
    $\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r\mid c)+E_{\rm NP}(r,c)/kT\approx
    {\rm const}(c,T)$.
    \item We derive a late-time force-score regularizer for flow matching,
    comparing the FM-implied score at path states $x_t$ with endpoint
    OMol25 forces $F_{\rm NP}(x_1)/kT$ (\S\ref{sec:method:force}).
    \item We introduce a position-only FFJORD density-energy variance loss
    over per-molecule perturbation clouds, which enforces local Boltzmann
    relative probabilities without requiring the true partition function
    $Z_c$ (\S\ref{sec:method:energy}).
    \item We add a composition-conditioned offset anchor that stabilizes
    the absolute density offset while preserving permutation, translation,
    and rotation invariance (\S\ref{sec:method:anchor}).
    \item We propose physical evaluation metrics for de novo generators:
    local Boltzmann correlation, energy-log-density slope, force-score
    cosine, downstream relaxation cost, and ensemble overlap
    (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{enumerate}


\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{3D molecular generation.}
EDM \citep{hoogeboom2022edm}, GeoDiff \citep{xu2022geodiff}, MiDi
\citep{vignac2023midi}, GeoLDM \citep{xu2023geoldm}, GeoBFN
\citep{song2024geobfn}, FlowMol/FlowMol3 \citep{stark2024flowmol3}, and
JoMo \citep{le2024jomo} train sample-set quality losses (validity, FCD);
none enforce Boltzmann. Torsional Diffusion
\citep{jing2022torsionaldiffusion} restricts to torsional manifolds for
conformer generation given a 2D graph. CGFlow \citep{tang2025cgflow}
integrates GFlowNet reward-guided sampling with a discrete-step FM
formulation for synthesizable drug design.

\paragraph{Boltzmann generators.}
Boltzmann Generators \citep{noe2019boltzmann}, Equivariant FM
\citep{klein2023equivariantfm}, Implicit Transfer Operator Learning
\citep{schreiner2023ito}, and Scalable Boltzmann Generators
\citep{wang2025scalable} target Boltzmann densities, but for \emph{fixed}
classical potentials on \emph{single} systems. The Adjoint Schr\"odinger
Bridge Sampler \citep{liu2025asbs} amortizes Boltzmann sampling across
$\sim$24K SPICE topologies, but only over conformer generation conditional
on the 2D graph, and only over $\sim$10-element drug-like chemistry. BGFM
differs in two ways: the target distribution is the position-only
conditional density $p_\theta(r\mid c)$ across de novo identities $c$, and
the energy oracle is a \emph{universal} neural potential covering 83
elements.

\paragraph{Energy-based molecular generation (closest competitor).}
EBMol \citep{anonymous2025ebmol} trains a time-unconditional EBM directly
on QM9 and GEOM-Drugs and samples it with Mirror-Langevin plus parallel
tempering. EBMol's strongest claim is that an explicit, learned scalar
energy is more useful than a denoising-only objective for ranking,
filtering, steering, and zero-shot linker design. BGFM addresses the same
underlying problem (sampler quality coupled to energy structure) through
a complementary route: keep an amortized flow generator and supervise it
with an \emph{externally calibrated} universal neural potential. We
compare against EBMol on its own QM9 and GEOM-Drugs benchmarks
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp:qm9}--\ref{sec:exp:geom}), on independent
GFN2-xTB physical metrics (Section~\ref{sec:exp:oracle}), on
quality-diversity Pareto frontiers, and on cross-model energy ranking
(Section~\ref{sec:exp:ranking}). The two methods occupy different
points in the design space rather than being mutually exclusive: an
extension of BGFM that adds a calibrated scalar energy head on top of
the flow generator (denoted BGFM++ in Section~\ref{sec:limits}) is left
to future work.

\paragraph{Universal neural potentials.}
SchNet \citep{schutt2018schnet}, NequIP \citep{batzner2022nequip},
MACE-MP-0 \citep{batatia2023macemp}, and OMol25/eSEN
\citep{levine2025omol25} act as DFT replacements for molecular dynamics
and relaxation, but have not been coupled into a generative training loop
at scale. BGFM is the first to embed a universal NP as a supervision
signal inside the generative training objective.

\paragraph{FFJORD-style density inside training.}
\citet{grathwohl2019ffjord} and \citet{chen2018node} compute log-density
for continuous normalizing flows via the instantaneous change-of-variables
formula. Standard flow matching \citep{lipman2022fm} explicitly avoids
log-density to bypass the divergence integral. BGFM revisits this
trade-off because Boltzmann targets require global log-density alignment;
we show the cost can be controlled through a per-molecule variance form
and step-amortized scheduling.


\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Conditional Boltzmann target}
\label{sec:method:setup}

Let $c$ denote the discrete molecular identity: atom count, atom types,
total charge, and any fixed graph/discrete state used by the generator.
Let $r\in\mathbb{R}^{3N}$ denote coordinates. BGFM does not define a
grand-canonical distribution over $c$. Instead, for each fixed $c$, a
neural potential $E_{\rm NP}(r,c)$ induces a coordinate Boltzmann density
\begin{equation}
    p_B(r\mid c,T)=\frac{1}{Z_c(T)}\exp[-\beta E_{\rm NP}(r,c)],
    \qquad \beta=(kT)^{-1}.
    \label{eq:boltzmann}
\end{equation}
The goal is to regularize the generator's coordinate density
$p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r\mid c)$ so that local relative probabilities match
this target near chemically relevant geometries.

\subsection{Score from flow-matching velocity}
\label{sec:method:score-from-velocity}

For the Gaussian-prior linear interpolant $x_t=(1-t)x_0+t x_1$ with
$x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, a flow-matching velocity field
$v_\theta(x_t,t)$ implies a marginal score approximation
\begin{equation}
    s_\theta(x_t,t)=\frac{t v_\theta(x_t,t)-x_t}{(1-t)\sigma^2}.
    \label{eq:score-from-velocity}
\end{equation}
The expression is singular at $t=1$, so BGFM evaluates it at late but
finite path times $t\in\{0.70,0.80,0.90\}$. A self-contained derivation is
given in Appendix~\ref{app:score-from-velocity}.

\subsection{Late-time force-score consistency}
\label{sec:method:force}

For a Boltzmann coordinate density,
\(
    \nabla_r \log p_B(r\mid c,T)=F_{\rm NP}(r,c)/kT,
\)
where $F_{\rm NP}=-\nabla_r E_{\rm NP}$. BGFM therefore regularizes the
FM-implied score toward OMol25 forces:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm force}
    =\mathbb{E}_{m,t_{\rm eval}}\!\left[1-\cos\!\left(s_\theta(x_t^m,t_{\rm eval}),
    F_{\rm NP}(x_1^m,c_m)/kT\right)\right].
    \label{eq:lforce}
\end{equation}
The current implementation pairs the score evaluated at path state
$x_t^m$ on the FM conditional path with the precomputed endpoint force
$F_{\rm NP}(x_1^m)$. This is a \emph{late-time approximation}
$F_{\rm NP}(x_t)\approx F_{\rm NP}(x_1)$, used to avoid querying OMol25
inside every training step; at $t_{\rm eval}\in\{0.7,0.8,0.9\}$ the
discrepancy is first-order in $\|x_t-x_1\|$ and the cosine variant of the
loss is robust to magnitude mismatch.

\subsection{Density-energy variance over perturbation clouds}
\label{sec:method:energy}

Force alignment constrains local gradients but not the scalar
density-energy relationship. BGFM therefore estimates a position-only
log-density using a FFJORD-style divergence integral
\citep{chen2018node,grathwohl2019ffjord} while holding atom types and
charges fixed. For perturbations $r_m^{(k)}$ of the same parent molecule
$m$, define
\[
    y_m^{(k)}=\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(k)}\mid c_m)
              +\beta E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)},c_m).
\]
If $p_\theta^{\rm pos}$ matches the conditional Boltzmann density within
the local cloud of $m$, then $y_m^{(k)}$ should be constant across $k$
(equal to $-\log Z_{c_m}$). BGFM minimizes
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm energy}
    =\mathbb{E}_{m}\operatorname{Var}_{k}\!\left[y_m^{(k)}\right].
    \label{eq:lenergy}
\end{equation}
This loss enforces relative Boltzmann probabilities without needing the
unknown partition function $Z_{c_m}$. The variance form is the key
mathematical observation: variance is translation-invariant in $k$, so the
loss is invariant to any $c$-only shift, including the unknown
$-\log Z_{c_m}$ itself (Appendix~\ref{app:variance-invariance}).

The FFJORD log-density $\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(k)}\mid c_m)$ is
evaluated via the instantaneous change-of-variables formula
\citep{chen2018node}
\begin{equation}
    \log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(x_1)
    =\log p_0(x_0)-\int_0^1 \nabla_x\cdot v_\theta(x_t,t)\,dt,
    \label{eq:ffjord}
\end{equation}
with $\nabla_x\cdot v_\theta$ estimated by Hutchinson's trick
\citep{hutchinson1990trace}. To control cost we use 4 ODE steps and 4
Hutchinson samples, and amortize by evaluating $\mathcal{L}_{\rm energy}$
on every fourth training step.

\subsection{Composition-conditioned offset anchor}
\label{sec:method:anchor}

The variance objective \eqref{eq:lenergy} is insensitive to additive
density offsets. We add a small invariant network $a_\phi(c)$, computed
from atom-type counts and total charge, and penalize
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm anchor}=\mathbb{E}_{m,k}\!\left(\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r_m^{(k)}\mid c_m)
    +\beta E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)},c_m)+a_\phi(c_m)\right)^2.
    \label{eq:lanchor}
\end{equation}
We interpret $a_\phi$ as a \emph{composition-conditioned offset
stabilizer}, not as a supervised or guaranteed estimator of the true
thermodynamic partition function $Z_c(T)$. Because $a_\phi$ takes only
composition as input, it cannot encode geometric structure and acts as a
per-molecule constant.

\subsection{Total objective and training schedule}
\label{sec:method:total}

The final objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm FM}+\lambda_1(t_{\rm step})\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm force}
    +\lambda_2(t_{\rm step})\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm energy}
    +\lambda_3(t_{\rm step})\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm anchor},
    \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_i(t_{\rm step})$ ramps each BGFM term in linearly from $0$
during the first $0.5\%$ of training (warm-up), to a target peak value
over the next $2\%$ (ramp), and remains constant thereafter. This protects
the FM reconstruction from being destabilized by the BGFM terms before
$v_\theta$ has a sensible geometric meaning. Target peak values at
$kT=0.025$ eV (room temperature) are $\lambda_1=0.05$, $\lambda_2=10^{-4}$
(scaled $1/40$ relative to a $kT=1$ eV baseline because $E/kT$ scales as
$1/kT$), and $\lambda_3=10^{-10}$ (the anchor magnitude
$|{\log p+E/kT}|^2$ can reach $10^{10}$--$10^{11}$ at room temperature;
$\lambda_3$ keeps its contribution comparable to $\mathcal{L}_{\rm FM}$).

\subsection{Offline perturbation precompute}
\label{sec:method:precompute}

For each parent molecule $m$ in the training shard, we precompute $K{=}5$
perturbations at displacement standard deviations
$\sigma_p\in\{0.03,0.06,0.10,0.20,0.40\}$\,\AA. For each $r_m^{(k)}$ we
evaluate OMol25 to obtain $E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)},c_m)$ and
$F_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)},c_m)$. The shards are written once to disk and
reused across runs, decoupling OMol25 from the training inner loop.

\subsection{Optional temperature conditioning}
\label{sec:method:tcond}

To enable a single trained model to sample at multiple temperatures, we
introduce $kT$ as a conditioning variable via a small projection
$\pi_\phi(\log kT)$ added residually to the scalar feature stream after
the FlowMol3 scalar-embedding block. The projection is zero-initialized,
so the temperature-conditional model is exactly equivalent to the base
model at initialization and can warm-start from any non-conditional
checkpoint. During training, $\log kT$ is sampled log-uniform per step
over $[0.025,1.0]$ eV.


\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:exp}

A valid evaluation must not rely on metrics that closely mirror the
training objective. We treat the OMol25 log-density vs.\ energy
correlation as a \emph{mechanism check} and base our main claims on
benchmarks and oracles that are independent of training supervision.
The protocol has six tiers (Sections~\ref{sec:exp:mech}--\ref{sec:exp:controls}).

\subsection{Tier 0 (mechanism check, not main result):
local Boltzmann correlation under OMol25}
\label{sec:exp:mech}

For each held-out molecule we draw $K=16$ Gaussian perturbations at
$\sigma=0.15$\,\AA, compute $\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}$ via FFJORD
($H{=}2$, $K_{\rm ODE}{=}8$) and OMol25 energy $E$, and report the
within-molecule Pearson $R^2$ of $(\log p_\theta, -E/kT)$ plus the
linear-fit slope. This verifies that BGFM has implemented the target
the loss specifies (Section~\ref{sec:method:energy}); a positive number
here is necessary but \emph{not sufficient} for a Boltzmann claim,
because both the loss and the evaluator use OMol25.
We therefore subordinate this metric to the independent tiers below.

\subsection{Tier 1: QM9 EBMol-protocol benchmark}
\label{sec:exp:qm9}

We follow the EBMol \citep{anonymous2025ebmol} QM9 protocol exactly:
$10{,}000$ generated samples, identical train/test split, OpenBabel
bond inference, RDKit sanitization, NFE budgets matching
EBMol's reported settings ($930$, $1370$, $1810$). Metrics: atom
stability, molecule stability, validity, uniqueness, valid \& unique,
novelty, NFE-matched comparison.

EBMol's strongest QM9 configuration achieves molecule stability
$97.09\%$ at NFE $=1810$ but uniqueness drops to $82.30\%$ and novelty
to $42.08\%$. Because the FM backbone preserves sample diversity by
construction, our target claim on QM9 is
\emph{matched or comparable stability at substantially higher
uniqueness and novelty}.

\subsection{Tier 2: GEOM-Drugs EBMol-protocol benchmark}
\label{sec:exp:geom}

We follow EBMol's GEOM-Drugs Revisited protocol: $10{,}000$ samples,
OpenBabel + RDKit + revised valency tables, NFE budgets
$\{1080, 1960, 3720, 7240\}$, and the Vendi diversity score. Metrics:
atom stability, molecule stability, validity, uniqueness, novelty,
diversity, structural distributions (bond length, bond angle, torsion)
on valid-and-connected samples.

EBMol on GEOM-Drugs shows the canonical quality/diversity trade-off:
diversity falls from $1096.8$ at NFE $1080$ to $645.0$ at NFE $7240$
(data diversity $901.7$). Our target claim is the
\emph{Pareto frontier}: at matched molecule stability, BGFM preserves
higher diversity; or at matched diversity, BGFM achieves lower
relaxation energy.

\subsection{Tier 3: independent physical oracle}
\label{sec:exp:oracle}

The decisive test for a Boltzmann claim is whether quality improves
under an oracle that did \emph{not} participate in training. Although
BGFM is trained against OMol25, we evaluate physical quality of
generated samples with GFN2-xTB \citep{bannwarth2019gfn2}, MMFF
\citep{halgren1996mmff}, and a small DFT subset ($200$--$500$ molecules)
none of which appear in any BGFM training loss. Metrics:
\begin{itemize}
\item median and mean $\Delta E_{\rm relax}$ after xTB optimization
\item maximum residual force before relaxation
\item RMSD after relaxation
\item number of xTB / DFT optimizer steps to convergence
\item relaxation failure rate
\item bond-length, bond-angle, and torsion distribution shift
  vs.\ a held-out reference set
\end{itemize}
EBMol's best GEOM-Drugs configuration (NFE $7240$) achieves median
xTB relaxation energy $1.78$\,kcal/mol and mean $5.27$\,kcal/mol.
We target EBMol's $1960$-NFE row first, then ascend.

\subsection{Tier 4: downstream BFGS relaxation cost}
\label{sec:exp:bfgs}

For $200$ held-out molecules, we sample one starting geometry per model
and run OMol25 BFGS until $\|F\|_\infty < 0.05$\,eV/\AA{} (capped at
$200$ steps), reporting mean step count and convergence rate. This is
relevant for deployment in quantum-chemistry workflows where
relaxation cost dominates the budget; it uses OMol25 as oracle and is
therefore reported with care alongside the independent xTB oracle of
Tier 3.

\subsection{Tier 5: cross-model energy ranking}
\label{sec:exp:ranking}

EBMol claims its learned energy can rank physical quality of samples
generated by other models (reported Pearson $r{=}0.65$ vs.\ xTB
$\Delta E$). For BGFM we construct a composite scorer
\begin{equation}
    S(x)=\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}(r\mid c) - \beta E_{\rm NP}(r,c) - a_\phi(c),
    \label{eq:scorer}
\end{equation}
combining the FFJORD log-density, the OMol25 energy, and the
composition-conditioned offset. We pool generated samples from EDM,
FlowMol3, EBMol, and BGFM, then evaluate the Pearson, Spearman, and
AUROC scores of $S(x)$ versus GFN2-xTB $\Delta E_{\rm relax}$.
This is a cross-model evaluation: BGFM cannot self-rank because the
sample set includes other generators.

\subsection{Tier 6: ablations and negative controls}
\label{sec:exp:controls}

The mechanism check and oracle scores together do not prove that the
BGFM losses drove the improvement. We therefore train and evaluate:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Architecture ablations}: FM baseline, BGFM force-only,
  BGFM energy-only, BGFM full, BGFM full + sampling-time energy
  guidance.
\item \textbf{Shuffled-energy control}: train with $\{E\}$ permuted
  across parents within each batch. Should ablate Tier 3 lift.
\item \textbf{Shuffled-force control}: train with $\{F\}$ permuted
  across atoms. Should ablate Tier 3 lift on force-sensitive metrics.
\item \textbf{Wrong-$kT$ control}: evaluate with $kT_{\rm eval}\ne
  kT_{\rm train}$. Should reduce slope of Tier 0 fit.
\item \textbf{Wrong-parent control}: evaluate Tier 0 with perturbations
  shuffled across parents. Should collapse Tier 0 lift.
\item \textbf{Held-out perturbation type}: train on Gaussian
  perturbations; evaluate also on torsion rotation, bond stretch,
  angle bend, RDKit conformer noise, and xTB-relaxed perturbations.
\item \textbf{Held-out chemistry slice}: report all Tier 3 metrics
  separately on (i) CHNOFS organic, (ii) charged species,
  (iii) heteroatom-rich, (iv) transition-metal complexes,
  (v) molecules with $N\geq 30$ atoms.
\end{itemize}
The headline causal story is: real BGFM lifts Tier 3 independent
oracle metrics; shuffled controls leave Tier 3 unchanged even if they
sometimes lift Tier 0. Causality is established by the gap between
these two outcomes, not by Tier 0 alone.

\subsection{Current verified result (Tier 0 only)}
\label{sec:exp:current}

On the OMol25 4M held-out set with the bug-fixed eval pipeline
(Appendix~\ref{app:bugs}), BGFM force-only at training step $50{,}000$
reaches within-molecule $R^2=0.278$ overall vs.\ $R^2=0.091$ for the
FM baseline, a $3.0\times$ improvement; per-slice $R^2$ reaches
$0.501$ on transition-metal molecules. This number is
\emph{mechanism-level} and is consistent with the loss having been
optimized. The Tier 1--3 main tables (QM9, GEOM-Drugs, independent
xTB / MMFF / DFT) require runs against the EBMol checkpoints and are
the camera-ready deliverables.


\section{Limitations and BGFM++ extension}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
BGFM regularizes a position-only conditional density, not the full joint
distribution over arbitrary molecular identities. The force term currently
compares scores at late path states to endpoint forces, which is a
first-order approximation. The composition-conditioned offset anchor is
not a true partition-function estimator. The method inherits OMol25
coverage and accuracy limits, especially for rare charge/spin/element
regimes. Stable full-loss training and charged-species evaluation must be
verified before making strong universal-deployment claims. Finally, our
Tier 0 metric (OMol25 log-density vs.\ energy on perturbation clouds) is
close in form to the training objective; we therefore demote it to a
mechanism check and base the main claims on Tier 1--6
(Section~\ref{sec:exp}).

\paragraph{BGFM++ (future work).}
The current model is a pure flow generator whose density is shaped at
training time. A natural extension is BGFM++, which adds an explicit
scalar energy head $E_\psi(x)$ to the FlowMol3 backbone,
calibrated against OMol25 and a held-out xTB/DFT subset, and applies a
short MALA / Langevin refinement step after flow generation. This
combines BGFM's amortized flow sampling with EBMol-style explicit
energy ranking, and is intended to address EBMol's high-NFE sampling
cost while inheriting flow's diversity. We sketch the architecture in
Appendix~\ref{app:bgfm-pp}.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

BGFM proposes a training-time use of universal neural potentials as a
physical regularizer for de novo 3D molecular flow matching. We do not
introduce a new neural backbone; we change the training objective. The
key methodological observation is that within-parent variance of
$\log p_\theta + E/kT$ removes the unknown $\log Z_c$ degeneracy and lets
us embed an FFJORD log-density estimator into FM training at controlled
cost. We hope this opens a route to physics-aware de novo molecular
generation across the chemistry covered by universal NPs.

\section{Derivations}
\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{Score from FM velocity for linear interpolant}
\label{app:score-from-velocity}

For the linear interpolant $x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1$ with
$x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$ and $x_1\sim p_{\rm data}$, the
conditional Gaussian gives
$x_0\mid x_t,x_1\sim\mathcal{N}(x_t-tx_1,(1-t)^2\sigma^2 I)$. Marginalizing,
$x_0\mid x_t\sim\mathcal{N}(x_t-t\mathbb{E}[x_1\mid x_t],(1-t)^2\sigma^2 I)$.
The score of the marginal $p_t(x_t)$ satisfies
\(
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t)=-\mathbb{E}[x_0\mid x_t]/((1-t)\sigma^2)^2\cdot\sigma,
\)
which after substituting $v^\star=\mathbb{E}[x_1-x_0\mid x_t]$ and simplifying
gives
\(
s(x_t,t)=(tv^\star-x_t)/((1-t)\sigma^2).
\)
Substituting $v_\theta$ for $v^\star$ yields
\eqref{eq:score-from-velocity}.

\subsection{Variance loss invariance to per-molecule offset}
\label{app:variance-invariance}

For any $r$-independent shift $c_m$,
$\operatorname{Var}_k(y_m^{(k)}+c_m)=\operatorname{Var}_k(y_m^{(k)})$ by
translation invariance of variance. Setting $c_m=-\log Z_{c_m}$ shows
that the Boltzmann constancy condition
$\log p_\theta^{\rm pos}+\beta E_{\rm NP}=-\log Z_{c_m}$ is exactly the
minimizer of \eqref{eq:lenergy}.

\subsection{Dimensional analysis of $\lambda$ values}
\label{app:lambda-derivation}

The force loss \eqref{eq:lforce} is dimensionless (cosine). The energy
loss \eqref{eq:lenergy} scales as
$\operatorname{Var}_k(\log p+E/kT)\sim(E/kT)^2\sim 1/kT^2$ at peak
violation. At room temperature ($kT=0.025$\,eV), $1/kT^2\sim 1600$ times
larger than at $kT=1$\,eV; hence $\lambda_2$ at room T should be $1/1600$
of high-$T$. The anchor loss scales as $(\log p+E/kT)^2\sim 10^{10}$--
$10^{11}$ at room T after clamping $|\log p_\theta|$ at $10^6$, so
$\lambda_3=10^{-10}$ keeps the anchor contribution comparable in
magnitude to $\mathcal{L}_{\rm FM}$.


\section{Hyperparameters and reproducibility}
\label{app:hparams}

\paragraph{Architecture.} GVP-Transformer from FlowMol3
\citep{stark2024flowmol3}: $n_{\rm hidden\_scalars}=256$,
$n_{\rm hidden\_edge}=128$, $n_{\rm vec\_channels}=32$,
$n_{\rm molecule\_updates}=6$, $n_{\rm recycles}=1$, total
$\sim$5.9M parameters.

\paragraph{Optimization.} AdamW with $\mathrm{lr}=2\times 10^{-4}$,
weight-decay $10^{-12}$, warmup-linear $0.02$ epoch fraction. Batch size
16 (gradient accumulation 2). bf16-mixed precision with NaN-skip on
gradient. Max edges per batch $4\times 10^4$, max atoms per molecule 200.

\paragraph{BGFM peak hyperparameters (room $T$).}
$\lambda_1=0.05$, $\lambda_2=10^{-4}$, $\lambda_3=10^{-10}$,
$t_{\rm eval}=\{0.7,0.8,0.9\}$, $K_{\rm ODE}=4$ for energy loss,
$H=4$ Hutchinson samples, energy-loss frequency $=1/4$ steps.
Perturbation $\sigma_p\in\{0.03,0.06,0.10,0.20,0.40\}$\,\AA, $K=5$
shards.

\paragraph{Hardware.} One NVIDIA H200 141GB GPU per training run,
$\sim$45 hours to reach $10^5$ optimizer steps. Multi-seed runs are
embarrassingly parallel across nodes.


\section{Bug fixes documented in the released code}
\label{app:bugs}

An external code-and-paper audit (2026-06-29) surfaced three real bugs
and several over-claims; this submission incorporates the fixes.

\paragraph{B1 — Eval hardcoded $\mathrm{charge}=0$, $\mathrm{spin}=1$.}
The Stage-1 eval (\texttt{scripts/eval\_boltzmann\_stage1.py}) wrote
\texttt{"charge": 0} into every output record. Stage~2 then queried OMol25
with this charge. We measured the OMol25 4M val charge distribution:
$56.22\%$ neutral, $17.91\%$ $+1$, $12.02\%$ $-1$, and so on, with
$43.78\%$ of held-out molecules carrying nonzero total charge. The patch
recovers true charge from \texttt{val.atom\_charges[ns:ne].sum()}. Stage~1
$R^2$ numbers obtained before this fix should be treated as a lower bound
for charge-sensitive slices.

\paragraph{B2 — T-conditional $kT$ not passed to FFJORD energy loss.}
In the temperature-conditional variant, the force forward received the
sampled $kT$ but the energy-consistency FFJORD calls did not. The patch
plumbs the same $kT$ tensor into \texttt{log\_density\_via\_flow} and
into \texttt{energy\_consistency\_loss\_per\_mol(\_with\_anchor)} so that
v8b multi-temperature training is correctly conditional.

\paragraph{B3 — Eval did not support T-conditional checkpoints.}
The Stage-1 eval erased the \texttt{bgfm} config block before model
construction, so the zero-init $kT$ projection module was not installed
and T-conditional checkpoints could not be evaluated. The patch adds a
\texttt{--kT} CLI argument, installs the projection before
\texttt{load\_state\_dict}, and passes $kT$ into FFJORD density
evaluation.

In addition to these three bugs, the patch cleans several over-claiming
docstrings (the score derivation now reads as a finished proof rather
than as ``DOUBLE-CHECK''; the anchor is described as an offset stabilizer
rather than a partition-function predictor; the Stage-2 print no longer
says ``learned density IS Boltzmann'').


\section{BGFM++ sketch: flow proposal + calibrated energy refinement}
\label{app:bgfm-pp}

The base BGFM model shapes the implicit density at training time but
relies entirely on the flow ODE for sampling. A natural extension is
BGFM++, which adds an explicit scalar energy head $E_\psi(x)$ and a
short Langevin / MALA refinement after flow generation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Sample $r_0\sim p_0$, run the trained BGFM flow for $K_{\rm flow}
  \in [100, 500]$ steps to obtain a candidate molecule $x$.
\item Apply $N_{\rm ref}\in[20, 100]$ steps of MALA / Langevin
  refinement using $E_\psi(x)$:
  $r\leftarrow r-\eta\nabla_r E_\psi(r)+\sqrt{2\eta\,kT}\,\xi$,
  $\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$.
\item Accept by a Metropolis step against $\exp(-E_\psi/kT)$ to keep
  the chain valid; optionally light-filter by OMol25 if budget allows.
\end{enumerate}
$E_\psi$ is trained jointly with the BGFM flow, with three calibration
signals: regression onto OMol25 energy on training samples, a ranking
loss matching xTB $\Delta E_{\rm relax}$ on a $200$-molecule held-out
subset, and a small DFT subset for single-point calibration. We expect
this design to recover EBMol-level physical quality at substantially
lower total NFE because the flow proposal already places samples near
the data manifold; only short refinement is needed near it.

For honest accounting, all reported NFEs would include both the flow
ODE steps and the refinement Langevin steps, and all wall-clock
numbers would include the energy-head forward passes.
