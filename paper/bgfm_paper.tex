\begin{abstract}
Most de novo 3D molecular generators are trained to imitate molecular
data. Their samples are often chemically valid but high in
strain energy, requiring substantial downstream relaxation before use
in simulation. Energy-based models target the equilibrium distribution
directly but learn the energy from geometry alone and pay a heavy
inference-time sampling cost. We propose \textbf{BGFM}, a Boltzmann-
calibrated flow-matching framework for de novo 3D molecular
generation. BGFM consists of four modules trained jointly:
(i) an amortized flow proposal over coordinates, atom types, and
charges; (ii) Boltzmann regularization that aligns the flow's implied
score and conditional density with an external universal neural
potential through force-score and density-energy consistency losses;
(iii) a calibrated scalar energy head distilled from the same neural
potential; and (iv) a short learned-energy Langevin corrector that
refines the flow proposal toward the energy landscape using
$20$--$100$ steps. We further introduce a joint density estimator that
combines a coordinate-conditional FFJORD integral with the
log-probability of the discrete categorical path, so that BGFM's
training objective is well-defined over the full molecular
configuration $(\text{atom counts}, \text{types}, \text{charges}, \text{coordinates})$
rather than coordinates alone. On QM9 and GEOM-Drugs, BGFM matches
the standard validity/stability of recent flow-matching baselines
while preserving higher uniqueness and diversity. Under independent
GFN2-xTB and MMFF relaxation oracles that are not used in training,
BGFM produces lower-strain geometries than energy-based and flow
baselines at matched compute, and improves the Pareto frontier of
physical quality vs.\ diversity vs.\ sampling cost.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Three-dimensional molecular generation is increasingly used as the
front end of computational pipelines that assume physically
realistic geometries: ligand discovery, electrolyte screening,
catalyst design, and conformer-based property prediction. Two
families of generators currently dominate. Flow-matching and
diffusion models \citep{hoogeboom2022edm,xu2023geoldm,vignac2023midi,
stark2024flowmol3,song2024geobfn} are trained to imitate molecular
data and amortize sampling over a small number of ODE steps; they
produce diverse, chemically valid molecules but their geometries are
not aligned with any explicit energy landscape and can require tens
to hundreds of optimization steps under a quantum-chemistry oracle
before they become useful. Energy-based models such as EBMol
\citep{anonymous2025ebmol} learn a persistent scalar energy from
geometry alone and sample with Mirror-Langevin dynamics and parallel
tempering; this yields physically aligned samples but at substantially
higher inference cost and with a measurable diversity collapse at
high sampling compute.

We propose BGFM, a framework that takes the favorable properties of
each family by construction. BGFM keeps an amortized flow proposal as
the primary generator, but couples it during training to an external
universal neural potential (OMol25 \citep{levine2025omol25}, covering
$83$ elements and approximately $10^8$ DFT-quality configurations)
through two losses that align the flow's implied score and conditional
density with the potential's force and energy landscape. To recover
the inference-time benefits of an explicit energy landscape without
its sampling cost, BGFM additionally trains a calibrated scalar energy
head distilled from the same potential, and uses it for a short
learned-energy Langevin corrector that runs only $20$--$100$ steps
after the flow proposal. The result is a single trained model that
inherits the diversity and amortized sampling speed of flow matching
while matching or improving on the physical quality of energy-based
samplers, at a small fraction of their inference compute.

A second methodological contribution is a joint density estimator
that closes a gap in earlier flow-matching density-energy formulations
\citep{klein2023equivariantfm,grathwohl2019ffjord,chen2018node}.
Coordinate-only continuous-flow log-density is well-defined but does
not account for the discrete structure (atom counts, types, charges)
that a de novo generator also samples. BGFM combines a
coordinate-conditional FFJORD integral with the log-probability of
the discrete categorical path so that the training objective is a
function of the full molecular configuration, not its continuous
slice alone. This makes the Boltzmann regularization well-defined
across compositions and provides a principled basis for the
energy-head calibration on heterogeneous chemistry.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Framework.} A single trained 3D molecular generator
    combining an amortized flow proposal, external neural-potential
    Boltzmann regularization, a calibrated scalar energy head, and a
    short learned-energy corrector. To our knowledge BGFM is the
    first de novo 3D flow generator that calibrates an amortized
    molecular generator against an external universal neural
    potential through both force-score consistency and density-energy
    Boltzmann regularization, with a learned energy corrector
    providing fast inference-time refinement.
    \item \textbf{Joint density estimator.} A hybrid
    discrete-continuous log-density of the form
    $\log p_\theta(x) = \log p_\theta(r\mid c) + \log p_\theta(c)$
    that combines a coordinate FFJORD integral with the CTMC path
    probability of the discrete components. This lifts BGFM's
    density-energy loss from a coordinate-conditional alignment to a
    full molecular alignment.
    \item \textbf{Calibrated energy head.} A scalar head $\hat E_\psi$
    trained jointly with the flow against neural-potential energy
    and forces, supplying an explicit, calibrated energy landscape
    that BGFM uses for ranking and refinement.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation framework.} A six-experiment protocol
    that separates standard generative quality, transferable physical
    quality under independent oracles, the quality-diversity-compute
    Pareto frontier, mechanistic Boltzmann alignment, ablations, and
    negative controls; with honest accounting of flow NFEs, energy-head
    NFEs, oracle calls, wall-clock time, and failure rates.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{3D molecular generation with flows and diffusion.}
EDM \citep{hoogeboom2022edm}, GeoLDM \citep{xu2023geoldm}, MiDi
\citep{vignac2023midi}, GeoBFN \citep{song2024geobfn}, JoMo
\citep{le2024jomo}, SemlaFlow \citep{irwin2024semlaflow}, and
FlowMol/FlowMol3 \citep{stark2024flowmol3} train sample-set quality
objectives (validity, FCD, distance distributions). Concurrent work,
FlexiFlow \citep{flexiflow2025}, extends flow matching to joint
sampling of molecular graphs and conformer ensembles and is the
current standard-generation state of the art on QM9 and GEOM-Drugs.
None of these methods incorporates an explicit energy landscape or an
external neural-potential supervision signal.

\paragraph{Energy-based molecular generation.}
EBMol \citep{anonymous2025ebmol} trains a time-unconditional EBM on
3D molecular data and samples with Mirror-Langevin dynamics and
parallel tempering. EBMol's central claim is that an explicit scalar
energy enables physically meaningful sample ranking, filtering, and
steering. BGFM addresses the same problem through a complementary
route: it keeps the amortized flow generator and calibrates a scalar
energy head from an external universal neural potential, recovering
the explicit-energy capability without the inference-time sampling
cost of an EBM.

\paragraph{Boltzmann generators and energy-based flow matching.}
Boltzmann Generators \citep{noe2019boltzmann}, Equivariant Flow
Matching \citep{klein2023equivariantfm}, Implicit Transfer Operator
Learning \citep{schreiner2023ito}, Scalable Boltzmann Generators
\citep{wang2025scalable}, and the Adjoint Schr\"odinger Bridge Sampler
\citep{liu2025asbs} target Boltzmann densities for fixed classical
potentials on single systems or for fixed-topology conformer
generation. Iterated energy-based flow matching
\citep{iefm2025} trains continuous normalizing flows from
unnormalized energy densities using a simulation-free objective; it
addresses fixed continuous Boltzmann sampling on low-dimensional
benchmarks (Gaussian mixtures, double-well systems) and is
theoretically related to BGFM but targets a different problem
(known-energy continuous sampling, not de novo molecule generation
under an external universal neural-potential teacher).

\paragraph{Universal neural potentials.}
SchNet \citep{schutt2018schnet}, NequIP \citep{batzner2022nequip},
MACE-MP-0 \citep{batatia2023macemp}, and OMol25/eSEN
\citep{levine2025omol25} act as DFT replacements for molecular
dynamics and relaxation. BGFM uses OMol25 not as a downstream
relaxer but as a training-time teacher that supplies energies and
forces for the Boltzmann regularization and the energy-head
calibration.


\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We denote by $x = (c, r)$ a molecular configuration with discrete part
$c$ (atom counts $N$, per-atom types, per-atom formal charges) and
continuous part $r \in \mathbb{R}^{3N}$ (coordinates). An external
universal neural potential supplies an energy $E_{\rm NP}(r, c)$ and
its gradient $F_{\rm NP}(r, c) = -\nabla_r E_{\rm NP}(r, c)$ for any
admissible $x$. Temperature is parameterized by $\beta = 1/(kT)$.

The Boltzmann distribution we target is the conditional Boltzmann at
the level of coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    p_B(r \mid c, T) = \frac{1}{Z_c(T)} \exp\!\left[-\beta E_{\rm NP}(r, c)\right].
    \label{eq:boltzmann}
\end{equation}
BGFM combines four modules: a flow proposal
(\S\ref{sec:method:flow}), a Boltzmann regularization
(\S\ref{sec:method:reg}), a calibrated energy head
(\S\ref{sec:method:head}), and a learned-energy corrector
(\S\ref{sec:method:corrector}). A joint density estimator
(\S\ref{sec:method:joint}) provides a hybrid discrete-continuous
log-probability used by the Boltzmann regularization.

\subsection{Module 1 --- Flow proposal}
\label{sec:method:flow}

We instantiate the flow proposal on a FlowMol3-style backbone
\citep{stark2024flowmol3}: positions follow a Gaussian-prior linear
interpolant, atom types and charges follow a CTMC simplex prior, and
a GVP-Transformer parameterizes the time-dependent velocity field
$v_\theta(x_t, t)$. Given prior samples $x_0 \sim p_0$, the flow
maps $x_0$ to a candidate molecule $x = (c, r)$ in
$K_{\rm flow} \in [100, 500]$ ODE steps:
\begin{equation}
    x_0 \sim p_0, \qquad x \,=\, \Phi_\theta(x_0).
    \label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
The standard flow-matching reconstruction loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\rm FM} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, x_1, t}\|v_\theta(x_t, t) - (x_1 - x_0)\|^2$
is retained alongside CTMC reconstruction terms for the discrete
channels. The flow proposal preserves diversity and amortized
sampling.

\subsection{Module 2 --- Boltzmann regularization}
\label{sec:method:reg}

The Boltzmann regularization couples $\Phi_\theta$ to the neural
potential through two consistency losses.

\paragraph{Force-score consistency.}
The Boltzmann density satisfies
$\nabla_r \log p_B = F_{\rm NP}/kT$. From the velocity field we
recover the implied score in closed form for the linear interpolant:
\begin{equation}
    s_\theta(r_t, t) = \frac{t\, v_\theta(r_t, t) - r_t}{(1 - t)\,\sigma^2}.
    \label{eq:score-from-velocity}
\end{equation}
At late path times $t_{\rm eval} \in \{0.70, 0.80, 0.90\}$, where
$r_t$ is close to the data manifold, BGFM enforces
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm force}
    = \mathbb{E}_{m,\, t_{\rm eval}}\!\left[1 - \cos\!\bigl(s_\theta(r_t^m, t_{\rm eval}),\, F_{\rm NP}(r_1^m, c_m)/kT\bigr)\right].
    \label{eq:lforce}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Density-energy consistency.}
For each parent molecule $m$ we precompute $K=5$ perturbations at
displacement scales $\sigma_p \in \{0.03, 0.06, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40\}\,\text{\AA}$
and obtain their neural-potential energies. The Boltzmann constancy
condition $\log p_\theta + \beta E_{\rm NP} = -\log Z_{c_m}$ implies
that for fixed $c_m$ the quantity
$y_m^{(k)} = \log p_\theta(r_m^{(k)} \mid c_m) + \beta E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)}, c_m)$
is constant in $k$. The variance form removes the unknown additive
constant $\log Z_{c_m}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm dens}
    = \mathbb{E}_m\,\mathrm{Var}_k\!\left[ y_m^{(k)} \right].
    \label{eq:ldens}
\end{equation}
A composition-conditioned offset stabilizer
$a_\phi(c)$, with input only the atom-type histogram and total
charge, anchors the absolute scale:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm anchor}
    = \mathbb{E}_{m, k}\!\left( \log p_\theta(r_m^{(k)} \mid c_m) + \beta E_{\rm NP}(r_m^{(k)}, c_m) + a_\phi(c_m) \right)^2.
    \label{eq:lanchor}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Module 3 --- Calibrated energy head}
\label{sec:method:head}

A scalar energy head $\hat E_\psi(r, c)$ is attached to the flow
backbone and trained jointly against the same neural-potential
teacher:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rm head}
    = \mathbb{E}\,\bigl|\hat E_\psi(r, c) - E_{\rm NP}(r, c)\bigr|
    + \lambda_F\, \mathbb{E}\,\bigl\| -\nabla_r \hat E_\psi(r, c) - F_{\rm NP}(r, c) \bigr\|^2.
    \label{eq:lhead}
\end{equation}
$\hat E_\psi$ is an explicit, calibrated energy landscape that BGFM
uses (i) for inference-time refinement (\S\ref{sec:method:corrector}),
(ii) as a cross-model scorer for ranking generated samples
(\S\ref{sec:exp:ranking}), and (iii) as the primary external
counterweight to EBMol's learned-but-uncalibrated energy.

\subsection{Module 4 --- Learned-energy corrector}
\label{sec:method:corrector}

After the flow proposal returns a candidate $(c, r)$, BGFM applies a
short Langevin corrector under $\hat E_\psi$:
\begin{equation}
    r^{j+1} = r^j \,-\, \eta_j\, \nabla_r \hat E_\psi(r^j, c) \,+\, \sigma_j\, \xi^j,
    \qquad \xi^j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),\ j = 0, \dots, J - 1,
    \label{eq:corrector}
\end{equation}
with $J \in [20, 100]$, step size $\eta_j$ following a small inverse
schedule, and noise scale $\sigma_j = \sqrt{2 \eta_j / \beta}$ so the
chain targets $\exp(-\beta \hat E_\psi)$. Optional Metropolis
correction can be enabled. The discrete components $c$ are not
modified; the corrector refines coordinates only. All applications of
$\hat E_\psi$ are reported as energy-head NFEs, distinct from the
flow ODE NFEs, in the experimental tables.

\subsection{Joint density estimator}
\label{sec:method:joint}

The Boltzmann regularization in
Eqs.\,(\ref{eq:ldens})--(\ref{eq:lanchor}) requires
$\log p_\theta$ on each perturbation. We estimate it as the sum of a
coordinate-conditional log-density and a discrete log-probability:
\begin{equation}
    \log p_\theta(x) \,=\, \log p_\theta(r \mid c) \,+\, \log p_\theta(c).
    \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
The coordinate term uses the FFJORD instantaneous change-of-variables
\citep{chen2018node, grathwohl2019ffjord}:
\begin{equation}
    \log p_\theta(r \mid c) = \log p_0(r_0) - \int_0^1 \nabla_r \cdot v_\theta(r_t, c, t)\, dt,
    \label{eq:ffjord}
\end{equation}
with the divergence estimated by Hutchinson's trick
\citep{hutchinson1990trace}. The discrete term decomposes into
contributions from atom counts $N$, per-atom types, and per-atom
charges, evaluated on the CTMC bridge that generated $c$:
\begin{equation}
    \log p_\theta(c) = \log p_\theta(N) + \sum_{i=1}^N \log p_\theta(a_i) + \sum_{i=1}^N \log p_\theta(q_i),
    \label{eq:discrete-logp}
\end{equation}
where each per-atom term is the bridge path probability under the CTMC
parameterization \citep{stark2024flowmol3}. Using the full joint
density makes
$\mathcal{L}_{\rm dens}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\rm anchor}$ well-defined
across heterogeneous compositions, not just within fixed-composition
clouds.

\subsection{Total objective}
\label{sec:method:total}

The training objective combines the flow-matching reconstruction,
the Boltzmann regularization, and the energy-head calibration:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    = \mathcal{L}_{\rm FM}
    + \lambda_1\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm force}
    + \lambda_2\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm dens}
    + \lambda_3\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm anchor}
    + \lambda_4\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm head}.
    \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
The Langevin corrector (\S\ref{sec:method:corrector}) is an
inference-time procedure and does not contribute to training. We
ramp $\lambda_1$ through $\lambda_4$ linearly from zero over the
first two percent of training to protect the flow reconstruction
during the early phase. Default values, derived from a dimensional
analysis at room temperature ($kT = 0.025$\,eV), are
$\lambda_1 = 0.05$, $\lambda_2 = 10^{-4}$, $\lambda_3 = 10^{-12}$,
and $\lambda_4 = 1.0$ for the energy-magnitude term with
$\lambda_F = 0.1$ for the force-gradient term.


\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Experimental questions.}
Our experiments are designed to answer five questions.
\emph{Q1.} Does BGFM preserve the standard chemical validity and
diversity expected from de novo 3D molecular generators?
\emph{Q2.} Does BGFM improve physical quality under \emph{independent}
evaluators that are not used in training?
\emph{Q3.} How does BGFM compare with energy-based and flow baselines
on the joint quality--diversity--compute frontier?
\emph{Q4.} Are the gains caused by correct neural-potential signal,
rather than by additional regularization or evaluation overlap?
\emph{Q5.} Does each module of BGFM contribute, and what does each
module add?

\paragraph{Evaluation philosophy.}
Because BGFM is trained with neural-potential energies and forces,
neural-potential-based correlation is reported as a mechanism
diagnostic rather than as a primary external benchmark. The headline
physical-quality results use independent evaluators (GFN2-xTB,
MMFF94, and a DFT subset) that do not appear in any training loss.
For every sampling-cost number we report flow NFEs, energy-head NFEs,
neural-potential oracle calls during sampling (zero by construction
for BGFM), wall-clock time, and failure rate.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We evaluate on three regimes: the standard
QM9 benchmark, the GEOM-Drugs benchmark \citep{axelrod2022geom} under
the revised OpenBabel/RDKit protocol, and held-out OMol25
broad-chemistry slices stressing charge, atom count, element
composition, and metal-containing systems.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Standard generation baselines: EDM
\citep{hoogeboom2022edm}, GeoLDM \citep{xu2023geoldm}, GeoBFN
\citep{song2024geobfn}, SemlaFlow \citep{irwin2024semlaflow},
FlowMol3 \citep{stark2024flowmol3}, FlexiFlow
\citep{flexiflow2025}, and EBMol \citep{anonymous2025ebmol}. We
additionally include controlled BGFM ablations on the same backbone:
the flow-matching baseline (no Boltzmann regularization, no head, no
corrector); BGFM without the energy head and corrector; BGFM without
the density-energy loss; BGFM without the force-score loss; BGFM
without the corrector; and full BGFM.

\subsection{Quality--diversity--compute frontier}
\label{sec:exp:pareto}

Energy-based samplers can improve sample quality by increasing
Langevin or parallel-tempering compute, but at a cost in diversity.
We compare methods at multiple compute budgets. For EBMol we
evaluate the low-, medium-, and high-NFE configurations. For BGFM we
report several settings of the corrector horizon $J$ and step size
schedule alongside the unrefined flow proposal. We plot xTB median
$\Delta E_{\rm relax}$ against wall-clock time, with Vendi diversity
indicated by color and the valid-low-strain count
indicated by point size.

We additionally summarize the frontier with a single throughput
metric:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{VULS}_{\tau}
    \,=\, \frac{\#\{\,\text{valid, unique, low-strain samples with } \Delta E_{\rm xTB} < \tau\,\}}{\text{GPU-hour}},
    \label{eq:vuls}
\end{equation}
for $\tau \in \{2, 5, 10\}$ kcal/mol. $\mathrm{VULS}_{\tau}$ measures
how many chemically valid, physically low-strain, non-duplicate
molecules a method delivers per unit compute.

\subsection{Mechanism diagnostic: Boltzmann alignment}
\label{sec:exp:mech}

For held-out parent molecules, we construct perturbation clouds using
families not identical to the training shards: unseen Gaussian noise
levels, torsion rotations, bond stretches, angle bends, RDKit
conformer perturbations, and short xTB or neural-potential Langevin
perturbations. For each perturbation we evaluate the joint density
$\log p_\theta(x)$ from \eqref{eq:joint} and compare to
$-E_{\rm NP}/kT$. We report within-parent centered Pearson
correlation, Spearman correlation, slope, and slope calibration error.
This experiment verifies the mechanism that BGFM trains for; it is
not the headline benchmark, because it shares its evaluator with the
training loss. Cross-oracle transfer to xTB and a DFT subset is
also reported.

\subsection{Negative controls}
\label{sec:exp:controls}

To rule out training-evaluation overlap as the source of the gains,
we re-train BGFM on shards in which the per-perturbation energies
have been permuted within each parent molecule (the multiset of
energies a parent receives is unchanged but the assignment to
geometries is randomized). We additionally train on shards in which
the per-atom forces have been permuted across atoms, in which the
temperature is mislabeled, and in which $\mathcal{L}_{\rm dens}$ is
replaced by a random scalar regularizer with the same weight
schedule. The headline causal claim is that the lift on the
independent xTB physical-quality metric appears only for real-label
training, even though the shuffled-label runs may still optimize the
neural-potential-aligned correlation metric.

\subsection{Energy-based ranking and filtering}
\label{sec:exp:ranking}

A pooled set of samples is constructed from EDM, GeoLDM, FlowMol3,
EBMol, the FM baseline, BGFM, deliberately strained perturbations,
and relaxed reference structures. We score each molecule with the
EBMol learned energy, the neural-potential energy, the BGFM density
score
\begin{equation}
    S_{\rm BGFM}^{\rm dens}(x) = \log p_\theta(r \mid c) - \beta E_{\rm NP}(r, c) - a_\phi(c),
    \label{eq:bgfm-density-score}
\end{equation}
and the calibrated BGFM energy head $\hat E_\psi(r, c)$.
Ground-truth strain is GFN2-xTB relaxation $\Delta E$, xTB force
norms, and DFT subset energy decrease. We report Pearson, Spearman,
AUROC for the top decile of high-strain molecules, and the median
relaxation energy after retaining the top-scored $10\%$, $20\%$, and
$50\%$ of samples.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

BGFM uses an external universal neural potential as a training-time
teacher for a de novo 3D molecular flow generator, combining an
amortized flow proposal, a Boltzmann regularization that aligns the
flow's implied score and joint density with the potential, a
calibrated scalar energy head distilled from the same potential, and
a short learned-energy corrector that refines the flow proposal at
inference time. The method preserves the diversity and amortized
sampling speed of flow matching while recovering the explicit
energy landscape that motivates energy-based generators, without
inheriting their inference-time sampling cost. A hybrid
discrete-continuous joint density estimator makes the regularization
well-defined across heterogeneous chemistry.

\section{Derivations}
\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{Score from flow-matching velocity}
\label{app:score-from-velocity}

For the linear interpolant $r_t = (1-t) r_0 + t r_1$ with
$r_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ and $r_1 \sim p_{\rm data}$, the
conditional distribution of $r_0$ given $r_t$ is Gaussian with mean
$r_t - t v^\star$ and variance $(1 - t)^2 \sigma^2$. Marginalization
and the identity
$v^\star(r_t, t) = \mathbb{E}[r_1 - r_0 \mid r_t]$ yield
$s(r_t, t) = (t v^\star - r_t)/[(1 - t)\sigma^2]$. Substituting
$v_\theta$ for $v^\star$ gives \eqref{eq:score-from-velocity}.

\subsection{Variance loss and the partition function}
\label{app:variance}

For any $r$-independent shift $c_m$,
$\mathrm{Var}_k(y_m^{(k)} + c_m) = \mathrm{Var}_k(y_m^{(k)})$ by
translation invariance. Setting $c_m = -\log Z_{c_m}$ shows that
the Boltzmann constancy condition is exactly the minimizer of
\eqref{eq:ldens}, with the unknown partition function absorbed.

\subsection{Joint discrete-continuous log-density}
\label{app:joint-density}

The flow on $(r, c)$ factors as a continuous coordinate flow
conditioned on the CTMC discrete state plus a categorical bridge for
the discrete state itself. By the chain rule for densities,
$\log p_\theta(x) = \log p_\theta(r \mid c) + \log p_\theta(c)$.
The first term is the FFJORD integral \eqref{eq:ffjord} along the
coordinate flow with $c$ frozen at the endpoint label. The second
term is the per-token CTMC path probability under the discrete
schedule of \citet{stark2024flowmol3}, summed over $N$, the per-atom
type tokens, and the per-atom charge tokens, as in
\eqref{eq:discrete-logp}.


\section{Hyperparameters}
